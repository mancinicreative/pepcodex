## How to Read GLP-1 Agonist Evidence

The beginner definition is the [GLP-1 agonists guide](/guide/what-are-glp1-agonists). Mechanism belongs on [how GLP-1 agonists work](/guide/how-do-glp-1-agonists-work). Class adverse-event tables live on [GLP-1 safety overview](/safety/glp1-safety-overview). This page is how to read approved-drug evidence vs research-peptide claims — not a second class intro.

Approved GLP-1 medicines have large outcome trials. That is a different evidence grade than unapproved products sold as research chemicals.

## How Do They Work?

GLP-1 agonists work by:

1. **Increasing insulin** when blood sugar is high
2. **Reducing glucagon** (a hormone that raises blood sugar)
3. **Slowing digestion** to keep you full longer
4. **Reducing appetite** through brain signaling

## Approved Uses

- **Type 2 diabetes** — blood sugar control
- **Obesity** — weight management (some formulations)
- **Cardiovascular protection** — reducing heart attack/stroke risk (some)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea (usually improves)
- Diarrhea or constipation
- Decreased appetite
- Injection site reactions

## What's New?

Newer medications like tirzepatide add GIP receptor activity, and investigational drugs like retatrutide add glucagon activity for potentially greater effects.
