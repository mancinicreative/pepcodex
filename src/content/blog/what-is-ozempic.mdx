## Ozempic vs Wegovy vs Rybelsus: Same Molecule, Three Labels

The beginner definition is the [Ozempic guide](/guide/what-is-ozempic). The compound record is [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide). This page is the three-label split with [Wegovy](/blog/what-is-wegovy) and [Rybelsus](/blog/what-is-rybelsus) — not a second "what is it" article.

Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus are three FDA labels for the same active ingredient. The molecule is semaglutide. The decision here is which label matches which indication and route — not whether semaglutide "is" a diabetes drug or a weight-loss drug.

## Three Labels, One Molecule

| Brand | Ozempic | Wegovy | Rybelsus |
|-------|---------|--------|----------|
| **Approved for** | Type 2 diabetes | Weight management | Type 2 diabetes |
| **Form** | Weekly pen | Weekly pen | Daily tablet |
| **First FDA approval** | 2017 | 2021 | 2019 |

## How Does Ozempic Work?

Ozempic mimics a natural hormone called GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). When you inject Ozempic weekly, it:

1. **Stimulates insulin release** when blood sugar is elevated
2. **Reduces glucagon secretion** to prevent excess glucose production
3. **Slows gastric emptying** to reduce post-meal blood sugar spikes
4. **Acts on the brain** to reduce appetite

## What is Ozempic Approved For?

### FDA-Approved Indications
- **Type 2 diabetes** — to improve blood sugar control alongside diet and exercise
- **Cardiovascular risk reduction** — to reduce risk of major cardiovascular events in adults with type 2 diabetes and established heart disease

### Not Approved For
- Weight loss (use Wegovy instead)
- Type 1 diabetes
- Diabetic ketoacidosis

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea (usually improves with time)
- Diarrhea or constipation
- Abdominal pain
- Decreased appetite

## Who Should Not Use Ozempic?

- People with personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2
- Those with known hypersensitivity to semaglutide
