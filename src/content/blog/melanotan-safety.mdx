## How to Read Melanotan Safety Warnings

Agency tables and known-concern lists are the [melanotan safety page](/safety/melanotan-safety). This post yields those tables and only maps which record you are on.

Melanotan I is the [melanotan-I dossier](/peptides/melanotan-i) — the slug is not the display name. Melanotan II is a separate record ([melanotan-II](/peptides/melanotan-ii)). Do not treat "Melanotan" as one compound.

## Regulatory Warnings

The agency-by-agency table lives on [melanotan safety](/safety/melanotan-safety). FDA, TGA, and MHRA warnings are listed there — not restated as a second table here.

## Known Safety Concerns

### Skin-Related Concerns
- **Mole changes** — darkening of existing moles
- **New mole development** — emergence of new pigmented lesions
- **Melanoma risk** — theoretical concern given effects on melanocytes

### Cardiovascular
- Blood pressure changes
- Flushing
- Reported cardiac events

### Other Effects
- Severe nausea and vomiting
- Facial flushing
- Fatigue
- Spontaneous erections (in men)

## The Mole Problem

Melanotan II affects melanocytes (the cells that produce pigment). This raises serious concerns:
- Changes to existing moles can mask melanoma
- New moles make skin monitoring difficult
- Darkening of atypical nevi is particularly concerning

## Product Risks

- Sold illegally online
- No quality control
- Unknown purity
- Contamination possible
- No pharmaceutical oversight

## Do Not Use

Health authorities recommend against using Melanotan II. The risks are not justified by cosmetic tanning benefits.
