**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this briefing described a Phase 2 trial of
a thymosin beta-4 fragment ("TB4-AF") in 180 patients with diabetic foot ulcers,
reporting 52% versus 31% complete wound closure. No such trial or compound could be
located in any trial registry or the published literature, and those figures have been
removed. The verified clinical record is summarised below — and it points the other way.

## First, Get the Compound Right

TB-500 is not thymosin beta-4. Analytical characterisation of material sold under the
name identified it as the **N-terminal acetylated 17–23 fragment** of thymosin beta-4 —
seven residues out of the protein's forty-three. The full identification story is in
[What is TB-500?](/blog/what-is-tb-500) and the [TB-500 dossier](/peptides/tb-500).

That distinction controls how every wound-healing claim should be read. The human
trials described below were conducted with thymosin beta-4, the full protein, in a
topical gel formulation (RGN-137). They are **parent-protein evidence**. TB-500 itself
— the fragment — has no identified human clinical trials. Evidence from the protein
cannot be transferred to a seven-residue fragment without testing, and for this
fragment that testing has not been done in humans.

## What Was Actually Tested in Humans

The registered dose-response study of thymosin beta-4 in venous stasis ulcers was a
randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 72 participants
[tb4-venous-ulcer-trial]. A parallel Phase 2 programme evaluated pressure ulcers.

**What those trials found:** the protein was safe and well tolerated across all
administration levels, with no drug-related serious adverse events. On efficacy, the
trials did not show statistically significant differences from placebo for complete
wound healing or rate of healing. The mid-level group was described as appearing the
most active, with more rapid initiation of healing — but that observation did not reach
statistical significance.

Earlier clinical work on thymosin in venous ulcers is also published
[guarnera-thymosin-venous], and a review of the dermal-healing programme reports
accelerated healing in preclinical models and in patients [tb4-dermal-healing] — the
review-level claim sitting alongside registered trials whose primary endpoints were
not met.

This is the central point for anyone evaluating TB-500 claims: even the parent protein,
taken into properly controlled human wound trials, did not establish efficacy. The
fragment sold as TB-500 sits a full evidentiary step below that.

## The Science, Kept in Its Lane

Thymosin beta-4 is a naturally occurring 43-residue protein involved in actin
sequestration, cell migration, and angiogenesis. Its breadth as a regenerative
candidate — dermal, corneal, cardiac — is documented at review level [tb4-review], and
the mechanistic case is genuine: it promotes keratinocyte and fibroblast migration,
stimulates angiogenesis, and has anti-inflammatory properties relevant to chronic
wounds.

None of that mechanism has been demonstrated to survive truncation to seven residues in
a human. A fragment can retain, lose, or alter the parent's activity; which of those
applies to TB-500 is an empirical question that remains unanswered in humans. A 2026
review in *Sports Medicine* of approved and unapproved peptide therapies for
musculoskeletal injury places TB-500 squarely on the unapproved, unevidenced side of
that landscape [sports-med-peptides-review].

## What It Means

**For patients with chronic wounds:** diabetic foot ulcers and venous stasis ulcers
remain a serious unmet need, and no thymosin beta-4 product — let alone any fragment —
is approved to treat them. Standard wound care remains the evidence-based option.

**For the research peptide community:** the thymosin beta-4 programme is a cautionary
case twice over. Strong preclinical data and a coherent mechanism did not reproduce as
statistically significant benefit in randomized placebo-controlled trials of the
protein — and the compound actually sold as TB-500 was never in those trials at all.
The same preclinical-to-human caution applies to [BPC-157](/peptides/bpc-157), whose
musculoskeletal evidence is examined in
[our systematic-review briefing](/blog/bpc-157-systematic-review-musculoskeletal).

**On product claims:** TB-500 sold as a research chemical is not the trial formulation,
was not the trial compound, and carries no verified purity basis. It is prohibited in
competitive sport, with validated detection methods in place.

## What's Next

Thymosin beta-4 development has continued in other indications, including ophthalmic
formulations. As of 2026-08-17, no human trial of the TB-500 fragment itself appears in
our verified registry. Any future claim of wound-healing efficacy — for the protein or
the fragment — should be checked against a registered trial with posted results rather
than a press release or a vendor page.
