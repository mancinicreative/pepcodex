AOD-9604, a modified fragment of human growth hormone, remains popular in wellness and anti-aging communities on the strength of its fat-loss reputation. A search of the peer-reviewed literature does not support that reputation: there is no published human efficacy trial for weight or fat loss, and the compound's obesity development programme was abandoned after phase 2.

## What We Know

The published AOD-9604 literature indexed in PubMed consists almost entirely of preclinical pharmacology, analytical-chemistry and anti-doping detection work, and passing mentions in obesity-drug review articles. **No randomised controlled trial of AOD-9604 for fat loss in humans appears in the peer-reviewed record**, and consequently no meta-analysis of such trials exists either.

What does exist is a contemporaneous development profile from *Current Opinion in Investigational Drugs*, which records that the Australian company Metabolic Pharmaceuticals was developing AOD-9604 for obesity and that phase IIa trials were underway by February 2002 [aod9604-trial-history]. The programme did not produce a published efficacy paper, and development for obesity did not continue.

Absence of published results is itself informative. A positive phase 2 in obesity is publishable and commercially valuable; the absence of any such publication, combined with the programme's discontinuation, is the most that can honestly be said.

### The AOD-9604 Story

AOD-9604 consists of amino acids 177-191 of human growth hormone, modified with a tyrosine at position 182. Preclinical studies in rodents suggested it retained the lipolytic (fat-burning) properties of growth hormone: in obese mice, 14 days of chronic intraperitoneal administration reduced body weight and body fat, increased beta-3 adrenergic receptor RNA expression and enhanced lipolytic sensitivity [gh-fragment-mechanisms].

Despite the discontinued drug programme, AOD-9604 has been marketed in supplements and through compounding and wellness channels.

The disconnect between animal and human results may reflect species differences in growth hormone biology or the doses achievable in humans. The peptide's short half-life also creates challenges for sustained effect.

## What It Means

For individuals currently using or considering AOD-9604 for weight loss, there is no published placebo-controlled human evidence to support an expectation of meaningful fat reduction beyond diet and exercise. Marketing claims of proven fat-loss efficacy are not backed by any trial in the peer-reviewed record.

The AOD-9604 situation illustrates broader challenges in the wellness peptide market. Products gain popularity based on preclinical promise or anecdotal reports despite absence of human efficacy data. Once community belief becomes established, contradictory evidence struggles to change perceptions.

Quality and safety concerns compound the efficacy question. AOD-9604 sold through unregulated channels may not contain the labeled compound or may contain contaminants. Without therapeutic benefit to offset these risks, the risk-benefit calculation is unfavorable.

For prescribers and clinics offering AOD-9604, the evidence base does not support claims of fat loss efficacy. Continued promotion of the peptide for weight loss based on available data raises ethical questions about evidence-based practice.

## What's Next

The gap between marketing claims and the published record is the obvious regulatory exposure here. Asserting fat loss benefits without supporting evidence violates advertising standards in most jurisdictions, though enforcement in the wellness space has been inconsistent.

Scientific interest in growth hormone fragments continues, though focused on mechanisms rather than clinical development. Understanding why the preclinical promise did not translate may inform development of other growth hormone-derived therapeutics.

For individuals seeking pharmacological support for weight loss, approved medications with demonstrated efficacy—including GLP-1 agonists—represent evidence-based alternatives. These medications have clear clinical trial data supporting their use and regulated manufacturing ensuring product quality.

The broader peptide community may benefit from increased emphasis on evidence-based evaluation. As more peptides become available through gray-market channels, distinguishing those with genuine therapeutic potential from those with only theoretical promise becomes increasingly important.
