N-Acetyl Selank Amidate must be assessed separately from parent Selank. **Evidence for this derivative remains very low:** the parent-compound studies cited here do not test the N-acetylated, C-terminally amidated form.

### What the September 2026 search found

On September 5, 2026, seven PubMed name searches were run both without date restrictions and with a June 7 to September 5, 2026 publication, indexing, or record-modification window. No records were returned in the window. The unrestricted searches returned two unique records about parent Selank in rats, not direct evidence about this derivative. PubMed broadened some unrecognized quoted phrases, so these are the results of specific database searches, not proof that no derivative research exists anywhere. The two retrieved rat papers have similar authors and experiments; they are not counted here as independent derivative studies. [PMID:12449836](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12449836/), [PMID:14552529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14552529/).

### What parent Selank evidence can establish

The 2008 clinical report studied 62 people with generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia: 30 received parent Selank and 32 received medazepam. The abstract reports similar anxiety effects, but provides no numerical between-group estimate and insufficient detail to appraise randomization or blinding. PubMed classifies it as a randomized controlled trial. It did not test N-Acetyl Selank Amidate, and it cannot establish equivalent effects, better absorption, or longer duration for that derivative. [PMID:18454096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18454096/).

A 2004 laboratory study examined how parent Selank affected enzymes that break down enkephalins in human plasma. Human plasma research is **in vitro evidence**, not a trial treating people and not a demonstration of improved mood from this derivative. [PMID:15344652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15344652/).

A 2008 parent Selank paper concerns BDNF expression in rat hippocampus. PubMed supplies no abstract for that record, so this review does not infer a numerical effect, improved human cognition, or a demonstrated mechanism for N-Acetyl Selank Amidate. A separate parent Selank study examined behavior and plasma enkephalin-degrading enzymes in mice; it likewise does not establish derivative benefits in humans. [PMID:18841804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18841804/), [PMID:12432865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12432865/).

### Stability, safety, and remaining uncertainty

The sources reviewed for this correction do not establish a measured half-life, greater bioavailability, clinical onset, sustained benefit, or combination safety for N-Acetyl Selank Amidate. Chemical-modification arguments and parent Selank findings are insufficient to claim those outcomes for this molecule. A 2018 review describes terminal modification among general strategies for improving peptide metabolic stability. It is background context, not a derivative-specific pharmacokinetic experiment. [PMID:29956618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29956618/).

The seven-source inventory comprises one parent Selank clinical publication, five parent Selank preclinical publications (including the human-plasma experiment), and one general review. These are publication counts, not counts of independent experiments or direct derivative studies. The two rat learning papers may describe the same experiment. Full-text access status was not comprehensively assessed in this correction; a free PubMed abstract is not equivalent to open-access full text. Numerical evidence and effectiveness scores are withheld pending a source-backed rubric reassessment; parent-compound evidence does not support a measured treatment-effect score for this derivative.
