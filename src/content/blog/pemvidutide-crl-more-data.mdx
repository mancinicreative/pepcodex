[Pemvidutide](/peptides/pemvidutide), Altimmune's GLP-1/glucagon dual receptor agonist for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), is frequently discussed in terms that overstate where it sits. This is a plain account of its actual regulatory position.

**Pemvidutide has not been submitted for marketing approval anywhere.** As of 17 August 2026, there is no FDA approval, no EMA approval, and no approval decision of any kind — favourable or unfavourable — because no application has been filed. No application for pemvidutide appears in Drugs@FDA. What exists are development designations and a completed Phase 2b programme.

## What Pemvidutide Has Received

The FDA has granted pemvidutide **Breakthrough Therapy Designation** for MASH, and **Fast Track** designation for MASH [altimmune-btd-press].

It is worth being clear about what these are. Both are development-stage designations intended to speed the process — more frequent FDA interaction, eligibility for rolling review. Neither is an approval, neither guarantees approval, and neither reflects a completed efficacy assessment. Breakthrough Therapy Designation in this case was granted on the basis of Phase 2b data.

## The Evidence Behind It

The designation and the Phase 3 plan rest on the IMPACT Phase 2b trial, which randomised 212 patients across 83 sites in the United States and Australia to once-weekly pemvidutide at 1.2 mg or 1.8 mg, or placebo [pemvidutide-phase2-data]. Our [IMPACT results analysis](/blog/pemvidutide-impact-mash-resolution) covers the trial in depth.

At 24 weeks:

- **MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis** was achieved by 58% (1.2 mg) and 52% (1.8 mg), versus 20% on placebo — both statistically significant (p&lt;0.0001)
- **Fibrosis improvement without worsening of MASH** was achieved by 33% (1.2 mg) and 36% (1.8 mg), versus 28% on placebo — **neither significant** (p=0.59 and p=0.27)

Adverse events were predominantly mild to moderate, and discontinuations due to adverse events were rare in every arm [pemvidutide-phase2-data].

## Why Phase 3 Is Necessary

The IMPACT result is a genuine positive on the resolution endpoint in a modestly sized trial. It is not a registration package, for reasons the FDA's own guidance on drug development in noncirrhotic NASH with liver fibrosis makes clear: histological endpoints in this disease need to be established in adequately powered trials, and the fibrosis endpoint carries particular weight because fibrosis stage is what links most strongly to long-term clinical outcomes [fda-mash-guidance].

Altimmune has stated that it completed an end-of-Phase-2 meeting with the FDA and reached alignment on the parameters for a registrational Phase 3 trial in MASH patients with moderate to advanced fibrosis, evaluating multiple doses over a 52-week treatment period with biopsy-based endpoints intended to support potential accelerated approval.

## What This Means for the MASH Field

MASH remains a disease with very limited approved pharmacotherapy, and several mechanisms are advancing simultaneously — thyroid hormone receptor agonism, GLP-1 monotherapy, GLP-1/GIP and GLP-1/glucagon dual agonism. The most closely related programme, [survodutide](/peptides/survodutide), published its first phase 3 liver readout in June 2026. Cross-trial comparison between these agents is unreliable: entry criteria, fibrosis stage distribution, biopsy reading methodology and trial duration all differ, and apparent differences in resolution rates often reflect those design choices rather than drug effect.

## What This Means for Patients

Pemvidutide is available only through clinical trials. Anyone with MASH should discuss options with a hepatologist, including approved medication, trial participation, and evidence-based lifestyle intervention.

Claims that pemvidutide has been approved, rejected, or issued a Complete Response Letter do not correspond to any regulatory event on record.
