## What is known about safety?

The [FDA safety page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks), reviewed September 5, 2026, describes unresolved human safety-data, immunogenicity, and product-characterization concerns for MOTs-C. Its inclusion among withdrawn nominations is not an approval or demonstration of safety.

Naturally occurring MOTS-c and an administered product are different evidence questions. Measuring the peptide in human blood or tissue does not establish tolerability, adverse-event frequency, or long-term effects of administering it. For example, an aging study measured plasma and muscle concentrations; it was not a treatment-safety trial. [PMID:32182209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32182209/)

## A relevant experimental warning

A June 2026 study found that MOTS-c activated metabolic signaling in human donor-derived stromal cells but reduced their proliferation and increased senescence-related and inflammatory signals. In related mouse experiments, cell pretreatment did not restore or could impair kidney-repair effects. These findings show that a plausible mechanism need not produce a useful outcome. They do not establish that the same effects occur in patients or quantify clinical risk. [PMID:42324588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324588/)

## Why a recruiting label is insufficient

The September 5 snapshot of [NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) labels a MOTS-c study recruiting and contains no posted results. This review has not independently verified participant recruitment, product identity, or actual administration.

Another record naming the same sponsor, Hudson Biotech, describes its TB-500 study as fictional. The MOTS-c record does not make that statement. The shared sponsor context warrants verification; it does not establish that the MOTS-c entry is fictional. Neither record supplies clinical safety results for native MOTS-c here. [NCT07487363](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07487363)

## Analogue data cannot fill native-peptide gaps

The completed [NCT03998514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) record concerns CB4211. The [sponsor describes CB4211 as a MOTS-c analogue](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1522602/000121390018006921/f8k052918ex99-1_cohbar.htm). Results or pharmacokinetic information about an analogue would require their own appraisal and cannot automatically establish the safety of native MOTS-c.

## Pharmacokinetics and evidence limits

The [FDA’s May 2026 briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download) reported that its review had not identified clinical pharmacokinetic data for MOTS-c-related bulk substances. Its discussion of degradation in human blood concerned an in-vitro experiment, not exposure after administration to people. That evidence does not establish a human half-life or duration of effect.

The reviewed material does not establish native MOTS-c interaction safety, adverse-event rates, or long-term tolerability. Absence of quantified harms in these sources is not evidence that harm cannot occur.

The [MOTS-c dossier](/peptides/mots-c) distinguishes biomarker observations from administered-peptide experiments. This page is a research summary, not individualized medical advice or a treatment recommendation.
