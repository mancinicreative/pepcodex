## What is actually being compared?

Human follistatin (FST) is an endogenous protein with multiple isoforms. The FST344 precursor gives rise to the mature FST315 protein; the two numbers do not describe interchangeable administered products. Follistatin binds activin-family ligands, including activin A and myostatin, but a binding mechanism is not itself a demonstrated treatment effect. [UniProt human FST](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprot/P19883-1) documents the sequence and processing, while the [activin A–follistatin structure](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16198295/) establishes one ligand-binding interaction.

IGF-1 LR3 (Long R3 IGF-I) is an engineered IGF-1 analogue with an N-terminal extension and a position-3 Glu-to-Arg substitution. An [original analogue study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1378742/) documented lower binding-protein interaction and cell responses that depended on the model: LR3 was less potent than native IGF-1 in cells without detectable IGF-binding proteins. A separate [human dermal fibroblast culture study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8691093/) reported receptor interaction with no detectable IGFBP-3 binding in its assay. These laboratory observations do not imply stronger receptor affinity, superior human efficacy, or established safety.

## What the studies measured

In a six-person, open-label [Becker muscular dystrophy AAV1-FS344 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25322757/), four participants had reported six-minute-walk gains and two did not improve. A separate [sporadic inclusion-body myositis report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28279643/) involved six recipients of AAV1-FS344 plus exercise and eight matched untreated comparators, without random assignment. Its efficacy interpretation was [publicly challenged](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28927986/). Both reports concern gene transfer in defined diseases, not direct follistatin protein or enhancement in healthy adults. A separate [DMD AAV1-FS344 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02354781) posted results for three participants: none had increased six-minute-walk distance and one had North Star Ambulatory Assessment improvement. This small single-group record does not establish effectiveness or safety.

ACE-083 is also a distinct intervention. A [phase 1 study in 58 postmenopausal women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29486514/) reported greater local muscle volume but no mean strength improvement. A [randomized phase 2 FSHD study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35428982/) likewise found volume changes without consistent functional improvement. These observations cannot be used as proof of native follistatin's clinical effect.

IGF-1 LR3 has directly measured effects in animals, with results varying by species and context. A [rat experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8371075/) found stronger growth-related responses than unmodified IGF-1 under its experimental conditions. In contrast, [finisher pigs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9488001/) given LR3 had lower daily gain and food intake. In a more recent [growth-restricted fetal-sheep study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39679943/), one week of LR3 did not improve fetal weight compared with vehicle. None of these is a human muscle-benefit or long-term-safety trial.

## Why a combination cannot be inferred

A [mouse follistatin pathway study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22087027/) examined the role of IGF-1 receptor/Akt/mTOR signaling in follistatin-associated hypertrophy. It did not test an administered follistatin-plus-IGF-1-LR3 regimen. A [follow-up animal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26219865/) further separated IGF-I ligand from insulin-related effects. Neither paper establishes that combining the two named compounds is effective, compatible, or safe in people.

## Evidence boundary

The current primary records allow a comparison of biological targets and experimental models. They do **not** support a head-to-head effectiveness ranking, a human muscle-growth claim for either named compound, or a prediction of combination safety. The limited gene-transfer studies and preclinical LR3 experiments leave dose-response, long-term outcomes, and safety of the proposed comparison in people unresolved.
