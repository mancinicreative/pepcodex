The IMPACT phase 2b trial of pemvidutide, a GLP-1/glucagon dual receptor agonist, published its 24-week results in *The Lancet* in December 2025 [impact-trial-results]. The trial had **two** primary endpoints. It met one and missed the other, and coverage that reports only the first is describing half a trial.

The full compound record is in the [pemvidutide dossier](/peptides/pemvidutide); the closest comparator with published data is [survodutide](/peptides/survodutide).

## What the Published Trial Showed

IMPACT ([NCT05989711](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05989711)) is an ongoing 48-week international, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2b trial in patients with biopsy-confirmed MASH and fibrosis stage F2 or F3. Across 83 sites in the USA and Australia, 1,557 patients were screened and **212 randomised** 1:2:2 to once-weekly subcutaneous pemvidutide 1.2 mg, 1.8 mg, or placebo. Both primary endpoints were assessed at 24 weeks in the intention-to-treat population [impact-trial-results].

**Primary endpoint 1 — MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis: met.**

| Group | MASH resolution | Difference vs placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Placebo (n=86) | 18/86 (20%) | — |
| Pemvidutide 1.2 mg (n=41) | 24/41 (**58%**) | +38 points (95% CI 21–56), P&lt;0.0001 |
| Pemvidutide 1.8 mg (n=85) | 45/85 (**52%**) | +32 points (95% CI 19–46), P&lt;0.0001 |

**Primary endpoint 2 — fibrosis improvement without worsening of MASH: not met.**

| Group | Fibrosis improvement | Difference vs placebo |
|---|---|---|
| Placebo (n=86) | 24/86 (28%) | — |
| Pemvidutide 1.2 mg (n=41) | 13/41 (33%) | +5 points (95% CI −13 to 22), P=0.59 |
| Pemvidutide 1.8 mg (n=85) | 30/85 (36%) | +8 points (95% CI −6 to 22), P=0.27 |

Both fibrosis confidence intervals cross zero. The trial's own conclusion states it met the MASH-resolution endpoint and **did not** meet the fibrosis endpoint at this timepoint, with longer trials planned [impact-trial-results].

Two further details deserve attention. The lower 1.2 mg group outperformed the higher 1.8 mg group on the endpoint that was met — not the monotone dose-response the mechanism predicts, and with 41 patients in that arm the estimate is imprecise. And the placebo MASH-resolution rate was 20%, high enough that a 24-week window may simply be too short to separate fibrosis signal from noise. An accompanying *Lancet* comment discusses the trial in that context [impact-comment].

For comparison, resmetirom (Rezdiffra), the first FDA-approved MASH treatment, achieved roughly 30% MASH resolution in its pivotal trials — but cross-trial comparison of biopsy endpoints is notoriously unreliable given differences in population, reading protocol and placebo rate.

### The Dual Agonist Mechanism

Pemvidutide activates both GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, a combination that may offer particular advantages for liver disease. Understanding why requires examining what each receptor contributes:

**GLP-1 receptor activation** promotes weight loss and metabolic improvement through appetite suppression, enhanced insulin secretion, and various tissue-specific effects. GLP-1 agonists like semaglutide have demonstrated benefit in MASH trials, though their effects on fibrosis have been more modest than their impact on steatohepatitis.

**Glucagon receptor activation** directly stimulates hepatic fatty acid oxidation and promotes the clearance of fat from liver cells. This hepatocentric mechanism may explain why dual agonists appear to achieve more pronounced effects on liver fat compared to GLP-1 agonists alone.

The combination appears to provide complementary effects: GLP-1 activity improves systemic metabolic health and promotes weight loss, while glucagon activity directly addresses hepatic lipid accumulation. In the IMPACT trial, participants experienced substantial weight loss alongside liver-specific improvements, though the relative contribution of each mechanism remains under investigation.

### Weight, Liver Fat and Metabolic Effects

An earlier randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of pemvidutide in MASLD, published in the *Journal of Hepatology*, characterised the compound's effect on liver fat rather than histology [pemvidutide-masld].

A 2026 GRADE-assessed meta-analysis pooled the randomised pemvidutide trials against placebo [pemvidutide-meta]. At 12 weeks it found significant reductions in liver fat content (mean difference −52.90, 95% CI −71.60 to −34.20), CAP score, body weight (−3.50 kg, 95% CI −5.00 to −2.00) and blood pressure. At 24 weeks it found improvements in ALT (−18.09, 95% CI −30.12 to −6.06), AST, liver fat content and ELF score, with safety comparable to placebo apart from more frequent mild-to-moderate nausea.

Those are consistent, real biochemical and imaging effects. None of them is a fibrosis outcome, which is the endpoint IMPACT missed and the one regulators ultimately weigh.

## What We Don't Know

Despite the promising results, several important questions remain before pemvidutide can be considered a proven treatment for MASH.

**Phase 3 confirmation required:** The IMPACT trial, while well-designed, represents phase 2 data with relatively limited participant numbers. Phase 3 trials with larger populations and longer duration will be necessary to confirm efficacy, characterize safety, and support regulatory approval. Altimmune has announced plans to initiate phase 3 development.

**Long-term outcomes:** The 48-week treatment period, while adequate for regulatory endpoints based on histological improvement, does not demonstrate whether pemvidutide prevents clinically meaningful outcomes like progression to cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, liver transplantation, or liver-related mortality. Demonstrating impact on these hard endpoints would require much longer trials.

**Durability of response:** What happens after treatment discontinuation remains unclear. Whether the liver improvements persist, partially reverse, or completely reverse off therapy has implications for how the drug would be used clinically.

### Safety Considerations

Adverse events were reported in 78% of the 1.2 mg group, 81% of the 1.8 mg group and 67% of placebo, the majority mild or moderate. Discontinuation due to adverse events was rare and no higher than placebo: none of 41 on 1.2 mg, one of 85 on 1.8 mg, and two of 86 on placebo [impact-trial-results]. Note that pemvidutide was given **without** titration in this trial, which makes the tolerability result more informative than it first appears.

A theoretical concern with glucagon receptor agonism relates to effects on glucose metabolism, as glucagon raises blood glucose. In practice, the concurrent GLP-1 activity appears to offset this effect, and pemvidutide did not worsen glycemic control in trial participants. However, longer-term safety data in diabetic populations will be important.

Additionally, the substantial weight loss observed raises questions about body composition changes and whether lean mass preservation is adequate. Some GLP-1 agonists have faced scrutiny regarding muscle loss, and this will likely be an area of continued evaluation for pemvidutide.

## How Strong Is the Evidence?

The evidence from the IMPACT trial qualifies as suggestive rather than definitive. The trial was randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, and used gold-standard histological endpoints including paired liver biopsies. One primary endpoint was met with a large, statistically significant effect; the other was not met, on confidence intervals that include no benefit [impact-trial-results].

However, several factors warrant caution:

**Phase 2 limitations:** The trial randomised 212 participants, with only 41 in the 1.2 mg arm that produced the strongest resolution result — reasonable power for the pooled comparison, limited ability to detect rare adverse events or characterise subgroups.

**The dose ordering:** the lower arm outperformed the higher one on the met endpoint. That can happen by chance in arms this size, and it is a reason to treat the specific percentages as provisional.

**Single trial:** Replication in independent studies will strengthen confidence in the findings. The history of MASH drug development includes multiple agents that showed promise in phase 2 but failed in larger trials.

**Surrogate endpoints:** The FDA has accepted MASH resolution and fibrosis improvement as basis for accelerated approval, but these remain surrogate endpoints. The ultimate measure of a MASH treatment is prevention of liver-related clinical events.

**Histological variability:** Liver biopsies are subject to sampling variability, and pathologist interpretation of MASH can vary. While the trial used central pathology reading to minimize this issue, it remains an inherent limitation of histological endpoints.

The evidence is strong enough to justify continued development and generate considerable scientific interest, but not yet sufficient to establish pemvidutide as a proven treatment for MASH.

## What's Next

The positive IMPACT results set the stage for several important developments.

**Phase 3 planning:** Altimmune will design phase 3 trials based on the IMPACT learnings, selecting optimal doses and refining patient selection criteria. These larger trials will need to confirm efficacy while generating the safety database required for regulatory approval.

**Competitive landscape:** Pemvidutide enters an increasingly competitive field. Resmetirom received FDA approval in 2024 as the first MASH treatment. Multiple GLP-1 agonists, including semaglutide and tirzepatide, are being studied in MASH. Survodutide, another GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist from Boehringer Ingelheim and Zealand Pharma, has reported its own positive MASH trial results.

**Combination approaches:** Whether pemvidutide might be combined with other agents, such as resmetirom, which has a complementary mechanism targeting thyroid hormone receptor beta, represents an area of potential investigation.

**Broader metabolic disease:** Pemvidutide's effects on weight and metabolic parameters suggest potential applications beyond MASH. Registered phase 2 work extends to alcohol-associated liver disease ([NCT07009860](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07009860)) and alcohol use disorder ([NCT06987513](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06987513)) — indications that follow from the same weight and hepatic-fat effects [pemvidutide-meta].

**Regulatory pathway:** The FDA's acceptance of accelerated approval based on histological endpoints for MASH treatments provides a potential path to market before long-term outcome data mature, though confirmatory trials demonstrating clinical benefit would likely be required post-approval.

The IMPACT results represent meaningful progress in addressing MASH, a condition affecting millions globally for which treatment options remain limited. Whether pemvidutide fulfills its apparent promise will depend on successful phase 3 development and, ultimately, demonstration of benefit in clinical practice.
