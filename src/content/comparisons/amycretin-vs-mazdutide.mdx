## What the evidence actually compares

Amycretin is a single molecule designed to activate GLP-1 and amylin receptors. Mazdutide instead activates GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Amycretin is not the separate cagrilintide-plus-semaglutide combination called CagriSema.

Published randomized early-phase amycretin studies include an oral formulation trial and a subcutaneous phase 1b/2a trial in adults with overweight or obesity. These establish early clinical weight and tolerability observations for their particular formulations and follow-up periods; they are not a published head-to-head comparison with mazdutide or a substitute for phase 3 outcome results. [Oral amycretin, PMID 40550229](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40550229/) · [Subcutaneous amycretin, PMID 40550231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40550231/)

Mazdutide is a GLP-1/glucagon receptor dual agonist. Its published GLORY-1 and GLORY-2 weight trials enrolled Chinese adults; GLORY-2 included some adults with type 2 diabetes, whereas the separate US phase 2 trial enrolled adults without it. The trials used different follow-up and analysis methods. [GLORY-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40421736/) · [GLORY-2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42251595/) · [US phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42628555/)

Innovent reports Chinese NMPA approvals for specified adult weight-management and type 2 diabetes uses. The reviewed source is a [sponsor exchange filing](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2025/1125/2025112500387.pdf), not an independently retrieved Chinese label. No US FDA mazdutide approval was verified; an [FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-sciences-721805-03312026) identifies one US seller's mazdutide products as unapproved new drugs.

## What cannot be inferred

Mazdutide's cited GLORY phase 3 trials and amycretin's cited early-phase trials differ in phase, geography, population and endpoint timing. Their published percentages should not be ranked across trials.

Amycretin remained investigational in the reviewed sources; no FDA approval was verified for it or for mazdutide. Innovent separately reports specified Chinese mazdutide approvals. The source for the Amycretin evidence above is [this primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40550231/); regulatory claims about mazdutide are supported by the linked sponsor filing and FDA letter. No combination-use claim follows from these separate studies.

This comparison is educational research context, not medical advice or a treatment protocol.
