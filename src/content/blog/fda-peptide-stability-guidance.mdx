## What actually governs peptide stability

FDA has adopted ICH Q1A(R2), *Stability Testing of New Drug Substances and
Products* (November 2003 on FDA’s guidance page; ICH Step 4 dated 6 February
2003) [ich-q1a-r2]. That document is the current U.S. stability data package
for new molecular entities and their products. It is not peptide-specific.

For abbreviated new drug applications, FDA’s May 2014 questions-and-answers
guidance clarifies stability-data recommendations for ANDAs. It points back to
the June 2013 ANDA stability guidance; it is also not a peptide-only document
[fda-anda-stability-qa].

ICH has a later draft that *does* name polypeptides. The Q1 Step 2 draft
endorsed 11 April 2025, still under public consultation as of that date,
consolidates Q1A–F and Q5C and states that it applies to chemically synthesised
drug substances including polypeptides, and to therapeutic
proteins/polypeptides [ich-q1-step2]. That is an ICH draft, not an FDA
peptide-stability finalization.

## What ICH Q1A(R2) actually requires

Q1A’s stated purpose is to show how quality varies with time under
temperature, humidity, and light, and to support a re-test period (drug
substance) or shelf life (drug product) [ich-q1a-r2].

For the general-case drug product, the minimum data package at submission is
[ich-q1a-r2]:

- Long-term: 12 months at 25°C ± 2°C / 60% RH ± 5% RH, or 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH
- Intermediate (if needed): 6 months at 30°C ± 2°C / 65% RH ± 5% RH
- Accelerated: 6 months at 40°C ± 2°C / 75% RH ± 5% RH

“Significant change” for a drug product is defined in Q1A as a 5% change in
assay from the initial value (or failure to meet a biological/immunological
potency criterion); any degradation product exceeding its acceptance
criterion; failure on appearance, physical attributes, or functionality; and,
as appropriate, failure on pH or dissolution [ich-q1a-r2]. Specification-setting
is pointed to ICH Q6A/Q6B, not invented as a 90% potency / 10% total-degradant
rule.

Stress testing is product-specific. Q1A says the nature of the stress work
depends on the individual drug substance and should examine susceptibility to
temperature, humidity, oxidation, and light [ich-q1a-r2]. In-use stability after
constitution or dilution, when applicable, is part of the formal program
[ich-q1a-r2].

## What peptide formulation papers add

The chemistry of peptide degradation is documented in the formulation
literature, not in a missing FDA peptide draft.

A 2023 review of aqueous therapeutic-peptide formulation strategies covers
chemical routes (oxidation, deamidation, isomerization) and physical
instability (aggregation, precipitation) and the formulation levers used to
slow them [pmid-36986796]. A 1999 review of solid-state chemical stability in
proteins and peptides addresses degradation in the dry state [pmid-10229638]. A
2024 case study compares peroxide-quantitation assays in a peptide drug-product
degradation setting [pmid-38953302]. Those papers inform method choice and
formulation design. They are not FDA guidance.

## What it means for developers

Until a peptide-specific FDA stability guidance exists, a stability protocol
for a peptide NDA or ANDA is still designed against Q1A(R2) (and the ANDA
stability Q&A where the application is an ANDA) [ich-q1a-r2]
[fda-anda-stability-qa]. Peptide-specific degradation mapping belongs in the
protocol because the formulation literature shows those pathways are common
[pmid-36986796], not because FDA issued a 2026 peptide annex.

The April 2025 ICH Q1 draft is the document to watch if the question is
“will the next harmonized stability guideline treat polypeptides explicitly?”
It is still Step 2 consultation text [ich-q1-step2].

## What’s next

As of 2026-09-01:

- ICH Q1A(R2) remains the adopted FDA stability guidance for new drugs
  [ich-q1a-r2].
- ICH Q1 Step 2 (11 April 2025) is a draft consolidation, not a final
  [ich-q1-step2].
- No FDA peptide-only stability draft was located on this day’s fetch. That
  absence is dated to 2026-09-01 and is not a claim that FDA will never issue
