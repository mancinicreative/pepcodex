SURMOUNT-OSA is two phase 3, double-blind, randomised trials in adults with moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and obesity: trial 1 in people not on positive airway pressure (PAP) at baseline, trial 2 in people who were. Together they randomised 469 participants (234 and 235). Participants received the maximum tolerated dose of tirzepatide or placebo for 52 weeks. The primary end point was change in the apnea-hypopnea index (AHI; events per hour of sleep) [surmount-osa-results].

**The indication is no longer pending.** FDA approved Zepbound (tirzepatide) on 20 December 2024 for moderate to severe OSA in adults with obesity, to be used with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity — the agency's first drug treatment option for that use [fda-osa-approval]. The current ZEPBOUND label carries the same indication [zepbound-label].

The full compound record is in the [tirzepatide dossier](/peptides/tirzepatide). The indication story is also on [Zepbound approved for sleep apnea](/blog/zepbound-sleep-apnea-approval); brand-versus-molecule literacy is on [How to Read the Zepbound Evidence](/blog/what-is-zepbound).

## What We Know

At week 52, mean AHI change was −25.3 events per hour with tirzepatide versus −5.3 with placebo in trial 1 (estimated treatment difference −20.0; 95% CI −25.8 to −14.2). In trial 2 it was −29.3 versus −5.5 (difference −23.8; 95% CI −29.6 to −17.9) [surmount-osa-results].

Percent-change in AHI was a key secondary end point, not the primary. In the published table it was −50.7% versus −3.0% in trial 1 and −58.7% versus −2.5% in trial 2 [surmount-osa-results].

A separately specified secondary counted participants who reached AHI below 5, or AHI of 5 to 14 with an Epworth Sleepiness Scale score of 10 or less: 42.2% versus 15.9% in trial 1, and 50.2% versus 14.3% in trial 2 [surmount-osa-results]. That is not the same as “AHI below 5” alone, and it is not a licence to drop PAP.

Percent-change in body weight, also secondary, was −17.7% versus −1.6% in trial 1 and −19.6% versus −2.3% in trial 2 [surmount-osa-results]. FDA's approval note states that the AHI improvement is likely related to body-weight reduction [fda-osa-approval]. The trials were not designed to separate weight loss from other mechanisms.

A separate meta-analysis of GLP-1 receptor agonists in obstructive sleep apnea places the class in context rather than treating SURMOUNT-OSA as unique to tirzepatide [osa-glp1-meta].

The primary NEJM report has a published erratum [surmount-osa-erratum]. The PubMed erratum record does not list which numbers changed; quote the corrected article, not a press-release round.

Patient-reported outcomes from SURMOUNT-OSA were analysed separately in 2025 [surmount-osa-pro]. AHI is a count of respiratory events; how a person feels during the day is a different measurement.

## What It Means

PAP remains standard care for moderate-to-severe OSA; this page is not a treatment guide and does not compare tirzepatide with PAP as a replacement.

The label change matters for how the indication is written: moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity sits on Zepbound alongside weight management [zepbound-label] [fda-osa-approval]. Coverage and practice questions belong to a clinician and a payer, not this briefing.

## What's Next

Open questions after approval are durability, what happens if the drug is stopped, and how much of the AHI change is explained by weight. Those are not answered by SURMOUNT-OSA's 52-week table.

Other incretin medicines may pursue similar indications. That is pipeline context, not a result.
