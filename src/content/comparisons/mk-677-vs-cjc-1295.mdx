## Mechanisms and evidence

MK-677 (ibutamoren) is an **oral, nonpeptide ghrelin-receptor agonist**. CJC-1295 in the cited human trials is a **long-acting, injected GHRH analog**. FDA documents multiple CJC-1295-related substances and ambiguous naming in the human literature; results from one form should not be transferred to another. [FDA ibutamoren evaluation](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download); [FDA CJC-1295 assessment](https://www.fda.gov/media/183819/download); [Teichman et al., PMID 16352683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683/).

| Question | MK-677 | Studied CJC-1295 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human study context | Older adults and other distinct research populations | Short placebo-controlled studies in healthy adults |
| Demonstrated endpoint | Increased GH, IGF-1 and fat-free mass in the older-adult trial | Increased GH and IGF-1 after injection |
| Clinical benefit | No significant strength, function or visceral-fat benefit detected in the older-adult trial | Strength, function and fat-loss benefit not established by the cited hormone studies |
| FDA status | Not approved | Not approved |

The randomized MK-677 study included **65 healthy adults aged 60–81**. Fat-free mass increased, but strength and function did not improve significantly, and abdominal visceral fat did not decrease significantly. The authors explicitly said the study duration and participant number were insufficient to evaluate functional endpoints. Fasting glucose rose and insulin sensitivity fell; increased appetite, transient edema and muscle pain were reported. These observations do not define a comparative safety rate against CJC-1295. [Nass et al., PMID 18981485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18981485/).

Two short placebo-controlled CJC-1295 studies found sustained GH and IGF-1 increases in healthy adults. They were **hormone and pharmacokinetic studies**, not evidence of muscle, fat-loss or anti-aging benefit. A separate study assessed GH pulsatility after the long-acting analog. [Teichman et al., PMID 16352683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683/); [Ionescu et al., PMID 17018654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17018654/).

The FDA's 2024 review states that Merck ended its earlier MK-0677 program in 1999 but that ibutamoren was subsequently developed as **LUM-201**. An [official Phase 3 pediatric registration, NCT06948214](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06948214), was marked recruiting in the September 29, 2026 registry archive. A registry status is dated development information, not proof of efficacy or approval. [FDA review](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download).

No cited head-to-head or combination trial supports a clinical ranking, synergy claim or regimen. This is an educational comparison, not medical advice.
