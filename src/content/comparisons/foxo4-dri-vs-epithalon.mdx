## What each experiment addresses

| Question | FOXO4-DRI | Epithalon (Epitalon, AEDG) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposed action | Interferes with FOXO4-p53 signaling associated with survival of some senescent cells | Alters telomerase- and telomere-related measures in cultured cells |
| Direct evidence cited here | Cell and mouse experiments, including selected fitness, fur and renal endpoints | Cultured human fetal fibroblasts and later normal and cancer cell lines |
| Human longevity outcome | Not established | Not established |
| Human safety | Not established | Not established |

FOXO4-DRI was tested in senescent-cell systems and in naturally aged or accelerated-aging mice. Baar and colleagues reported senescent-cell apoptosis and selected mouse fitness, fur and renal changes in *Cell* in 2017. Those outcomes do not measure longer human life or demonstrate selective clearance throughout a person's body. [Baar et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28340339/)

Epithalon is a four-amino-acid peptide (AEDG). A 2003 study reported telomerase activity and telomere elongation after adding it to cultured human fetal fibroblasts. A 2025 study reported telomere-related changes in normal and breast-cancer cell lines, with different pathway readouts. A published correction replaced incorrect Figures 1–3 in that paper, so the corrected figures are part of interpreting its results. Human-derived cell lines do not establish an effect on telomeres, aging or lifespan in people. [Khavinson et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682/) · [Al-Dulaimi et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40908429/) · [Correction, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41240216/)

## What the contrast does not show

The mechanisms are different, but the available experiments do not rank these peptides for human anti-aging use. A separate pulmonary-hypertension mouse study included FOXO4-DRI among senescent-cell removal interventions and reported worse pulmonary measures in its disease model. This is a model-specific adverse signal, not a quantified human risk; it also rules out a blanket assumption that senescent-cell clearance is beneficial in every context. [Born et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36515093/)

Neither paper set establishes the safety or benefit of using the two peptides together. Evidence for **human longevity outcomes is very low for each**. This comparison describes research and is not medical advice or guidance for self-treatment.
