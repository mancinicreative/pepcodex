## Distinct research questions

Epithalon (epitalon, AEDG) is a synthetic tetrapeptide, distinct from pineal extracts. MOTS-c is encoded in the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region. Telomere and AMPK findings are laboratory or animal mechanisms, not interchangeable anti-aging effects in people. [Epithalon cell study, PMID 12937682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682/) · [MOTS-c discovery/mouse study, PMID 25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) · [human exercise and mouse intervention, PMID 33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/) · [native-peptide trial registration, NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745)

| Question | Epithalon | MOTS-c |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct evidence | Telomere-related changes in cultured human cells, including a 2025 independent report | Mouse intervention experiments plus four selected human observational/physiology papers |
| Human administered-peptide result cited? | None | None; a native Phase 2 trial is registered without an outcome in this review |
| Therapeutic-claim grade | **Low** for human longevity | **Low** for human metabolic or longevity benefit |

The 2003 Epithalon study reported telomerase activity in cultured human fetal fibroblasts. A 2025 independent study reported telomere-related changes in cultured cells, including alternative-lengthening activity in breast-cancer lines; a published correction replaced figures. Neither establishes human healthspan, cancer risk or safety after administration. [PMID 12937682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682/) · [PMID 40908429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40908429/) · [published correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12619744/)

MOTS-c administration affected glucose-related measures in mice. Human sleep-apnea, diabetes and exercise papers measured the body's own peptide rather than giving it as treatment. A 2026 physiology study did not detect an exercise-related human arterio-venous MOTS-c difference, even though it reported a muscle interstitial signal. [PMID 40538389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40538389/) · [PMID 40855115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40855115/) · [PMID 41520850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41520850/)

[NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) registers native MOTS-c for an insulin-sensitivity question in adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity; no efficacy or safety result was cited in the accepted review. The completed [CB4211 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) concerns a distinct analog. A kidney-transplant anesthesia study measures MOTS-c but does not administer it. [NCT07678073](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07678073)

A 2026 C26 mouse and cultured-cell study reported partial preservation of skeletal muscle with preventive MOTS-c exposure, without preventing overall body or fat loss; it did not treat established human cachexia. [PMID 42266945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42266945/)

Neither peptide has an FDA-approved medicine verified here. FDA's compounded-product safety discussion is route and substance specific; endogenous MOTS-c measurements do not establish administration safety. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

This is a selected-source comparison, not a longevity or treatment recommendation.
