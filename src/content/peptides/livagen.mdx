faqs:
  - question: What is Livagen?
    answer: >-
      Livagen is a synthetic tetrapeptide developed by Russian scientist
      Vladimir Khavinson, claimed to support liver function through modulation
      of hepatic gene expression. It is part of the Russian bioregulator peptide
      research program. The primary literature consistently identifies its
      sequence as Lys-Glu-Asp-Ala (KEDA); the tripeptide Lys-Glu-Asp (KED) is a
      different bioregulator, sold as Vesugen for vascular tissue.
  - question: Is Livagen FDA approved?
    answer: >-
      No, Livagen is not approved by the FDA or any Western regulatory agency.
      It has never undergone controlled human clinical trials. All research
      originates from Russian institutions, primarily the St. Petersburg
      Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology.
  - question: What is the evidence for Livagen?
    answer: >-
      Evidence is very low quality, consisting entirely of Russian preclinical
      studies and small uncontrolled observational studies. No independent
      Western validation exists. No randomized controlled human trials have been
      conducted.
  - question: Does Livagen improve liver function?
    answer: >-
      There is no reliable evidence that Livagen improves liver function in
      humans. While Russian cell culture and animal studies suggest
      hepatoprotective effects, translation to human liver benefits is
      completely unconfirmed.
  - question: How does Livagen compare to other liver supplements?
    answer: >-
      Unlike established hepatoprotective agents (silymarin, NAC, TUDCA) which
      have human clinical data, Livagen has no Western validation or controlled
      human trials. Its proposed mechanism of gene expression modulation remains
      theoretical.

### Important Limitations

- 100% of research from Russian institutions (primarily St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology)
- No independent Western replication of any findings
- No controlled human clinical trials have been conducted
- Specific molecular targets and binding sites not characterized
- Pharmacokinetics, bioavailability, and metabolism in humans unknown — the only relevant data is a rat study reporting that Livagen resists small-intestinal peptide hydrolases, which is not evidence of systemic absorption. [PMID:16075683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16075683/)
- The human-cell work is ex vivo: cultured lymphocytes from elderly donors, not treated patients. No clinical outcome has been measured in anyone. [PMID:12533768](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12533768/)
- Translation from cell culture to human liver benefits is **unconfirmed**
- Regulatory status: Not approved anywhere; research compound only
