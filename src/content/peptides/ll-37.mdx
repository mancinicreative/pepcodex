LL-37 is a 37-amino-acid active peptide processed from the human cathelicidin precursor hCAP18, encoded by the CAMP gene. The historically predicted FALL-39 is a related but distinct 39-residue peptide, not another name for mature LL-37. LL-37's presence in human granulocytes and wound tissue supports a host-defense role, but endogenous expression is not a treatment result. [Gudmundsson et al., 1996, PMID 8681941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8681941/) · [Heilborn et al., 2003, PMID 12603850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12603850/)

### What human treatment studies found

A randomized pilot in 34 people with hard-to-heal venous leg ulcers reported improved healing measures at lower topical LL-37 concentrations over a short treatment period. A later phase IIb trial in 148 people with the same broad condition found **no significant improvement in its full population** versus placebo with compression care. Its favorable large-ulcer analysis was post hoc and needs a trial designed to test that subgroup. These are two independent treated populations, not two confirmations of benefit. [Grönberg et al., 2014, PMID 25041740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25041740/) · [Mahlapuu et al., 2021, PMID 34687253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34687253/)

A randomized trial of LL-37 cream in mildly infected diabetic foot ulcers reported greater granulation-index improvement at measured visits. It did **not** find a significant reduction in inflammatory markers or aerobic bacterial colonization. The paper and NCT04098562 describe the same population; the registry is not another efficacy study. [Miranda et al., 2023, PMID 37480520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37480520/) · [NCT04098562](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04098562)

An open-label randomized study in mild Omicron COVID-19 investigated **engineered *Lactococcus lactis* expressing LL-37**, not purified peptide. Its overall comparison for time to negative viral test was not significant; an early-treatment subgroup had a signal. The formulation, disease, and outcome do not transfer to topical or injected LL-37. [Zhao et al., 2023, PMID 37605995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37605995/)

ClinicalTrials.gov also records a completed, single-group intratumoral LL-37 melanoma study. It enrolled four people and posted results in 2021; its outcome tables report groups of only two and one people. These registry results are distinct from a journal paper, which was not identified for this cohort. They cannot establish antitumor efficacy or general safety for this route, let alone for topical or systemic use. [NCT02225366](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02225366)

### Why context matters

LL-37 can bind self-DNA and stimulate immune signaling in laboratory experiments; abnormal cathelicidin processing was implicated in human rosacea tissue. A 2026 human bronchial epithelial-cell study found that citrullination changed LL-37-linked inflammatory signaling. These findings show why an antimicrobial effect in one assay cannot be generalized to all tissues or routes. [Lande et al., 2007, PMID 17873860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17873860/) · [Yamasaki et al., 2007, PMID 17676051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17676051/) · [Ramotar et al., 2026, PMID 41535907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41535907/)

FDA lists cathelicidin LL-37 among withdrawn bulk-drug nominations. It says safety information is insufficient for compounded LL-37 and cites potential immunogenicity, peptide-characterization concerns, and nonclinical findings about male reproduction and pro-tumor effects in some tissues. Those findings do not prove that every tested topical formulation has the same risk. [FDA compounding safety page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

No systemic or broad infection use is established by these data. This dossier is a research summary, not medical advice or self-treatment guidance.
