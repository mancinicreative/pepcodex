[Orforglipron](/peptides/orforglipron), Eli Lilly's once-daily oral GLP-1 receptor agonist, moved into a broad Phase 3 development programme on the strength of its Phase 2 obesity data — and that programme has since delivered. The FDA approved orforglipron as **Foundayo** (NDA 220934) for chronic weight management on 1 April 2026; it is not approved for type 2 diabetes. This is a look at what the programme showed, and where it stands as of 17 August 2026.

## The Phase 2 Data Behind It

The Phase 2 obesity trial randomised 272 participants and tested four orforglipron doses against placebo over 36 weeks [attain-2-results]:

- Mean weight change ranged from −9.4% to −14.7% across the orforglipron dose groups, versus −2.3% on placebo
- Between 46% and 75% of orforglipron participants achieved at least 10% weight reduction by week 36, compared with 9% on placebo

Those figures are what justified a large Phase 3 investment. They are also a Phase 2 result in fewer than 300 people, and the later Phase 3 obesity trial (ATTAIN-1) reported a more modest **11.2%** at its highest strength over a longer 72-week period, versus 2.1% with placebo, on the treatment-regimen estimand [attain-1-nejm]. The 12.4% figure that circulated at topline came from the sponsor's efficacy estimand, which models outcomes as if all participants adhered; the published treatment-regimen number is the headline. Our [ATTAIN-1 analysis](/blog/orforglipron-attain-1-results) covers the trial in detail.

## Why a Small Molecule Matters

Orforglipron is a non-peptide, small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist rather than a peptide analogue [oral-glp1-comparison]. Two consequences follow:

**Manufacturing.** It is made by chemical synthesis using established small-molecule infrastructure, rather than by peptide synthesis. Peptide production capacity was the primary constraint on GLP-1 availability during the shortage, and small-molecule routes sidestep it.

**Formulation.** Because it is not a peptide, it does not depend on the absorption-enhancing excipient that oral semaglutide requires to survive the gut and cross the intestinal barrier.

The small-molecule structure also means no refrigeration requirement, simplifying storage and distribution relative to injectable peptides.

## What the Programme Delivered

**FDA approval.** Orforglipron was approved on 1 April 2026 as Foundayo (NDA 220934, Eli Lilly) for chronic weight management — the first oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist to be approved. The approval covers weight management only; type 2 diabetes is not an approved indication. A labeling supplement under the same application (SUPPL 3) carries FDA action status of 4 August 2026.

**Published diabetes trials.** The ACHIEVE type 2 diabetes programme has begun publishing. ACHIEVE-2, a phase 3 non-inferiority trial against dapagliflozin in 962 adults on metformin, reported its primary result on the treatment-regimen estimand — the trial's named primary analysis [achieve-2-lancet]. ACHIEVE-5 tested orforglipron added to titrated insulin glargine in 546 adults: HbA1c fell 1.58, 1.88 and 1.82 percentage points across the three strengths versus 0.79 with placebo at week 40 [achieve-5-jama].

## What Is Registered

The Phase 3 programme is broad, spanning obesity, type 2 diabetes and several comorbidity populations. Registered Phase 3 studies include:

- **ATTAIN-1** — obesity or overweight with weight-related comorbidities, 3,127 participants [attain-1-registration]
- **ATTAIN-2** — obesity or overweight with type 2 diabetes, 1,613 participants
- **ATTAIN-MAINTAIN** — maintenance of body weight reduction, 376 participants
- **ATTAIN-Outcomes** — cardiovascular outcomes in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease, 7,140 participants
- Additional Phase 3 studies in obstructive sleep apnea, hypertension, knee osteoarthritis, peripheral artery disease, and paediatric and adolescent populations

The cardiovascular outcomes trial is the strategically important one: in this drug class, cardiovascular outcome data is what underpins broad reimbursement rather than just weight-loss labelling.

## What It Means

Advancing to a programme of this size signalled Lilly's confidence in the molecule and a strategic commitment to competing with both injectable and oral options — a bet the April 2026 approval vindicated.

For the broader market, an effective oral alternative expands the addressable population. Many people who would benefit from GLP-1 therapy decline or discontinue injectable treatment because of needle aversion or injection burden.

The healthcare economics could shift too. If a small molecule can be produced more cheaply than peptide alternatives at comparable efficacy, pricing pressure across the class increases and payers gain leverage.

The comparative-efficacy question has been answered in outline: the Phase 3 obesity readout showed meaningful but lower weight loss than the leading injectables such as [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) and [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) [attain-1-nejm], which means route of administration, tolerability and cost, rather than efficacy alone, will determine where it fits. In diabetes, ACHIEVE-2's head-to-head showing against dapagliflozin [achieve-2-lancet] and ACHIEVE-5's insulin add-on result [achieve-5-jama] extend the evidence base — but as of 17 August 2026, the type 2 diabetes indication remains unapproved and the cardiovascular outcomes trial is still the strategic linchpin.
