Retatrutide activates the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. The clinical outcomes below distinguish bodyweight, glycaemia and biomarkers rather than treating all proposed mechanisms as demonstrated clinical benefits. [PMID:42250575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42250575/)

**Key Research:** Jastreboff AM et al. (2023) demonstrated 24.2% weight loss at 48 weeks in Phase 2. [PMID:37366315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)

### First published phase 3 result (TRANSCEND-T2D-1)

TRANSCEND-T2D-1, published in The Lancet on 6 June 2026, reports a peer-reviewed phase 3 trial of retatrutide ([PMID:42250575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42250575/), NCT06354660). It ran 40 weeks at 48 sites in the USA, Mexico and India, and randomised 537 adults whose type 2 diabetes was inadequately controlled by diet and exercise alone to once-weekly retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg or 12 mg, or placebo. Baseline mean HbA1c was 7.9% and mean BMI 35.8 kg/m².

The primary endpoint was HbA1c change at week 40, reported on the **treatment-regimen estimand** — the analysis that keeps every randomised participant in, regardless of whether they stayed on the drug. Mean change was -1.69% at 4 mg, -1.86% at 9 mg and -1.94% at 12 mg, against -0.81% with placebo. The corresponding differences versus placebo were -0.88% (95% CI -1.18 to -0.59), -1.04% (-1.32 to -0.76) and -1.12% (-1.39 to -0.85).

Bodyweight, a key secondary endpoint, fell 11.5%, 13.9% and 15.3% across the three doses against 2.6% with placebo. Gastrointestinal events were the most frequent adverse events and were generally mild to moderate; discontinuations for adverse events ran 2 to 5% on retatrutide and 0% on placebo. No severe hypoglycaemia was reported. Two deaths occurred, both in the 4 mg group, and both were reported as unrelated to the study drug.

This is a diabetes trial, not an obesity trial. The 15.3% weight reduction at 40 weeks came in a population selected for glycaemic control, and it does not answer the obesity question that the TRIUMPH program was built to answer.

### Phase 2 post-hoc cardiovascular biomarkers (not TRIUMPH)

Ruotolo et al., reviewed in the September 5, 2026 update ([PMID:42608321](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42608321/)), is a post-hoc analysis of two phase 2 randomised trials (NCT04881760, NCT04867785) using placebo-adjusted mixed models for repeated measures. It enrolled adults with obesity or overweight with type 2 diabetes (36 weeks) and without (48 weeks). Retatrutide was associated with reductions in non-HDL cholesterol (up to -21.0% and -26.9%), apolipoprotein B (-21.4% and -24.2%), total triglyceride-rich lipoprotein particles (-22.5% and -33.7%), large triglyceride-rich lipoprotein particles (-84.4% and -76.6%), triglyceride-rich lipoprotein cholesterol (-29.4% and -38.6%), total LDL particles (-19.7% and -23.5%) and small LDL particles (-32.6% and -32.3%). High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (-54.8%) and interleukin-6 (-29.6%) fell in the obesity-without-diabetes study only.

These biomarker changes do not establish fewer cardiovascular events. This is a post-hoc analysis of existing phase 2 trials, not a TRIUMPH phase 3 efficacy readout or a head-to-head against [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) or [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide).

### Phase 3 Clinical Program (TRIUMPH)

**TRIUMPH registrational program:** The Phase 3 TRIUMPH program consists of four multicenter, randomized, double-blind trials enrolling over 5,800 participants, evaluating weekly subcutaneous retatrutide against placebo for obesity and two related complications — obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and knee osteoarthritis (OA). It uses a basket trial design: two weight-management trials (TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2) with OSA/OA protocols nested within, one weight-management trial in a population with cardiovascular disease (TRIUMPH-3), and one stand-alone knee OA trial (TRIUMPH-4). The primary weight-management endpoint is percent change in body weight. [PMID:41090431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41090431/)

**Registry status checked September 5, 2026:** TRIUMPH-1 ([NCT05929066](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929066)), TRIUMPH-2 ([NCT05929079](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929079)) and TRIUMPH-3 ([NCT05882045](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05882045)) were listed as completed, without posted results in the records retrieved for this update. Completion of these trials does not imply completion of every study in the broader development program, and registry results status does not determine whether other reports exist.

**Sponsor-reported results:** Lilly announced TRIUMPH-4 toplines on [December 11, 2025](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-weight-loss-average), reported TRIUMPH-1 findings presented at the ADA meeting on [June 6, 2026](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-drove-substantial-improvements), and announced TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 toplines on [July 23, 2026](https://lilly.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-two-additional). These manufacturer announcements report trial outcomes; they are not the peer-reviewed TRANSCEND-T2D-1 paper or the TRIUMPH design paper. Headline efficacy-estimand estimates must remain distinct from treatment-regimen estimates and cannot be compared across trials as if randomized head-to-head.

### August 2026 network meta-analysis

Chen et al., published August 27, 2026, synthesized 58 trials with 24,214 participants with overweight or obesity **without diabetes**, searching through March 6, 2026. The network estimated a placebo-relative difference in percentage bodyweight change of -22.10 percentage points for retatrutide (95% CI -25.60 to -18.60). This pooled comparison is not the absolute weight loss in a new trial. Confidence intervals overlapped for several comparisons, and the network had limited direct head-to-head evidence. Low-certainty evidence suggested more treatment discontinuation with retatrutide. The paper adds synthesis of existing trials, not a new TRIUMPH result or proof of superiority over tirzepatide or semaglutide. [PMID:42688617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42688617/), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13536023/).

### Safety case report: uncertain product and competing explanations

An August 26, 2026 case report described a man with type 1 diabetes who developed gastrointestinal illness, hyperglycaemia, ketonaemia and acute kidney injury after using an online product marketed as retatrutide. **Diabetic ketoacidosis did not develop.** Concurrent Shigella infection, dehydration and insulin omission complicate attribution, and the product's composition was uncertain. The case cannot establish that retatrutide caused the illness or estimate its frequency; its relevance is the uncertainty surrounding this exposure, not evidence of efficacy or a drug-specific causal rate. This is very-low-certainty evidence for causation. [PMID:42669023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42669023/).

### Regulatory status and important limitations

As checked September 5, 2026, the FDA states that retatrutide is not a component of an FDA-approved drug and has not been found safe and effective for any condition. This is a US regulatory statement, not a worldwide approval search. [FDA: concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss).

- The published TRANSCEND-T2D-1 trial studied type 2 diabetes; its results must not be relabeled as a TRIUMPH obesity-only result. [PMID:42250575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42250575/)
- Sponsor announcements, journal publications and registry results are different evidence sources; the TRIUMPH announcement dates above should remain visible when interpreting their results.
- Gastrointestinal adverse events were common in the phase 2 obesity trial. The available follow-up cannot establish lifelong safety. [PMID:37366315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)
- Changes in cardiovascular biomarkers do not prove fewer cardiovascular events, and an indirect network ranking does not replace a direct comparative trial. [PMID:42608321](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42608321/), [PMID:42688617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42688617/)
