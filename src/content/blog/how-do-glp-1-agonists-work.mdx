## What GLP-1 mechanism studies can and cannot show

For the receptor-to-pancreas, gut, and appetite explanation, start with the [how GLP-1 agonists work guide](/guide/how-do-glp-1-agonists-work). Here the question is narrower: **what was measured in people, and what is still an interpretation?** Semaglutide activates the GLP-1 receptor; tirzepatide activates **both GIP and GLP-1 receptors**, so evidence for one cannot automatically be assigned to the other. [Wegovy label, section 12.1](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Zepbound label, section 12.1](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf)

## Claim 1: Pancreatic signaling explains glucose effects

**Supported:** The Wegovy label reports that semaglutide stimulates insulin secretion and reduces glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner. The Zepbound label reports glucose-dependent insulin secretion and reduced glucagon with tirzepatide. These pharmacodynamic observations can help explain lower blood glucose. [Wegovy label, section 12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Zepbound label, section 12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf)

**Limit:** Glucose dependence is **not** a promise that hypoglycemia cannot occur. Both current labels warn that using the product with insulin or an insulin secretagogue can increase hypoglycemia risk, including severe episodes. Zepbound's label also reports hypoglycemia in some adults without type 2 diabetes. Risk depends on the product and clinical context, not the receptor diagram alone. [Wegovy label, section 5.4](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Zepbound label, section 5.7](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf)

## Claim 2: Appetite effects are observed in humans

**Supported:** In a randomized, double-blind crossover study of **30 adults with obesity**, 12 weeks of semaglutide was associated with lower measured food intake at unrestricted meals, less reported hunger and food cravings, and weight loss compared with placebo. This is direct human evidence for changes in eating behavior under that study's conditions. [Blundell et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28266779/)

**Limit:** A small, short mechanistic study does not identify the exact neural circuit responsible, establish the contribution of every pathway, or predict an individual's long-term weight change. Wegovy's label describes GLP-1 receptors in brain areas involved in appetite, but its account of semaglutide activating specific neurons is based on **animal studies**. Human food-intake findings and animal circuit findings answer different questions. [Wegovy label, sections 12.1–12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Drucker, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29617641/)

## Claim 3: Delayed gastric emptying is one action, not a full weight-loss explanation

**Supported:** Both labels report delayed gastric emptying. Zepbound's label specifies that the delay is largest after the first dose and diminishes over time. A review of GLP-1 receptor agonist studies also describes waning gastric-emptying effects with longer-acting agents. [Wegovy label, section 12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Zepbound label, section 12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf) · [Nauck et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33068776/)

**Limit:** A slower stomach-emptying test cannot, on its own, tell us how much of a person's sustained weight change comes from the gut rather than appetite or other actions. The mechanism is a plausible contributor, not a substitute for a clinical outcome measurement. [Nauck et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33068776/) · [Wegovy label, section 12.2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf)

## Claim 4: Clinical benefit and safety need their own evidence

A receptor pathway cannot establish a cardiovascular outcome or an approved use. Wegovy's label reports clinical outcome studies and explicitly says the exact mechanism of cardiovascular risk reduction in adults has **not** been established. Product labels, populations, formulations, and endpoints therefore matter more than a class-wide mechanism claim. The dated [GLP-1 label and evidence update](/blog/what-are-glp1-agonists) covers those product-specific changes. [Wegovy label, sections 1, 12.1 and 14](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf)

The same boundary applies to safety. Wegovy and Zepbound each warn about acute pancreatitis, but the word **"rare"** without a specified product, study, and denominator would overstate what this mechanism comparison establishes. Their labels also report gastrointestinal adverse reactions. Consult the current label for the particular product rather than inferring a shared risk rate. [Wegovy label, sections 5.2 and 6](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s007lbl.pdf) · [Zepbound label, sections 5.5 and 6](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s041lbl.pdf)

**Evidence grade: moderate for this cross-product interpretation.** FDA labels support the product-specific pharmacology, warnings, and trial endpoints; a small randomized human study directly supports an appetite and food-intake observation. The exact contribution of each pathway to an individual's long-term outcome is not established here. This article is educational and does not provide medical advice or treatment recommendations.
