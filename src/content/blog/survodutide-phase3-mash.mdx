**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this briefing reported Phase 3 efficacy results for survodutide in MASH from a trial called "ACHIEVE." No such survodutide trial exists, and the figures reported in that version could not be traced to any primary source. The article has been replaced with the verified status of the program below.

## Where the Program Actually Stands

Boehringer Ingelheim's Phase 3 program for survodutide in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) consists of two registered trials:

- **LIVERAGE** — MASH with moderate or advanced liver fibrosis, approximately 1,800 participants, currently recruiting [liverage-phase3]
- **LIVERAGE - Cirrhosis** — MASH with cirrhosis, approximately 1,590 participants, currently recruiting [liverage-cirrhosis]

Neither trial has reported efficacy results. Any percentage attributed to survodutide "Phase 3 MASH" data at this time does not come from a completed registered trial.

Note that **SYNCHRONIZE** is Boehringer Ingelheim's Phase 3 *obesity* program for survodutide, not its MASH program. The two are frequently conflated.

## What Phase 2 Showed

The evidence base for survodutide in MASH is currently a single Phase 2 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine. It randomized 293 patients with biopsy-confirmed MASH and stage F1-F3 fibrosis to once-weekly subcutaneous survodutide at 2.4 mg, 4.8 mg, or 6.0 mg, or placebo, over 48 weeks [survodutide-phase2].

**MASH improvement with no worsening of fibrosis:** 47% (2.4 mg), 62% (4.8 mg), and 43% (6.0 mg) versus 14% with placebo.

**Fibrosis improvement by at least one stage:** 34%, 36%, and 34% across the three doses versus 22% with placebo.

**Liver fat reduction of at least 30%:** 63%, 67%, and 57% versus 14% with placebo.

Gastrointestinal adverse events were considerably more frequent with survodutide than placebo (nausea 66% vs. 23%; diarrhoea 49% vs. 23%; vomiting 41% vs. 4%). Serious adverse events were similar between groups (8% vs. 7%).

## The Glucagon Rationale

Survodutide's differentiated mechanism is its dual agonism. Unlike GLP-1-only agonists, survodutide also activates glucagon receptors, which are expressed in the liver. The hypothesis is that glucagon receptor activation contributes directly to hepatic lipid metabolism, in addition to the indirect benefit that follows weight loss.

Phase 2 is consistent with that hypothesis but does not establish it. Confirming whether the dual mechanism produces durable histological benefit — and whether that benefit translates into fewer liver-related clinical events — is what the LIVERAGE trials are designed to test.

## What to Watch

- Completion and readout of LIVERAGE and LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis
- Whether fibrosis improvement, which was modest relative to placebo in Phase 2, holds up in a larger population
- Long-term tolerability, given the high rate of gastrointestinal adverse events in Phase 2

Cross-trial comparisons against resmetirom, semaglutide, or tirzepatide are not reliable at this stage: MASH trials differ in fibrosis stage at entry, biopsy reading methodology, and endpoint definition.
