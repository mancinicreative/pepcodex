The proposed mechanisms of SHLP-6 are based on limited in vitro studies. The pro-apoptotic activity is observed but the molecular mechanism remains unknown.

### How It Works (Simplified)

SHLP-6 is unique among mitochondria-derived peptides for promoting cell death rather than protecting cells:

### Important Limitations

- Mechanism is observed but not explained at the molecular level
- **The direction of effect is disputed** — the 2016 discovery paper reports SHLP-6 as pro-apoptotic in cell lines, while 2025 zebrafish studies report antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects. Both cannot be the whole story. [PMID:27070352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27070352/), [PMID:40292418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40292418/)
- No binding partners or receptors have been *measured* — the only binding data are computational docking predictions. [PMID:40915070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40915070/)
- No selectivity for unwanted cells (cancer, senescent) demonstrated
- **Peptide length is not settled** — this dossier lists a 22-amino-acid sequence; the mitochondrial-genetics literature describes the canonical human peptide as 20 amino acids, with a 9-residue truncated isoform arising from the m.3017C>T polymorphism. [PMID:37675281](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37675281/)
- No one has been given SHLP-6 — the only human data measures endogenous plasma levels after exercise. [PMID:32271093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32271093/)
- In vivo mammalian effects and safety completely uncharacterized
- Translation to human physiology is entirely speculative
