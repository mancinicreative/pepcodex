## How to Read GLP-1 Class Safety Claims

Adverse-event tables and boxed-warning text live on the [GLP-1 safety overview](/safety/glp1-safety-overview). This post is class literacy: how to read a GI rate, a thyroid C-cell boxed warning, and a post-marketing signal without treating the class as one molecule.

[Semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide), [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide), and [liraglutide](/peptides/liraglutide) share a class shape. Tirzepatide is dual GIP/GLP-1. [Orforglipron](/peptides/orforglipron) is an oral small molecule, not a peptide — that distinction matters for which safety questions apply.

---

## Common Side Effects

The most frequently reported side effects are gastrointestinal in nature. These typically occur during dose initiation and titration, and often diminish over time.

### Gastrointestinal Effects

| Side Effect | Incidence Range | Notes |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Nausea** | 10-20% | Most common; usually transient |
| **Diarrhea** | 8-17% | Dose-dependent |
| **Vomiting** | 5-11% | More common during dose escalation |
| **Constipation** | 5-10% | Less common than other GI effects |
| **Abdominal pain** | 5-8% | Usually mild |
| **Dyspepsia** | 4-8% | Indigestion, heartburn |
| **Decreased appetite** | 5-10% | Often considered therapeutic effect |

### Managing GI Side Effects

Clinical trials and clinical practice suggest these strategies may help:

- **Gradual dose titration** (following recommended schedules)
- **Eating smaller meals**
- **Avoiding high-fat or greasy foods**
- **Staying hydrated**
- **Timing injections consistently**

Most patients find GI symptoms improve within 4-8 weeks of each dose increase.

## Serious Adverse Events

While less common, certain serious adverse events have been associated with GLP-1 agonists.

### Pancreatitis

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Incidence** | Rare (estimated at less than 1%) |
| **Clinical trial data** | Slightly elevated risk vs placebo in some studies |
| **FDA warning** | Listed in prescribing information |

**Symptoms to report immediately:**
- Severe abdominal pain (may radiate to back)
- Nausea and vomiting that doesn't improve
- Abdominal tenderness

**What the labels say:** the FDA-approved prescribing information directs that the drug be discontinued promptly if pancreatitis is suspected, and not restarted if pancreatitis is confirmed.

### Gallbladder Disease

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Incidence** | 1-3% in weight loss trials |
| **Risk factor** | Rapid weight loss increases risk |
| **Types** | Cholelithiasis (gallstones), cholecystitis |

Patients experiencing significant weight loss should be monitored for gallbladder symptoms.

### Acute Kidney Injury

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Mechanism** | Often related to dehydration from GI effects |
| **Risk factors** | Pre-existing kidney disease, dehydration |
| **Prevention** | Adequate hydration, monitoring during illness |

Patients with nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea should maintain adequate fluid intake.

### Diabetic Retinopathy Complications

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Observed in** | Some trials of GLP-1 agonists |
| **Proposed mechanism** | Rapid improvement in glycemic control |
| **At-risk patients** | Those with pre-existing retinopathy |

Patients with diabetic retinopathy should have regular eye examinations.

## Boxed Warnings

All GLP-1 receptor agonists (and tirzepatide) carry a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors.

### Thyroid C-Cell Tumors

**Important context:**
- This finding was observed in rats and mice given high doses
- Human thyroid C-cells have far fewer GLP-1 receptors than rodent C-cells
- No increase in MTC has been definitively confirmed in humans to date
- Post-marketing surveillance is ongoing

**Who should NOT use these medications:**
- Patients with personal history of MTC
- Patients with family history of MTC
- Patients with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications

| Contraindication | Applies To | Reason |
|------------------|------------|--------|
| Personal history of MTC | All GLP-1 agonists | Boxed warning |
| Family history of MTC | All GLP-1 agonists | Boxed warning |
| MEN 2 syndrome | All GLP-1 agonists | Boxed warning |
| Known hypersensitivity | Specific product | Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema |

Section 4 of these labels lists **only** two categories: MTC/MEN 2 history, and serious
hypersensitivity to the drug or its excipients. Two conditions widely described
elsewhere as contraindications are not ones: **pregnancy** appears in Use in Specific
Populations rather than Contraindications, and **type 1 diabetes** appears in neither — the
Wegovy label states that use in type 1 diabetes has not been evaluated, and the Ozempic and
Mounjaro labels do not mention type 1 diabetes at all.

### Conditions Named in the Labeling

These conditions are not contraindications. They appear in the Warnings and Precautions
(section 5) and Use in Specific Populations (section 8) parts of the US prescribing
information, and **what they say differs by product** — a difference a single class-wide
summary hides. The table below reports each label rather than generalizing across them.

| Condition | What the prescribing information states | Which labels |
|-----------|------------------------------------------|--------------|
| History of pancreatitis | Trial experience in these patients is limited, and whether prior pancreatitis raises the risk of pancreatitis on treatment is stated as unknown | Wegovy states this; the other labels reviewed here do not address prior pancreatitis |
| Severe gastroparesis | Use is "not recommended" in severe gastroparesis | Ozempic/Rybelsus tablets, Mounjaro, Zepbound, Saxenda. No such statement appears on the Wegovy label |
| Diabetic retinopathy in type 2 diabetes | Rapid improvement in glucose control has been associated with temporary worsening of retinopathy; the labels direct that patients with a retinopathy history be monitored for progression | All. Tirzepatide has not been studied in proliferative retinopathy, macular edema, or non-proliferative disease needing acute therapy |
| Renal impairment | No dosage adjustment is specified — the stated concern is post-marketing acute kidney injury from volume depletion when GI reactions are severe, not altered clearance | Mounjaro and Zepbound state no dosage adjustment; Ozempic/Rybelsus tablets state the dosage is the same as with normal renal function |
| Pregnancy | Discontinuation is directed once pregnancy is recognized; for the weight-management products, weight loss is described as offering no benefit in pregnancy and as possibly causing fetal harm | All |
| Planning a pregnancy | Discontinuation at least 2 months beforehand is directed, attributed in the label to semaglutide's long half-life | **Semaglutide only** (Wegovy, Ozempic/Rybelsus tablets). The tirzepatide and liraglutide labels carry no pre-pregnancy interval |
| Lactation | Ozempic/Rybelsus tablets state breastfeeding is not recommended; Wegovy states there are no data on semaglutide in human milk; tirzepatide was undetectable or low in a single-dose lactation study | Differs by product |

## Drug Interactions

### Oral Medications

GLP-1 agonists delay gastric emptying, which may affect absorption of oral medications.

| Interaction | What the labeling states | Which labels |
|-------------|---------------------------|--------------|
| **Oral medications generally** | Delayed gastric emptying has the potential to affect the absorption of co-administered oral drugs | All |
| **Insulin and sulfonylureas** | Risk of hypoglycemia is increased in combination, and the labels direct that a reduction in the dose of the insulin or secretagogue be considered | All |
| **Oral hormonal contraceptives** | Efficacy may be reduced through delayed gastric emptying; the label directs switching to a non-oral method or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and for 4 weeks after each dose increase | **Tirzepatide only** (Mounjaro, Zepbound). The semaglutide and liraglutide labels reviewed carry no equivalent direction — their contraceptive content is pharmacokinetic data only |

### Medications That May Increase Hypoglycemia Risk

When combined with insulin or sulfonylureas, GLP-1 agonists may increase hypoglycemia risk. Dose reductions of the other medications may be needed.

## Clinical Trial Safety Data

### Combined Analysis

Based on pooled clinical trial data for GLP-1 agonists:

| Safety Measure | Finding |
|----------------|---------|
| **Treatment discontinuation due to AEs** | 4-7% (vs 1-3% placebo) |
| **Serious adverse events** | Generally similar to comparators |
| **Deaths** | No increased mortality signal |
| **Cardiovascular safety** | Demonstrated in CVOTs (semaglutide SELECT trial) |

### Long-Term Data

- Most trials lasted 52-72 weeks
- Extension studies show maintained safety profile
- Post-marketing surveillance ongoing
- Cardiovascular outcomes trials have shown benefit or neutrality

## Post-Marketing Surveillance

### Ongoing Monitoring

Since FDA approval, these events are being monitored:

| Signal | Status | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Thyroid cancer | Ongoing monitoring | No confirmed signal to date |
| Pancreatitis | Known risk | Consistent with trial data |
| Gallbladder events | Known risk | Consistent with trial data |
| Ileus/bowel obstruction | Under review | Rare reports |
| Aspiration during anesthesia | Under review | Due to delayed gastric emptying |

### Anesthesia Considerations

In 2023, the American Society of Anesthesiologists issued guidance regarding GLP-1 agonists and elective procedures:

- Consider holding GLP-1 agonists before procedures requiring anesthesia
- Discuss with your anesthesiologist and prescribing physician
- Increased aspiration risk due to delayed gastric emptying

## What the 2026 Syntheses Added

Three pieces of evidence published in 2026 change the picture in specific, limited ways. All three are syntheses rather than new trials, which is the right level at which to assess class safety.

### Class-level benefit and harm, weighed together

The American College of Physicians commissioned a **living** systematic review and network meta-analysis of pharmacologic treatments in adults with overweight or obesity, covering 69 studies and 112,511 participants [acp-living-nma]. It found semaglutide and tirzepatide gave the greatest weight loss, and concluded that semaglutide **probably reduces mortality and major adverse cardiovascular events**.

A separate BMJ systematic review and network meta-analysis reached the same class-comparative question independently [bmj-nma]. Two large syntheses arriving at compatible conclusions is a stronger signal than either alone.

The safety implication is not that these drugs are risk-free. It is that on the endpoint that matters most — dying — the balance for semaglutide currently reads favourable, and that has to be weighed against the adverse events catalogued above rather than considered separately from them.

### A negative cardiovascular finding worth stating plainly

A 2026 meta-analysis found tirzepatide was **not** significantly better than GLP-1 receptor agonists for major adverse cardiovascular events in overweight or obesity: HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.70–1.04 [tirzepatide-mace-meta]. Heterogeneity was very high (I² = 90.4%) and the underlying studies were largely observational.

Read this carefully. A confidence interval crossing 1.0 means the difference is not demonstrated — it does not mean the two are equivalent, and it does not mean tirzepatide lacks cardiovascular benefit. It means this question is open, and tirzepatide's own dedicated obesity outcomes trial has not reported.

### Hepatic safety for the oral small molecule

Oral small molecules attract liver-safety scrutiny that injectable peptides generally do not. A pooled analysis across seven phase 3 orforglipron trials in 11,220 participants found **no drug-induced liver injury signal and no Hy's Law cases** [orforglipron-hepatic]. That is the largest dataset currently available on the question, and it applies to orforglipron specifically rather than to the class.

## Reporting Adverse Events

### How to Report

If you experience a serious adverse event:

1. **Contact your healthcare provider immediately**
2. **Report to FDA MedWatch:**
   - Online: www.fda.gov/medwatch
   - Phone: 1-800-FDA-1088
   - Mail: FDA MedWatch form

### What to Report

- Serious adverse events
- Unexpected side effects
- Quality problems with medication
- Therapeutic failures

## Summary

GLP-1 receptor agonists have a well-characterized safety profile based on extensive clinical trial data and growing post-marketing experience.

**Key points:**
- Most common side effects are gastrointestinal and often improve over time
- Serious events (pancreatitis, gallbladder disease) are rare, and remain under active post-marketing surveillance
- Boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors based on animal studies
- Contraindicated in patients with MTC history, family history of MTC, or MEN 2
- Drug interactions possible, especially with insulin and oral medications
- Two independent 2026 network meta-analyses place semaglutide and tirzepatide at the top of the class for weight loss, with semaglutide probably reducing mortality and MACE [acp-living-nma] [bmj-nma]
- Tirzepatide has **not** been shown superior to GLP-1 agonists for MACE in overweight or obesity — the confidence interval crosses no effect [tirzepatide-mace-meta]
