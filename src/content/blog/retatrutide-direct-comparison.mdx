**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this briefing reported head-to-head efficacy, safety, body-composition and liver-fat results comparing retatrutide with tirzepatide. Those figures could not be traced to any published trial and have been removed. The head-to-head trial exists but has not reported. This article now describes the verified state of the evidence.

## The Head-to-Head Trial Is Real — the Results Are Not Out

TRIUMPH-5 (NCT06662383) is a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind study evaluating the efficacy and safety of [retatrutide](/peptides/retatrutide) compared with [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) in adults who have obesity. It enrolled approximately 800 participants, and its primary outcome measure is percent change from baseline in body weight. As of 17 August 2026 the trial is listed as active but not recruiting, and no results have been posted [triumph-5].

Until TRIUMPH-5 reports, any specific "retatrutide beat tirzepatide by X percentage points" figure is not supported by a completed head-to-head trial.

## What the Separate Trials Show

The two drugs have so far been tested against placebo in separate programs, which is why direct comparison is difficult.

**Retatrutide, TRIUMPH-1 (Phase 3, placebo-controlled).** TRIUMPH-1 (NCT05929066) is one of four trials in the Phase 3 TRIUMPH registrational program, which enrolls over 5,800 participants across obesity, obstructive sleep apnea and knee osteoarthritis populations [triumph-program-design]. As of 17 August 2026, no TRIUMPH efficacy results appear in the verified registry — a dateless PubMed search for the TRIUMPH trials returns no efficacy or safety readouts, only the program design paper and adjacent methodology records. Percentages attributed to "retatrutide Phase 3 obesity" results do not come from a verified publication [triumph-1].

**Retatrutide, Phase 2.** The New England Journal of Medicine phase 2 trial reported mean weight loss of 24.2% at 48 weeks at the highest maintenance level [nejm-retatrutide-phase2]. This remains the verified obesity efficacy benchmark for retatrutide.

**Retatrutide, first published Phase 3 (diabetes, not obesity).** TRANSCEND-T2D-1, published in The Lancet on 6 June 2026, is the first phase 3 trial of retatrutide to report. In 537 adults with type 2 diabetes over 40 weeks, HbA1c fell 1.69 to 1.94 percentage points across the three strengths versus 0.81 with placebo on the treatment-regimen estimand, with 15.3% weight reduction at 40 weeks [transcend-t2d-1]. It is a diabetes trial in a population selected for glycaemic control, and does not answer the obesity question TRIUMPH was built for.

**Tirzepatide.** In its own placebo-controlled obesity program, tirzepatide has produced mean weight loss in the low-to-mid 20% range at the highest maintenance level.

### Why Cross-Trial Comparison Is Weak

Comparing percentages across separate trials is unreliable because the trials differ in:

- **Duration** — 72 weeks versus 80 weeks changes the achieved weight loss
- **Baseline population** — mean BMI, diabetes status, and sex distribution all shift results
- **Estimand** — treatment-policy versus efficacy estimands produce materially different numbers from the same data
- **Dose escalation schedule** — affects both efficacy and dropout

This is precisely the problem TRIUMPH-5 was designed to solve.

## The Triple Agonist Concept

Retatrutide activates three receptors rather than one (semaglutide) or two (tirzepatide):

**GLP-1 receptor:** appetite suppression, glucose-dependent insulin secretion, delayed gastric emptying.

**GIP receptor:** amplifies GLP-1 effects on appetite and insulin secretion, with additional effects on adipose tissue.

**Glucagon receptor:** increases energy expenditure and promotes hepatic fat oxidation. This is the component that distinguishes retatrutide from tirzepatide, and it is the mechanistic reason to expect a difference — but a mechanistic rationale is not evidence of superiority.

## What to Watch

- **TRIUMPH-5 readout** — the only trial that can settle the comparison
- **TRIUMPH obesity publications** — the completed weight-management trials in the TRIUMPH basket have not published efficacy results as of 17 August 2026 [triumph-program-design]
- **Tolerability trade-off** — in the phase 2 trial, gastrointestinal adverse events were common and rose with the amount administered [nejm-retatrutide-phase2], so any efficacy advantage has to be weighed against tolerability
- **Regulatory status** — retatrutide remains investigational and is not approved by the FDA; its only published phase 3 evidence is in type 2 diabetes [transcend-t2d-1]
