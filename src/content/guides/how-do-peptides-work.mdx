## What does it mean for a peptide to work?

A peptide is a chain of amino acids. Its effects depend on the **particular molecule** and its biological context. Some peptides act as signals by engaging cell receptors. Semaglutide, a modified GLP-1 analogue, provides a well-studied example; it does not describe every peptide. Structural researchers have observed semaglutide bound to the GLP-1 receptor and its associated G protein ([Zhang et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34260945/)).

**Evidence grade: moderate for this GLP-1 receptor example.** The cited structural and cell experiments support specific mechanistic steps. They do not establish an effect for an unrelated peptide.

## Three steps in a receptor example

### 1. The molecule engages a receptor

The GLP-1 receptor is a protein on the cell surface. Cryo-electron microscopy captured semaglutide in a complex with that receptor and a G protein. The images help identify how this **specific analogue** engages its target; a peptide's name or shape alone cannot prove receptor activity ([Zhang et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34260945/)).

### 2. The cell relays the signal

Receptor activity can be examined through downstream measurements. In experiments on pancreatic β-cells and islets, researchers measured the signaling molecule cAMP and insulin release while probing the GLP-1 receptor's role with an antagonist. Those experiments tested GLP-1 receptor biology; they were **not a clinical trial of semaglutide** ([Shuai et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33369112/)).

### 3. Researchers check the outcome separately

Observing a receptor complex or a cell response does not by itself establish a health benefit in people. A clinical claim needs studies of the actual product and outcome. Our [analysis of mechanism claims](/blog/how-do-peptides-work) follows that evidence chain using the same GLP-1 example.

## Why peptide structure and duration matter

Changing a peptide can affect both receptor activity and how long the molecule remains available. In the original semaglutide discovery work, researchers assessed GLP-1 receptor potency, resistance to enzymatic degradation, and albumin binding while developing a longer-acting analogue ([Lau et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26308095/)). These are measured properties of semaglutide, not a duration rule for peptides as a group.

When reading a mechanism claim, identify the exact molecule, the receptor or other measured target, the experimental system, and the outcome. The [peptide-versus-protein terminology guide](/guide/peptide-vs-protein) explains the underlying names.
