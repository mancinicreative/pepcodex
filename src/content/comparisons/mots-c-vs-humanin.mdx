## Overview

MOTS-c and Humanin are mitochondrial-derived peptides studied in different research settings. MOTS-c is a 16-residue peptide with cell/animal experiments and indirect human biomarker observations. Mitochondrial origin does not establish a shared treatment effect or a universal age-related decline.

## Scope of this update

MOTS-c evidence was reviewed on September 5, 2026. The retained Humanin material has not been independently re-audited in this update, including its clinical and regulatory statements. This page is not a current regulatory-status comparison.

The MOTS-c figures describe 12 selected dossier publications: four primary reports with human biomarker observations, five preclinical reports, two reviews and one hypothesis paper. Mixed human/animal reports are counted once under human evidence. These are not four native MOTS-c treatment trials; registry entries are excluded. This selected inventory is not an exhaustive literature census, and publication counts do not rank clinical benefit.

The [MOTS-c dossier](/peptides/mots-c) provides the full selected inventory and [safety page](/safety/mots-c-safety) describes its limits.

### Significance of MDP Discovery

MOTS-c discovery identified a mitochondrial coding origin and experimentally studied metabolic effects. That origin does not establish a therapeutic effect, longevity benefit or interchangeability with other mitochondrial-derived peptides. [PMID:25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/)

### MOTS-c Mechanisms

The 2015 discovery paper linked MOTS-c to folate/purine metabolism and AMPK-related effects in cells and mice. A later cell study examined nuclear translocation during metabolic stress and interactions with stress-response signaling. These experiments do not demonstrate treatment benefit in people. [PMID:25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/), [PMID:29983246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29983246/)

Mechanistic activity is not uniformly beneficial. A 2026 study of mesenchymal stromal cells from human donors reported AMPK activation alongside reduced proliferation and increased senescence/inflammatory signals; MOTS-c-pretreated cells did not improve repair in the mouse kidney-injury model. This is a cell/animal counterexample, not a measured rate of harm in patients. [PMID:42324588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324588/)

### Humanin Mechanisms

1. **Receptor Signaling**
   - FPRL1/2 receptor binding
   - ERK1/2 and STAT3 activation
   - Anti-apoptotic gene expression

2. **BAX Inhibition**
   - Direct binding to pro-apoptotic BAX
   - Prevents mitochondrial permeabilization
   - Blocks death cascade

3. **Cytoprotection**
   - Protects neurons from toxicity
   - Guards against amyloid-beta
   - Broad anti-stress effects

4. **IGF Modulation**
   - IGFBP-3 binding
   - Modulates insulin/IGF signaling

## Human Biomarker Data

### MOTS-c in Humans

A meta-analysis of case-control/cross-sectional studies reported lower concentrations in type 2 diabetes but higher concentrations in obesity. Association with a condition is not evidence that administering MOTS-c treats it. [PMID:39160573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39160573/)

Plasma and muscle findings differ: one study of healthy men found lower plasma concentrations but higher skeletal-muscle expression with age. The early mitochondrial-variant longevity paper proposed a hypothesis rather than establishing that the variant extends life. [PMID:32182209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32182209/), [PMID:26289118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26289118/)

A 2021 publication combined mouse intervention experiments with measurements of endogenous MOTS-c around exercise in humans. A 2026 cerebral-palsy/typically-developing-group study reported comparable basal concentrations and absent or modest exercise responses. These observations do not show that administering native MOTS-c reproduces exercise benefits in people. [PMID:33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/), [PMID:42349896](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42349896/)

## Disease Application Research

### MOTS-c Applications

Cell and mouse research has examined metabolic regulation, physical performance, islets and bone loss. These models are not established clinical indications. The donor-cell kidney-repair counterexample prevents a general organ-repair claim. See the study-specific findings above.

## Administration Research

The reviewed sources do not establish human native-MOTS-c pharmacokinetics or a validated administration regimen. CB4211 must be treated as a separate analogue. The native-labelled registry entry NCT07505745 is a record whose study conduct and peptide administration remain independently unverified. A separate same-sponsor TB-500 record explicitly identifies itself as fictional, warranting caution without proving that this MOTS-c record is fictional. NCT07678073 compares anesthetic techniques and measures MOTS-c as a biomarker; it does not administer MOTS-c. NCT03998514 concerns CB4211, an optimized analogue, whose findings cannot establish native MOTS-c safety or effectiveness. The [MOTS-c trial-record review](/blog/motsc-human-trials) describes these identity and authenticity limits.

## Safety Considerations

### MOTS-c

The selected native MOTS-c evidence does not establish clinical safety. Endogenous concentrations do not demonstrate tolerability of administered peptide. The donor-cell/mouse findings above include impaired repair, which limits assumptions based on favorable mechanisms. [PMID:42324588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324588/)

FDA's May 2026 briefing did not identify in-vivo pharmacokinetic studies; in-vitro degradation in human blood does not establish a half-life in people. Its compounding safety page identifies immunogenicity, impurity and identity-characterization concerns and a withdrawn nomination. These statements do not quantify clinical risk or establish safety of combinations. [FDA briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download), [FDA safety-risk page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

### Key Takeaways

- MOTS-c clinical-benefit evidence is very low despite experimental and human biomarker publications.
- Human observations, native-peptide interventions, analogues and biomarker-only trials must remain separate.
- Tissue age associations and exercise responses vary; mechanistic activity is not uniformly beneficial.
- The MOTS-c sources reviewed here do not establish clinical benefit or safety of combining these peptides. Mechanistic differences do not demonstrate compatibility or synergy.
- Humanin's remaining claims require an independent source review; this MOTS-c update does not certify them.
