The FDA approved Zepbound ([tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide)) on 20 December 2024 for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity [fda-zepbound-osa]. Drugs@FDA lists that action on NDA 217806 as supplement 13, dated 20 December 2024, classified Efficacy-New Indication [drugsfda-zepbound-217806]. This is the first FDA-approved medication specifically for OSA; CPAP remains the usual device-based treatment.

## The Sleep Apnea Burden

Obstructive sleep apnea is common in adults with obesity. The condition causes:

- **Repeated breathing interruptions:** Airways collapse during sleep, causing oxygen drops
- **Fragmented sleep:** Continuous arousals prevent restorative sleep
- **Daytime consequences:** Fatigue, cognitive impairment, increased accident risk
- **Cardiovascular risk:** Associated with hypertension, heart disease, stroke, and sudden death

Current treatment relies primarily on CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure) machines, which are effective but have significant adherence challenges. Studies show only 50-60% of prescribed patients consistently use CPAP [sleep-apnea-treatment-review].

## The SURMOUNT-OSA Trials

The approval was based on results from the SURMOUNT-OSA Phase 3 program, which evaluated tirzepatide in adults with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity.

### Study Design

Two parallel trials randomised 469 adults with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity [surmount-osa-results]:
- **Trial 1:** not on PAP at baseline (n=234)
- **Trial 2:** on PAP at baseline (n=235)

Participants were assigned to the maximum tolerated dose of tirzepatide or placebo for 52 weeks. In trial 2, PAP was suspended for 7 days before the scheduled sleep studies so PAP would not confound the AHI measurement [surmount-osa-results].

### Primary Results

The primary end point was change in apnea-hypopnea index (AHI; events per hour of sleep) at week 52 [surmount-osa-results].

**Trial 1 (not on PAP):**
| Measure | Tirzepatide | Placebo |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| AHI change (primary) | −25.3 events/hour | −5.3 events/hour |
| Percent change in AHI (secondary) | −50.7% | −3.0% |
| AHI &lt;5, or AHI 5–14 with ESS ≤10 (secondary) | 42.2% | 15.9% |
| Percent change in body weight (secondary) | −17.7% | −1.6% |

**Trial 2 (on PAP at baseline):**
| Measure | Tirzepatide | Placebo |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| AHI change (primary) | −29.3 events/hour | −5.5 events/hour |
| Percent change in AHI (secondary) | −58.7% | −2.5% |
| AHI &lt;5, or AHI 5–14 with ESS ≤10 (secondary) | 50.2% | 14.3% |
| Percent change in body weight (secondary) | −19.6% | −2.3% |

Estimated treatment differences for the primary AHI change were −20.0 events/hour in trial 1 (95% CI −25.8 to −14.2) and −23.8 in trial 2 (95% CI −29.6 to −17.9) [surmount-osa-results]. The combined secondary (AHI below 5, or mild AHI with ESS ≤10) is not the same as “AHI below 5” alone.

### Secondary outcomes that are in the paper

Systolic blood pressure at week 48: −9.5 vs −1.8 mm Hg in trial 1; −7.6 vs −3.9 mm Hg in trial 2 [surmount-osa-results]. FDA's approval note says the AHI improvement is likely related to body-weight reduction [fda-zepbound-osa].

This page does not quote Epworth percent reductions, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, or SF-36 figures that are not in the SURMOUNT-OSA abstract or the published key-secondary table fetched 2026-09-02.

## Mechanism of Benefit

FDA's 20 December 2024 approval states that by reducing body weight, studies show Zepbound also improves OSA, and that the AHI improvement is likely related to that weight reduction [fda-zepbound-osa]. SURMOUNT-OSA was not designed to separate weight loss from other mechanisms [surmount-osa-results].

## Clinical Implications

This page is not a treatment guide. FDA approved Zepbound for moderate to severe OSA in adults with obesity, with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity [fda-zepbound-osa]. Who is on the label, and who is not, is the prescribing information — not a numbered clinic pathway on this site.

Coverage, prior authorization, and price are payer-specific. This page does not quote a list price.

## Comparison to Other Approaches

SURMOUNT-OSA compared tirzepatide with placebo, not with CPAP, surgery, or oral appliances as randomised arms [surmount-osa-results]. Trial 2 enrolled people already on PAP and suspended PAP before the sleep studies. That is not a head-to-head “tirzepatide vs CPAP” efficacy table.

## Considerations and Limitations

### Not a Cure

Tirzepatide does not cure OSA:

- Benefits require ongoing treatment
- AHI may return if medication stopped
- Weight regain would likely worsen OSA
- Some patients may need combination with CPAP

### Who May Not Benefit

Certain patients may be poor candidates:

- OSA without significant obesity
- Anatomical causes (large tonsils, jaw abnormalities)
- Central sleep apnea (different mechanism)
- Contraindications to GLP-1/GIP agonists

### What Stays Open After Treatment

Because tirzepatide reduces AHI rather than eliminating it, OSA remains an open clinical question after weight loss rather than a resolved one. The questions that persist include:

- Whether a repeat sleep study after weight stabilization shows a changed severity category
- Whether ongoing CPAP is still needed
- Whether residual symptoms persist
- What cardiovascular risk remains

## Broader Implications

### Expanding GLP-1 Indications

This approval continues the expansion of incretin-based therapy indications. Zepbound's OSA use is the 20 December 2024 FDA action cited above [fda-zepbound-osa]. Other labelled uses for tirzepatide or semaglutide are not restated here unless they sit on this page's `sources[]`.

The pipeline is following a similar path: retatrutide is being studied in obstructive sleep apnea in the TRIUMPH phase 3 program [triumph-osa-rationale].

### Addressing Root Causes

The approval represents a shift toward addressing obesity as the root cause of multiple conditions rather than treating each consequence separately.

## What This Means

The FDA approval of Zepbound for obstructive sleep apnea provides patients with a new treatment option that addresses the underlying cause of their condition rather than just managing symptoms. For the millions of patients who struggle with CPAP adherence or prefer not to use a device, tirzepatide offers a meaningful alternative.

This approval also reinforces the growing recognition that obesity is a disease requiring treatment, and that effective obesity treatment can resolve or improve many obesity-related conditions.
