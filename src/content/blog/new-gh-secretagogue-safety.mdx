Growth hormone secretagogues stimulate endogenous growth hormone release. Receptor-cloning research established a distinct pathway through which these compounds act, but that mechanistic result cannot establish a drug's clinical benefit or safety [pmid-8688086]. MK-677, also called ibutamoren or LUM-201, provides a useful human example of that distinction [pmid-18981485] [nct-06948214].

## What a human MK-677 trial found

A randomized, placebo-controlled study enrolled 65 healthy adults aged 60–81. Its primary body-composition assessment was at one year. MK-677 increased GH, IGF-1 and fat-free mass, without a significant difference in visceral or total fat mass [pmid-18981485].

The increase in fat-free mass did not produce a detected improvement in strength or function. The authors cautioned that the sample size and duration were insufficient for evaluating functional endpoints. This is neither proof of a functional benefit nor proof that every possible functional effect is absent [pmid-18981485].

Fasting glucose rose and insulin sensitivity fell. Increased appetite, transient mild leg edema and muscle pain were among the reported adverse effects. These observations apply to this compound and studied population; they do not establish how it compares with another secretagogue [pmid-18981485].

## How to interpret the safety concerns

FDA states that ibutamoren is not approved. Its safety notification identifies potential glucose and insulin-sensitivity changes, fluid retention and a possible increased risk of congestive heart failure in some people. It also emphasizes uncertainty about long-term effects [fda-ibutamoren-warning].

Those concerns do not provide a precise adverse-event rate for every population. A study in healthy older adults and a trial in children with growth hormone deficiency address different clinical questions. Safety conclusions need the population, comparator and follow-up alongside the result [pmid-18981485] [nct-06948214].

## Research is still active

The ClinicalTrials.gov record for NCT06948214 lists a recruiting Phase 3 trial of LUM-201 in children with growth hormone deficiency, checked September 29, 2026. No results are posted in that record. An ongoing trial demonstrates active investigation; it does not establish efficacy, comparative safety or approval [nct-06948214].

## Reading claims about a “safer” secretagogue

The mechanistic study, older-adult trial and pediatric registration cited here answer different questions. Together, they do not support ranking MK-677 against ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 or hexarelin for overall safety. A stronger comparison would need directly relevant comparative evidence and meaningful clinical outcomes, rather than a table of hormone changes from separate experiments [pmid-8688086] [pmid-18981485] [nct-06948214].
