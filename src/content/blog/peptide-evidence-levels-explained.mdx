## A claim narrow enough to assess

What does the evidence say about **weight loss in adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition, who do not have diabetes**? That is more answerable than “does semaglutide work?” The [STEP 1 randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185/) enrolled 1,961 adults in that population. Participants received subcutaneous semaglutide or placebo, both alongside a lifestyle intervention, and were followed for 68 weeks. This is an example of reading evidence, not treatment guidance.

## What the sources establish

| Question | What the source shows | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did the trial measure a human outcome directly? | STEP 1 randomized adults and measured change in body weight. Mean change at 68 weeks was -14.9% with semaglutide and -2.4% with placebo. [PMID: 33567185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185/) | The result is for the studied population, comparison, outcome, and time frame. It does not measure every use of semaglutide. |
| Is this an approved use? | The [FDA prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s005lbl.pdf) includes weight reduction and long-term maintenance indications for specified populations and summarizes additional clinical studies. | Approval is a separate regulatory decision; it is not a grade for all possible benefits or harms. |
| Are safety questions closed? | The same [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/218316s005lbl.pdf) describes warnings, contraindications, and adverse reactions. | A weight-loss result does not establish safety for every person or settle every rare or long-term risk. |

## What this example says about the badge

The [semaglutide dossier](/peptides/semaglutide) shows a **High Evidence** badge for the compound. For **adult weight-loss efficacy in the population studied**, STEP 1 provides direct randomized human evidence, and the FDA label summarizes a broader clinical program. Those sources help explain why this particular claim has substantial support. The compound badge does **not** assign a separate high grade to that outcome or to every other claim about semaglutide. It is not a formal GRADE rating. A formal GRADE assessment examines the full body of evidence for a defined outcome, including risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. [PMID: 21208779](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21208779/)

Do not read the compound's High badge as a high-certainty conclusion for a different indication, population, or safety outcome. For those questions, start again with the claim and its relevant evidence. [GRADE's original framework](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18436948/) also separates confidence in evidence from a recommendation to use a treatment.
