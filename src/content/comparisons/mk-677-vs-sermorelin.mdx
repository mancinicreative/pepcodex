## What the comparison can establish

MK-677 (ibutamoren) is a **nonpeptide, orally studied growth hormone secretagogue** acting at the ghrelin receptor. Sermorelin is a **peptide growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog**. Receptor differences explain the research question; they do not establish that one has better muscle, sleep, metabolic or longevity outcomes. [FDA ibutamoren evaluation](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download); [FDA review noting Geref withdrawal](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2017/205598Orig1s000MedR.pdf).

| Issue | MK-677 | Sermorelin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pathway | Ghrelin receptor | GHRH receptor |
| Studied formulation | Oral | Injected historical Geref product |
| FDA history | No approved ibutamoren product | Historical Geref approval, followed by market withdrawal |
| Anti-aging or strength benefit | Not established | Not established by the historical approval |

In a randomized **65-person healthy older-adult** MK-677 study, GH, IGF-1 and fat-free mass rose. Strength and function did not improve significantly, but the authors said the study was insufficiently powered and too short to evaluate functional endpoints. Visceral fat did not significantly decline. Fasting glucose rose and insulin sensitivity fell. The findings cannot be read as either proven functional benefit or proof that no functional effect is possible. [Nass et al., PMID 18981485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18981485/).

The FDA's Geref record describes sermorelin's **historical approval and withdrawal** and says the withdrawal was not for safety or effectiveness reasons. That determination does not establish a “cleaner” glucose profile than MK-677 or validate adult anti-aging use. We found no direct comparative metabolic or functional trial to rank the two. [FDA medical review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2017/205598Orig1s000MedR.pdf).

Merck ended its earlier MK-0677 program, but the same molecule is in later pediatric development as **LUM-201**. The [official NCT06948214 Phase 3 registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06948214) was recruiting in the September 29, 2026 registry archive; neither a development program nor a compounding discussion makes ibutamoren FDA approved. [FDA ibutamoren evaluation](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download).

No cited controlled trial validates combining MK-677 with sermorelin. This page does not provide a treatment or sourcing recommendation.
