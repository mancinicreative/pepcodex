On 18 December 2025, Novo Nordisk announced the submission of a New Drug Application (NDA) to the FDA for once-weekly CagriSema (cagrilintide 2.4 mg / semaglutide 2.4 mg) for chronic weight management in adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity. If approved, CagriSema would be the first injectable GLP-1 receptor agonist and amylin analogue combination [novo-cagrisema-nda].

## What Is CagriSema?

CagriSema combines two peptide mechanisms in a single weekly injection:

**Semaglutide 2.4mg:** The GLP-1 receptor agonist already approved as Wegovy for obesity, providing appetite suppression through central and peripheral effects.

**Cagrilintide 2.4mg:** A long-acting amylin analog that complements GLP-1 effects through different but synergistic pathways [amylin-obesity-review].

### The Amylin Mechanism

Amylin is a hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells. It contributes to satiety and glucose regulation through:

- **Gastric emptying:** Slows stomach emptying, promoting fullness
- **Glucagon suppression:** Reduces post-meal glucagon secretion
- **Central satiety:** Acts on brain satiety centers
- **Different pathway than GLP-1:** Complementary rather than redundant effects

The native amylin peptide (pramlintide, brand name Symlin) is approved for diabetes but requires three-times-daily injection. Cagrilintide is acylated for once-weekly dosing.

## The REDEFINE Phase 3 Program

### REDEFINE 1: Primary Weight Loss Trial

The pivotal REDEFINE 1 trial (NCT05567796) randomised 3,417 adults without diabetes who had obesity (BMI ≥30) or overweight (BMI ≥27) with at least one weight-related complication:

- **Duration:** 68 weeks
- **Randomization:** 21:3:3:7 to CagriSema (n=2,108), semaglutide 2.4 mg (n=302), cagrilintide 2.4 mg (n=302), or placebo (n=705), all with lifestyle intervention
- **Primary endpoints:** Percent weight change and proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss

**Results:**
| Arm | Mean weight change at week 68 — treatment-policy estimand (published primary analysis) |
|-----|-------------------------------|
| CagriSema | -20.4% (treatment-policy) |
| Placebo | -3.0% (treatment-policy) |

The CagriSema-versus-placebo difference was -17.3 percentage points (95% CI -18.1 to -16.6; P&lt;0.001) [redefine-trials-overview]. The PubMed abstract fetched 2026-09-02 does not report a trial-product percent; this page does not quote one.

### REDEFINE 2: Type 2 Diabetes Population

The REDEFINE 2 trial (NCT05394519) evaluated CagriSema in adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes:

- **Enrollment:** 1,206 randomised, 3:1 to CagriSema (n=904) or placebo (n=302)
- **Duration:** 68 weeks
- **Population:** BMI ≥27 kg/m², HbA1c 7-10%

**Results:**
| Outcome (REDEFINE 2, type 2 diabetes, 68 weeks) | CagriSema | Placebo |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Mean weight change — treatment-policy estimand (published primary) | -13.7% | -3.4% |
| Reached HbA1c ≤6.5% | 73.5% | 15.9% |

The estimated difference in mean percent weight change was −10.4 percentage points (95% CI −11.2 to −9.5; P&lt;0.001) [pmid-40544432]. The PubMed abstract fetched 2026-09-02 does not report a trial-product percent; this page does not quote one.

REDEFINE 2 compared CagriSema against placebo only — it had no semaglutide comparator arm. Weight loss was smaller than in REDEFINE 1, consistent with the general pattern that people with type 2 diabetes lose less weight on incretin-based therapy.

### Additional REDEFINE Trials

Other trials in the programme include:

- **REDEFINE 3:** An event-driven cardiovascular outcomes trial of CagriSema versus placebo in roughly 7,000 adults with established cardiovascular disease, with or without type 2 diabetes
- **REDEFINE 4:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06131437 is an 84-week open-label (masking: none) randomised phase 3 trial of CagriSema versus tirzepatide; actual enrollment 809; primary outcome is non-inferiority on relative body-weight change at week 84. Status COMPLETED (primary completion 2025-12-08; study completion 2026-01-09; last update posted 2026-02-06). ## Efficacy Analysis

### Where CagriSema Sits Among Obesity Therapies

CagriSema's REDEFINE 1 result places it at the top of the injectable field on the published placebo-controlled primary analysis. The registered tirzepatide head-to-head (NCT06131437) is a different trial, a longer duration, and — as of 2026-09-02 — has no PubMed results record, so it does not supply a second percent for ranking [nct-06131437]. Cross-trial comparisons with other agents should be treated with caution: trial populations, durations, estimands and adherence differ.

### Responder Analysis

The REDEFINE 1 abstract reports that participants receiving CagriSema were more likely than those receiving placebo to reach the 5%, 20%, 25%, and 30% weight-loss targets (P&lt;0.001 for all comparisons) [redefine-trials-overview]. It does not publish the percent of participants at each threshold; this page does not invent those percents.

### Combination Versus Components

REDEFINE 1 randomised monotherapy arms (semaglutide n=302; cagrilintide n=302) as well as CagriSema (n=2,108) and placebo (n=705) [redefine-trials-overview]. The PubMed abstract fetched 2026-09-02 reports the CagriSema-versus-placebo mean (−20.4% vs −3.0%) and does not report the monotherapy percents; this page does not quote them.

## Safety Profile

### Gastrointestinal Events

As expected with incretin-based therapies, GI events were common. In REDEFINE 1 they occurred in 79.6% of the CagriSema group versus 39.9% on placebo [redefine-trials-overview], and in REDEFINE 2 in 72.5% versus 34.4% [pmid-40544432]. Investigators described them as mainly transient and mild-to-moderate in severity.

## What This Means

The CagriSema NDA filing is a regulatory milestone, not an approval. REDEFINE 1's published primary analysis was −20.4% versus −3.0% at 68 weeks on the treatment-policy estimand [redefine-trials-overview].

For patients struggling with obesity, this filing brings a further treatment option closer. For the field, REDEFINE 1's inclusion of both monotherapy arms is the more interesting result: it shows the combination outperforms either peptide alone, while falling short of a simple additive effect.

The FDA decision, expected in late 2026, will determine whether this combination reaches patients.
