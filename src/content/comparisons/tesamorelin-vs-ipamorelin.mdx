## Compare endpoints before compounds

Tesamorelin's FDA label covers reduction of **excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy**. Its phase 3 studies measured VAT, not a general weight-loss or anti-aging outcome. The label states long-term cardiovascular safety is unknown. [FDA prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=839334d3-8c1d-4c26-9036-2ab524a6ea75); [Falutz et al., 2007, PMID 18057338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18057338/).

Ipamorelin is a ghrelin-receptor agonist rather than a GHRH analog. A phase 2 randomized proof-of-concept study enrolled 117 bowel-resection patients to assess postoperative ileus. The median first-tolerated-meal times differed numerically, but the reported comparison was **not statistically significant** (p = 0.15). This is neither a VAT trial nor proof of benefit for general body composition. [Beck et al., 2014, PMID 25331030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25331030/).

FDA's ipamorelin compounding review notes serious adverse events, including deaths, in the intravenous postoperative study and insufficient safety information for certain other injectable routes. A bulk-substance or compounding assessment is not an FDA product approval. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks).

No cited direct trial compares tesamorelin with ipamorelin, and mechanistic differences do not establish a treatment ranking or combination safety.
