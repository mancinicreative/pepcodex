## Safety Overview

Tirzepatide has been evaluated in the SURPASS and SURMOUNT clinical trial programs with over 10,000 patient exposures.

## Common Side Effects

| Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Nausea | 12-18% | Transient, decreases over time |
| Diarrhea | 12-17% | Correlates with treatment intensity |
| Vomiting | 5-9% | More common during titration |
| Constipation | 6-7% | Less common than diarrhea |
| Decreased appetite | 5-10% | Part of mechanism |
| Dyspepsia | 5-8% | Indigestion symptoms |

## Serious Warnings

### Boxed Warning: Thyroid C-Cell Tumors
Rodent studies showed thyroid tumors. Clinical relevance to humans is uncertain but contraindicated in those at risk.

### Other Serious Risks
- **Pancreatitis** — Rare but serious; the prescribing information directs discontinuation if it is suspected
- **Gallbladder disease** — Increased with rapid weight loss
- **Hypoglycemia** — Increased risk with insulin or sulfonylureas

## Who Should NOT Use Tirzepatide

- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)
- Known hypersensitivity to tirzepatide

## Special Populations

The two tirzepatide products are labeled differently here, and the differences matter.

- **Pregnancy** — Both labels direct discontinuation once pregnancy is recognized. Zepbound's
  states that weight loss offers no benefit to a pregnant patient and may cause fetal harm;
  Mounjaro's states it should be used in pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the
  potential risk to the fetus. Neither label carries a pre-pregnancy discontinuation interval —
  that appears on the semaglutide labels, attributed to semaglutide's long half-life.
- **Lactation** — In a clinical lactation study following a single administration, tirzepatide
  in breast milk was undetectable or low relative to the amount the mother received. There are
  no data on effects on the breastfed infant or
  on milk production, and both labels frame this as a weighing of breastfeeding's benefits
  against the mother's clinical need rather than as a prohibition.
- **Pediatric use** — The two products diverge. Mounjaro's safety and effectiveness for
  glycemic control in patients aged 10 and older with type 2 diabetes are **established**, on
  the basis of a 30-week randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with a 22-week
  open-label extension in 99 pediatric patients. Zepbound's safety and effectiveness in
  pediatric patients have not been established.
- **Renal impairment** — Neither product's labeling specifies any adjustment for renal
  impairment, and no change in
  tirzepatide pharmacokinetics was observed even in end-stage renal disease. The labeled
  concern is acute kidney injury arising from volume depletion when gastrointestinal reactions
  are severe, not altered clearance of the drug.
