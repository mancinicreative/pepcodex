## Read the explanation behind the label

PepCodex's evidence labels are **high**, **moderate**, **low**, and **very-low**. Read a label together with its supporting studies, assessment date, and explanation of limitations. This guide does not independently regrade individual compounds or supply a new rule for assigning those labels.

When checking a dossier, ask:

- Which claim and outcome does the assessment address?
- Which studies support it, and which populations were studied?
- Does the explanation distinguish human results from laboratory or animal findings?
- What limitations or unanswered questions are recorded?
- Is there a dated assessment explaining the label, or only the label itself?

If the explanation is missing, treat that as a gap to investigate. Do not fill it in from a compound's name, trial phase, or number of citations.

## How this relates to GRADE

GRADE is a documented approach to assessing a body of evidence for specified outcomes. Its criteria include risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. It also calls for transparent records of the evidence and assessment methods. [GRADE Working Group: criteria for applying or using GRADE](https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/).

Using the same category names does not establish that a PepCodex rating followed GRADE. Look for a documented assessment before making that inference. This page does not certify the site's ratings as GRADE assessments.

## Keep regulatory status and evidence questions separate

Locate a dossier's regulatory-status information separately from its evidence assessment. Check the named product, use, jurisdiction, date, and cited regulatory source. Do not interpret an evidence label as a treatment recommendation or a statement that every proposed use has been established.
