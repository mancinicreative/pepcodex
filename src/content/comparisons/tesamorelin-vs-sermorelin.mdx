## Same receptor family, different clinical histories

Tesamorelin is a GHRH analog with a current FDA label for reducing **excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy**. Its pivotal randomized studies measured VAT, not general weight loss or anti-aging. Long-term cardiovascular safety remains unestablished in labeling. [FDA prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=839334d3-8c1d-4c26-9036-2ab524a6ea75); [Falutz et al., 2007, PMID 18057338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18057338/).

Sermorelin is a shorter GHRH fragment. A historical multicenter **open-label** study of 110 previously untreated prepubertal children with GH deficiency reported increased height velocity; 86 children were eligible for efficacy analysis. That pediatric study does not test adult VAT reduction or directly compare sermorelin with tesamorelin. [Geref International Study Group, 1996, PMID 8772599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8772599/).

| Question | Tesamorelin | Sermorelin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence grade for cited outcome | High for VAT reduction in indicated adults | Low to moderate for historical pediatric growth response; open-label design limits inference |
| Studied population | Adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat | Children with GH deficiency |
| FDA product history | Currently labeled for the stated indication | Historical Geref products are on FDA's discontinued list |
| Safety inference | Label warnings and unknown long-term cardiovascular safety | Discontinuation was not for safety or effectiveness; that is not a blanket safety finding |

In 2013 FDA determined the former Geref products **were not withdrawn for reasons of safety or effectiveness** and kept them on the Orange Book's discontinued list. That finding should not be recast as proof that sermorelin was unsafe, still marketed as Geref, or that a compounded preparation is FDA approved. [FDA Federal Register determination, 2013](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2013-04827.pdf); [FDA on compounded drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounding-oversight-and-compliance-actions).

The cited records offer no head-to-head efficacy ranking or validated combination claim. Trial outcomes in different ages and indications cannot be compared as if they share a patient population.
