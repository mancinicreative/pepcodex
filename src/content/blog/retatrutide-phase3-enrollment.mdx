Eli Lilly's TRIUMPH programme is the phase 3 obesity registration package for retatrutide, a triple agonist of the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. The headline that put the compound on the map came from phase 2. The first phase 3 paper to actually publish came from a different programme entirely — and it is worth separating what has read out from what has not.

## What Has Published

**TRANSCEND-T2D-1** ([NCT06354660](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06354660)) appeared in *The Lancet* in June 2026: a double-blind, randomised phase 3 trial in 537 people with type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled by diet and exercise [pmid-42250575]. This is the first published phase 3 result for retatrutide in any indication.

On the treatment-regimen estimand — the published primary analysis, which counts participants as randomised regardless of whether they stayed on treatment — mean HbA1c change at week 40 was:

| Arm | HbA1c change | Bodyweight change |
|---|---|---|
| Retatrutide 4 mg | −1.69% | −11.5% |
| Retatrutide 9 mg | −1.86% | −13.9% |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | −1.94% | −15.3% |
| Placebo | −0.81% | −2.6% |

Two things follow. The glycaemic effect is substantial and separates cleanly from placebo. And the weight change in a *diabetes* population at 40 weeks is well below the 24.2% seen in the phase 2 *obesity* trial at 48 weeks — which is the expected pattern, since weight loss with incretin therapy is consistently smaller in people with type 2 diabetes.

## What Has Not Published

The TRIUMPH obesity trials — TRIUMPH-1 ([NCT05929066](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05929066)), NCT05929079, NCT05882045 and NCT05931367 — have **not** published results and have posted no registry results as of 17 August 2026. TRIUMPH-1 randomised more than 2,300 adults with obesity or overweight to retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg, 12 mg, or placebo [triumph-program].

The obesity headline everyone quotes therefore still rests on phase 2. The [retatrutide dossier](/peptides/retatrutide) carries the full trial register and the current regulatory position: not FDA-approved, with no application appearing in Drugs@FDA.

## The Phase 2 Result, In Context

Phase 2 data published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* showed participants on the highest retatrutide arm achieved mean weight loss of 24.2% at 48 weeks, with some individuals losing more than 30% of body weight [nejm-retatrutide-phase2]. Those results exceeded outcomes observed with any other pharmacological obesity intervention at the time.

Phase 2 obesity results have historically overstated phase 3 by a few percentage points across this drug class, and the phase 2 trial was much smaller. Treating 24.2% as a settled expectation for TRIUMPH is a mistake the diabetes readout should discourage.

### The Triple-Agonist Mechanism

Retatrutide differs from currently approved agents in the number of receptors it engages. [Tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) (Zepbound, Mounjaro) activates GLP-1 and GIP; [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) activates GLP-1 alone. Retatrutide adds glucagon receptor activation [nejm-retatrutide-phase2].

Glucagon receptor agonism raises energy expenditure and acts directly on the liver, which is the mechanistic argument for both the larger weight effect and the interest in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. That same hepatic action is why glucagon-containing agonists are watched closely for glycaemic and hepatic safety signals.

## What It Means

A phase 3 programme of this scale is the last major step before a regulatory submission. Lilly has not published a confirmed filing date; any submission would follow completion of the programme.

The TRANSCEND-T2D-1 publication changes the evidence position in one specific way: retatrutide now has a published, peer-reviewed phase 3 result rather than a phase 2 result and a set of registrations. That moves the compound out of the category where the only numbers available come from conference slides and press releases.

Competitive dynamics between Lilly and Novo Nordisk continue to intensify. Novo's [CagriSema](/peptides/cagrisema) and Lilly's oral [orforglipron](/peptides/orforglipron) both published phase 3 data in the same window, so the next-generation obesity field is now being compared on published trials rather than on projections. The comparison pages for [tirzepatide versus retatrutide](/compare/tirzepatide-vs-retatrutide) and [retatrutide versus semaglutide](/compare/retatrutide-vs-semaglutide) track those matchups.

## What's Next

The obesity readouts remain the decisive event. Until TRIUMPH publishes, the strongest obesity evidence for retatrutide is a phase 2 trial.

Safety monitoring across the programme matters more than usual given the novel third receptor. Phase 2 showed a generally acceptable tolerability profile; the larger phase 3 population provides the statistical power to detect rarer events.

Beyond weight, ongoing work examines cardiovascular outcomes, liver disease and broader metabolic endpoints. Those indications would expand clinical utility, and none of them has published phase 3 evidence yet.
