    answer: >-

Liraglutide is an FDA-approved GLP-1 receptor agonist with extensive human clinical trial data. Its mechanisms are well-characterized through over 15 years of clinical use.

The 31-letter displayed backbone sequence is shorthand: the lysine at position 20 of that sequence (Lys26 in GLP-1 numbering) bears a C16 palmitoyl group through a glutamyl spacer, and position 28 is arginine, not lysine. The [NIH substance record](https://gsrs.ncats.nih.gov/ginas/app/beta/substances/839I73S42A) and [FDA Saxenda label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/206321s024lbl.pdf) describe the modified molecule.

### Current US label scope

The [FDA Saxenda label file](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/206321s024lbl.pdf) for NDA 206321/S-024 includes prescribing information revised February 2026 and Instructions for Use revised June 2026. Its listed recent changes include removal of the suicidal-behavior-and-ideation warning in February 2026 and removal of pregnancy from the contraindications section in May 2025; section 8 still says Saxenda may cause fetal harm and directs discontinuation when pregnancy is recognized. Removal of a warning does not establish that every psychiatric outcome has been ruled out. The label also warns of severe GI reactions, acute kidney injury due to volume depletion, hypoglycemia (including pediatric events), and pulmonary aspiration during anesthesia or deep sedation. [Victoza's US label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5a9ef4ea-c76a-4d34-a604-27c5b505f5a4) separately carries the LEADER-based CV indication for adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease.

### Important Limitations

- The Saxenda and Victoza indications are product-specific; LEADER's CV event result does not confer a CV indication on Saxenda
- Common GI side effects (nausea, vomiting) especially during titration
- Requires daily subcutaneous injection (less convenient than weekly alternatives)
- Boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors (based on rodent studies; human relevance uncertain)
- Contraindicated in personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2
- Weight regain typically occurs after discontinuation
- Less effective for weight loss than newer agents (semaglutide, tirzepatide)
