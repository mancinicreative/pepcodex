## What the evidence can establish

TB-500 is an inconsistently used name. This review focuses on **Ac-LKKTETQ**, the N-acetylated seven-residue fragment identified in analytical studies, rather than full-length thymosin beta-4. An assay that identifies a peptide does not establish its clinical safety. [PMID:22962027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22962027/) [PMID:23084823](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23084823/)

The 2024 fragment study evaluated laboratory human serum, enzyme systems, treated rats and fibroblast assays. It reported no cytotoxicity in its tested assay, and significant wound-healing activity only for the metabolite Ac-LKKTE. **Those results do not establish adverse-event rates, long-term safety, reproductive safety or clinical interactions in people.** Human serum in an experiment is not a clinical trial. [PMID:38382158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38382158/)

## Parent-compound observations are not fragment safety data

A 73-person venous-ulcer study reported an acceptable safety profile for full-length thymosin beta-4 compared with placebo. A 72-person parent-compound dry-eye trial reported no adverse events, while missing both primary efficacy endpoints. In the 18-person parent-compound corneal trial, adverse events occurred in 4/10 participants receiving the parent formulation and 3/8 receiving placebo. One active-group event was considered treatment related; one serious event in that group was classified as unrelated, and no treatment-related serious adverse events were reported. [SEER-1 full text, section 2.4 and Table 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9820614/). Its four-week primary healing comparison was not significant. These are observations from particular formulations, populations and study durations; they cannot establish the safety of Ac-LKKTETQ. [PMID:20536470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20536470/) [PMID:26056426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26056426/) [PMID:36613994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36613994/)

The reviewed evidence does not quantify human fragment risks or establish safe combinations. It also does not justify presenting proposed immune effects or tumor mechanisms as demonstrated clinical adverse effects of this fragment.

## Chemical identity matters

The tested TB500/TB1000 products in an analytical report were not consistently described by earlier labels. That finding is limited to the tested materials; it does not verify every product's composition or contamination status. Appearance and a market name cannot substitute for the chemical evidence in a study. [PMID:36482504](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36482504/)

## Dated regulatory evidence

The **May 15, 2026 FDA briefing** described TB-500 free base and acetate as lacking approved-drug-component status and highlighted inconsistent chemical naming. It is advisory material, not a final compounding rule. [FDA briefing, pages 6–9](https://www.fda.gov/media/193349/download).

## Review scope

This September 5, 2026 review uses selected publications, a June 7–September 5 literature scan and unrestricted identity queries. It did not verify a conducted human Ac-LKKTETQ trial. The registry record NCT07487363 describes itself as fictional/example material and is excluded as proof of human testing. [ClinicalTrials.gov record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07487363).

This is a bounded evidence review, not an exhaustive safety or international regulatory inventory. See the [TB-500 dossier](/peptides/tb-500) for the cited molecular and experimental distinctions. This information does not constitute medical advice.
