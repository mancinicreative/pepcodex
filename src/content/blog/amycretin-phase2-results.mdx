**Update (17 August 2026):** The compound covered in this briefing has been renamed. Both Lancet phase 2 reports published on 30 July 2026 open with the phrase "Zenagamtide (formerly amycretin)" — the molecule now carries the INN zenagamtide, and new literature is appearing under that name [zenagamtide-sc-phase2]. This article keeps the original name in its title because the early-phase papers it describes were published as amycretin. The full evidence-graded record is in the [zenagamtide (amycretin) dossier](/peptides/amycretin).

Novo Nordisk has published positive early-phase results for amycretin, a novel peptide that combines GLP-1 and amylin receptor agonist activity in a single molecule. In the subcutaneous phase 1b/2a study, the highest-dose group lost 24.3% of body weight by week 36, a magnitude that places the compound among the most active investigational obesity agents reported to date.

## What We Know

The phase 1b/2a trial (NCT06064006) allocated 125 adults aged 18-55 with overweight or obesity (BMI 27.0-39.9 kg/m²) to amycretin (n=101) or placebo (n=24). The study used single and multiple ascending doses, with once-weekly subcutaneous maintenance doses ranging from 1.25 mg to 60 mg and treatment lasting up to 36 weeks depending on study part. The primary endpoint was the number of treatment-emergent adverse events from baseline to end of study [amycretin-phase2-data].

Body-weight change was an exploratory outcome and differed by study part [amycretin-phase2-data]:

| Study part | Maintenance dose | Timepoint | Amycretin | Placebo |
|------------|------------------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| B | 60 mg | Week 36 | -24.3% | -1.1% |
| C | 20 mg | Week 36 | -22.0% | +1.9% |
| D | 5 mg | Week 28 | -16.2% | +2.3% |
| E | 1.25 mg | Week 20 | -9.7% | +2.0% |

All comparisons versus placebo were statistically significant.

The mechanism of amycretin differs from existing approved medications. While [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) acts solely on GLP-1 receptors and [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) targets both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, amycretin combines GLP-1 activity with amylin receptor agonism in a single molecule, creating a complementary approach to appetite regulation [amycretin-phase2-data].

### The Amylin Mechanism

Amylin is a peptide hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells that contributes to satiety and slows gastric emptying. Pramlintide, a synthetic amylin analog, has been available for diabetes treatment but requires multiple daily injections and produces modest weight loss when used alone.

The innovation of amycretin lies in uniting amylin and GLP-1 activity in a single molecule optimized for once-weekly dosing. This approach may provide effects beyond what either mechanism achieves independently, though the trial was not designed to test synergy [amycretin-phase2-data].

Amylin acts primarily in the area postrema and other brainstem regions to promote satiety, while GLP-1 works through both peripheral and central mechanisms. The combination potentially provides more comprehensive appetite control than single-mechanism approaches.

## What It Means

The results position amycretin as a serious contender in the increasingly competitive obesity medication landscape. Novo Nordisk, which currently leads the market with semaglutide products, is developing multiple next-generation candidates to maintain its position against competition from Eli Lilly and others.

The 24.3% mean reduction seen at 36 weeks in the highest-dose group is competitive with retatrutide and other advanced candidates, though these were small, early-phase groups with weight change measured only as an exploratory outcome.

For patients, the proliferation of highly effective obesity medications offers hope for matching individual physiology with optimal treatment. Different mechanisms may prove more effective for different patient populations, moving the field toward more personalized approaches.

The gastrointestinal tolerability profile will be critical for amycretin's commercial success. Nausea, vomiting, and other GI side effects are the primary reason patients discontinue GLP-1 medications. Gastrointestinal events — mainly nausea and vomiting — and decreased appetite were the most common adverse events in this trial and were most frequent at the higher doses [amycretin-phase2-data]. Larger trials will provide more definitive safety information.

## What Has Happened Since: Phase 2 in Type 2 Diabetes

Two 36-week phase 2 dose-finding trials in adults with type 2 diabetes — one for each formulation, both run under the single registration NCT06542874 — were published in The Lancet on 30 July 2026, under the compound's new name zenagamtide.

The subcutaneous trial randomised 262 adults with type 2 diabetes to one of six once-weekly maintenance doses (0.4 to 40 mg) or placebo. From a mean baseline HbA1c of 7.8%, estimated HbA1c change at week 36 ranged from −0.9% at 0.4 mg to −1.7% at 40 mg, a difference versus placebo of −1.56% (95% CI −2.05 to −1.07) at the top dose [zenagamtide-sc-phase2].

The oral trial randomised 186 adults to once-daily 6, 25 or 50 mg or placebo. Estimated HbA1c change at week 36 was −0.9%, −1.3% and −1.4% respectively, against baselines of 7.9 to 8.1%. Gastrointestinal adverse events rose with dose, from 26% at 6 mg to 47% at 50 mg, versus 23% on placebo [zenagamtide-oral-phase2].

One caveat is stated in the papers themselves: both trials assessed their primary outcome on the **efficacy estimand only** — on-treatment data without rescue medication — and neither reports a treatment-regimen figure. The results describe what happened in participants who stayed on treatment, not across everyone randomised. Neither trial had body weight as its primary endpoint.

## What's Next

As of 17 August 2026, phase 3 outcome data for zenagamtide are not in the verified registry; the published evidence extends through the two phase 2 type 2 diabetes trials and the earlier phase 1 and phase 1b/2a studies. The phase 3 obesity question — whether the early weight-loss signal holds at scale — remains open.

The competitive dynamics in obesity drug development continue to intensify. Eli Lilly's retatrutide and Novo's own [cagrilintide](/peptides/cagrilintide)-semaglutide combination are progressing through clinical development; the amylin mechanism zenagamtide shares with cagrilintide is examined further in our [REDEFINE-1 amylin analysis](/blog/cagrilintide-redefine-1-amylin-validated).
