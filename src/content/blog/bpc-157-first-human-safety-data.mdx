**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article described a 2025 intravenous
BPC-157 pilot study in healthy volunteers, tied to registry number NCT05765513. That
identifier registers an unrelated study, and no primary publication of such a pilot
could be located. As of 2026-08-17, no primary publication of an IV safety pilot is in
our verified registry, and the claims built on it have been removed. What follows is
the human evidence that can actually be verified.

BPC-157, a synthetic pentadecapeptide derived from a protein sequence in human gastric
juice, has one of the largest preclinical literatures of any research peptide — and one
of the thinnest human records. The full compound record is in the
[BPC-157 dossier](/peptides/bpc-157).

## What the Verified Human Record Contains

A 2026 biopharmaceutical review in *Pharmaceutics* is the most complete published
accounting of BPC-157's development status [bpc-157-review]. Its human-evidence tally is
short: **fewer than 30 human subjects, across three uncontrolled pilot studies**.

"Uncontrolled" is the load-bearing word. Without a comparator arm, a pilot study cannot
separate drug effect from natural recovery or expectation — a fundamental problem for a
compound whose claimed uses centre on healing from injury, where spontaneous improvement
is the norm. These pilots can suggest short-term tolerability; they cannot establish
safety, and they say nothing about efficacy.

The same review documents how little basic pharmacology is settled [bpc-157-review]:
intramuscular bioavailability spans 14% to 51% depending on species, plasma half-life is
under 30 minutes, and no approved formulation exists. Pharmaceutical development is
described as rudimentary.

On the efficacy side, a 2025 systematic review in *HSS Journal* examined BPC-157's
emerging use in orthopaedic sports medicine [hss-systematic-review]. Consistent with the
assessment in our dossier, the only human efficacy data in that literature is a
retrospective, uncontrolled case series in knee pain — self-reported improvement in 7 of
12 patients after intra-articular administration — alongside a Phase 2 programme
(PL-14736) that was halted before completion. No controlled human efficacy trial of
BPC-157 has been completed and published.

## The First Controlled Trial Is Now Registered

One placebo-controlled study is registered: **NCT07437547** (BPC-HAMSTR), a Phase 2
trial of pentadecapeptide BPC 157 versus placebo for acute hamstring muscle strain
repair, with a planned enrolment of 120 participants [bpc157-hamstring-trial]. As of an
August 2026 registry check it is recruiting, and no results have been posted.

This matters because it is the first BPC-157 study with a design capable of answering a
question. A placebo-controlled trial in a defined injury can distinguish drug effect
from natural healing in a way that none of the existing human data can.

## What This Means

- **Human safety is uncharacterised.** Fewer than 30 people have received BPC-157 in
  any published study, none under controlled conditions [bpc-157-review]. Long-term
  administration, repeated administration, and use in people with medical conditions
  have never been studied in humans.
- **Human efficacy is undemonstrated.** The animal literature is extensive but comes
  predominantly from a single research group, and animal results routinely fail to
  translate — a gap discussed in detail in our
  [BPC-157 safety review](/blog/is-bpc-157-safe).
- **The regulatory position reflects the data.** BPC-157 is not approved for any use,
  and the FDA has classified it as ineligible for compounding. Products sold as
  research chemicals are not manufactured or verified to pharmaceutical standards.
- **The evidence could change.** If BPC-HAMSTR completes and publishes, it will be the
  first controlled human data point. Until then, every therapeutic claim for BPC-157
  rests on animal models and uncontrolled observation.
