A registry entry can describe a study without proving that participants were enrolled or received an identified product. That distinction matters for [MOTS-c](/peptides/mots-c), where the cited human biomarker studies and animal experiments do not establish benefits from administering the native peptide to people.

## The MOTS-c record

[NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) describes a Phase 2 study of MOTS-c in adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity. In the record retrieved September 5, 2026:

| Field | What the record says |
|---|---|
| Sponsor | Hudson Biotech |
| Design | Randomized comparison of MOTS-c and placebo |
| Enrollment | Estimated 120 participants |
| Status | Recruiting |
| Last posted update | April 1, 2026 |
| Results | No results posted in the record |

These are statements in the registry, not independent verification of study conduct. The entry calls the design multicenter but lists one hospital. This review has not independently established hospital participation, actual recruitment, administration, or the identity of the study product. It therefore does not treat the entry as demonstrated human exposure or efficacy evidence.

## A specific reason for verification

Another registry entry, [NCT07487363](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07487363), names the same sponsor, Hudson Biotech, and explicitly describes its TB-500 study as fictional. The MOTS-c record does not contain that statement. The shared sponsor context raises a verification concern but does not prove that the MOTS-c entry is fictional.

An estimated enrollment figure or a recruiting label cannot resolve that concern. Until conduct and product identity are independently substantiated, the MOTS-c entry is best described as a registry record with unverified conduct. It cannot support a claim that a first human trial is happening or has produced results.

## Two commonly confused types of record

[NCT07678073](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07678073) compares anesthetic approaches during renal transplantation. Its investigators plan to measure MOTS-c as an outcome biomarker. The experimental treatments are anesthetics, not MOTS-c.

[NCT03998514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) describes a completed Phase 1 study of CB4211. The [sponsor’s source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1522602/000121390018006921/f8k052918ex99-1_cohbar.htm) identifies CB4211 as a MOTS-c analogue. That trial must remain separate from native MOTS-c research; an analogue can differ in pharmacological behavior. Its record contains no posted results in the September 5 retrieval, which does not rule out separately reported sponsor findings.

## What the published research does establish

The founding 2015 paper described metabolic effects in cells and mice. A 2021 paper reported improved physical performance in mice alongside human measurements of endogenous MOTS-c after exercise. Human measurements in that study were not outcomes of administering MOTS-c. [PMID:25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) [PMID:33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/)

An aging study found opposite directions in different compartments: plasma MOTS-c decreased with age, while skeletal-muscle expression increased. A narrative about replacing a universally declining factor would omit this distinction. [PMID:32182209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32182209/)

Experimental findings are also not uniformly beneficial. A June 2026 study found metabolic-pathway activation alongside increased senescence-related signals and impaired reparative function in donor-cell and mouse experiments. This is a reason to evaluate functional outcomes, rather than assume that activating AMPK must improve health. It is not a demonstrated patient harm rate. [PMID:42324588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324588/)

## Reading future evidence

An informative human treatment report would need to establish what product was administered, who received it, how the comparison was conducted, and what outcomes and adverse events were observed. A biomarker association, an analogue experiment, or a registry listing cannot substitute for those details.
