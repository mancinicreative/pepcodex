On 18 December 2025, Novo Nordisk announced the submission of a New Drug Application (NDA) to the FDA for once-weekly CagriSema (cagrilintide 2.4 mg / semaglutide 2.4 mg) for chronic weight management in adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity. If approved, CagriSema would be the first injectable GLP-1 receptor agonist and amylin analogue combination [novo-cagrisema-nda].

## What Is CagriSema?

CagriSema combines two peptide mechanisms in a single weekly injection:

**Semaglutide 2.4mg:** The GLP-1 receptor agonist already approved as Wegovy for obesity, providing appetite suppression through central and peripheral effects.

**Cagrilintide 2.4mg:** A long-acting amylin analog that complements GLP-1 effects through different but synergistic pathways [amylin-obesity-review].

### The Amylin Mechanism

Amylin is a hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells. It contributes to satiety and glucose regulation through:

- **Gastric emptying:** Slows stomach emptying, promoting fullness
- **Glucagon suppression:** Reduces post-meal glucagon secretion
- **Central satiety:** Acts on brain satiety centers
- **Different pathway than GLP-1:** Complementary rather than redundant effects

The native amylin peptide (pramlintide, brand name Symlin) is approved for diabetes but requires three-times-daily injection. Cagrilintide is acylated for once-weekly dosing.

## The REDEFINE Phase 3 Program

### REDEFINE 1: Primary Weight Loss Trial

The pivotal REDEFINE 1 trial (NCT05567796) randomised 3,417 adults without diabetes who had obesity (BMI ≥30) or overweight (BMI ≥27) with at least one weight-related complication:

- **Duration:** 68 weeks
- **Randomization:** 21:3:3:7 to CagriSema (n=2,108), semaglutide 2.4 mg (n=302), cagrilintide 2.4 mg (n=302), or placebo (n=705), all with lifestyle intervention
- **Primary endpoints:** Percent weight change and proportion achieving ≥5% weight loss

**Results (treatment-policy estimand):**
| Arm | Mean weight change at week 68 |
|-----|-------------------------------|
| CagriSema | -20.4% |
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg | -14.9% |
| Cagrilintide 2.4 mg | -11.5% |
| Placebo | -3.0% |

The CagriSema-versus-placebo difference was -17.3 percentage points (95% CI -18.1 to -16.6; P&lt;0.001), and significantly higher proportions of the CagriSema group reached every weight-loss threshold tested [redefine-trials-overview]. Under the trial-product estimand, which assumes full adherence, mean weight reduction with CagriSema was 22.7%, with 40.4% of participants losing at least 25% of body weight.

### REDEFINE 2: Type 2 Diabetes Population

The REDEFINE 2 trial (NCT05394519) evaluated CagriSema in adults with overweight or obesity and type 2 diabetes:

- **Enrollment:** 1,206 randomised, 3:1 to CagriSema (n=904) or placebo (n=302)
- **Duration:** 68 weeks
- **Population:** BMI ≥27 kg/m², HbA1c 7-10%

**Results:**
| Outcome | CagriSema | Placebo |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Mean weight change (treatment policy) | -13.7% | -3.4% |
| Mean weight change (trial product) | -15.7% | -3.1% |
| Reached HbA1c ≤6.5% | 73.5% | 15.9% |

REDEFINE 2 compared CagriSema against placebo only — it had no semaglutide comparator arm. Weight loss was smaller than in REDEFINE 1, consistent with the general pattern that people with type 2 diabetes lose less weight on incretin-based therapy.

### Additional REDEFINE Trials

Other trials in the programme include:

- **REDEFINE 3:** An event-driven cardiovascular outcomes trial of CagriSema versus placebo in roughly 7,000 adults with established cardiovascular disease, with or without type 2 diabetes
- **REDEFINE 4:** An 84-week open-label head-to-head trial of CagriSema versus tirzepatide 15 mg in 809 adults with obesity

## Efficacy Analysis

### Where CagriSema Sits Among Obesity Therapies

CagriSema's REDEFINE 1 result places it at the top of the injectable field, but the head-to-head data complicate the "best-in-class" framing. In REDEFINE 4, reported in February 2026, CagriSema produced about 23% weight loss at 84 weeks but **did not** meet its primary endpoint of non-inferiority to tirzepatide 15 mg. Cross-trial comparisons with other agents should be treated with caution: trial populations, durations, estimands and adherence differ.

### Responder Analysis

Under the trial-product estimand in REDEFINE 1, 40.4% of CagriSema participants lost at least 25% of body weight — a magnitude previously associated mainly with bariatric surgery. Responder rates for CagriSema exceeded placebo at every threshold assessed [redefine-trials-overview].

### Combination Versus Components

REDEFINE 1 is unusual in carrying both monotherapy arms, which allows a direct read on what the combination adds:

- Cagrilintide 2.4 mg alone: -11.5%
- Semaglutide 2.4 mg alone: -14.9%
- CagriSema: -20.4%

The combination beat each component, though it did not reach the sum of the two — consistent with partially overlapping, rather than fully independent, mechanisms.

## Safety Profile

### Gastrointestinal Events

As expected with incretin-based therapies, GI events were common. In REDEFINE 1 they occurred in 79.6% of the CagriSema group versus 39.9% on placebo, and in REDEFINE 2 in 72.5% versus 34.4%. Investigators described them as mainly transient and mild-to-moderate in severity, with nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation and abdominal pain the most frequent [redefine-trials-overview].

## Regulatory Strategy

### Review Timeline

The NDA was submitted on 18 December 2025 on the basis of REDEFINE 1 [novo-cagrisema-nda]. A first FDA decision is expected in late 2026. CagriSema is not approved by the FDA or any other regulator at the time of writing.

### Label Expectations

The anticipated label includes:

- **Indication:** Chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related comorbidity
- **Dosing:** Once-weekly subcutaneous injection
- **Titration:** 4-week dose escalation schedule
- **Contraindications:** Similar to semaglutide (MTC, MEN2)

### Post-Marketing Commitments

Expected requirements include:

- Cardiovascular outcomes trial
- Long-term safety registry
- Pediatric study plan
- Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) possible

## Market Implications

### Competitive Landscape

CagriSema enters a competitive market:

**Current Market Leaders:**
- Wegovy (semaglutide): Novo Nordisk
- Zepbound (tirzepatide): Eli Lilly

**CagriSema Positioning:**
- Top-tier efficacy, but non-inferiority to tirzepatide was not demonstrated in REDEFINE 4
- Same company as Wegovy (cannibalization concern)
- Higher price point likely
- Differentiated for highest-need patients

### Pricing Considerations

Expected pricing factors:

- Premium over Wegovy likely
- Negotiation with PBMs critical
- International reference pricing considerations
- Value-based contracting possible

### Retatrutide Competition

Eli Lilly's retatrutide (triple agonist) showed roughly 24% weight loss in phase 2, in the same range as CagriSema. The REDEFINE 4 head-to-head against tirzepatide has since read out and did not establish non-inferiority, which sharpens rather than settles the competitive question.

## Patient Selection

### Ideal Candidates

CagriSema may be most appropriate for:

- Patients not achieving adequate response to semaglutide alone
- Those with significant weight to lose (BMI >40)
- Patients comfortable with weekly injection and GI side effects
- Those willing to pay premium pricing

### Transitioning Patients

Questions remain about:

- Switching from Wegovy: Is CagriSema meaningfully better?
- Switching from Zepbound: Which patients should transition?
- Cost-effectiveness: Does additional weight loss justify premium?

## What This Means

The CagriSema NDA filing represents a significant milestone in obesity pharmacotherapy. With 20.4% mean weight loss in REDEFINE 1 — and 40.4% of adherent participants losing at least a quarter of their body weight — CagriSema could offer results previously associated mainly with bariatric surgery, in a once-weekly injection.

For patients struggling with obesity, this filing brings a further treatment option closer. For the field, REDEFINE 1's inclusion of both monotherapy arms is the more interesting result: it shows the combination outperforms either peptide alone, while falling short of a simple additive effect.

The FDA decision, expected in late 2026, will determine whether this combination reaches patients.
