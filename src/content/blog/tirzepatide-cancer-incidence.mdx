**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article described a retrospective
cohort of 125,000 patients (42,000 on tirzepatide vs 83,000 matched controls),
2.4 years of follow-up, and an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.56 (95% CI 0.44–0.71)
for obesity-related cancers, plus a per-cancer HR table. Those numbers were not
tied to a resolving study. Body citations `[tirzepatide-cancer-study]`,
`[obesity-cancer-link]`, and `[glp1-cancer-mechanisms]` were not in `sources[]`.
The identifier that *was* stored (`pmid-38976276` / PMID 40900093) resolves to a
different paper: gastrointestinal and hepatobiliary safety of GLP-1 receptor
agonists in type 2 diabetes, not tirzepatide-specific cancer incidence
[pmid-40900093]. Two further frontmatter sources were already flagged
`verified: false` after resolving to different documents; they have been removed
rather than re-attached to new titles.

Obesity is associated with several cancers, and GLP-1-based therapies produce
substantial weight loss. That biological sketch is not the same thing as a verified
tirzepatide cancer-incidence result. The compound record is in the
[tirzepatide dossier](/peptides/tirzepatide). For the published obesity
head-to-head against semaglutide 2.4 mg, see
[SURMOUNT-5](/blog/surmount-5-tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide) and the
[tirzepatide vs semaglutide comparison](/compare/tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide).

## What PMID 40900093 Actually Is

Niu et al., *American Journal of Gastroenterology* (epub 2025; print 2026), is
titled "Gastrointestinal and Hepatobiliary Safety of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1
Receptor Agonists in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes" [pmid-40900093]. It is a
class-level GI and hepatobiliary safety paper in type 2 diabetes. It is **not**:

- A tirzepatide-only cancer-incidence cohort
- A 125,000-patient matched analysis of 13 obesity-related cancers
- A source for HR 0.56, or for colorectal / breast / endometrial / pancreatic /
  liver cancer sub-hazard ratios

Citing it under a fabricated cancer-prevention headline was a real-id-wrong-paper
error: the number resolved, the claim did not.

## What This Page Does Not Claim

As of 2026-09-01, this URL does **not** assert that tirzepatide reduces cancer
incidence. Weight loss and metabolic change are plausible reasons researchers
*study* cancer outcomes on incretin drugs. Plausibility is not a substitute for a
drug-matched, identifier-backed analysis.

Dedicated cancer-prevention trials of tirzepatide are not established by the
single verified source on this page. Cardiovascular outcomes programmes may
capture cancer events as safety endpoints; that is not the same as a published
cancer-incidence effect size.

## How to Read GLP-1 Cancer Headlines

Class safety papers, obesity epidemiology, and compound-specific observational
cohorts are three different objects. Mixing them produces a confident-looking
article that fails a PubMed check. If a later observational or trial analysis of
tirzepatide and incident cancer is published with a PMID that drug-matches, it
can be added here. Until then, evidence level stays **low** and the headline stays
a correction, not a benefit claim.
