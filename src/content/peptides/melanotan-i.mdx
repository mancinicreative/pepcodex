The name Melanotan-1 is commonly associated with afamelanotide, a synthetic
alpha-MSH analogue. The clinical and regulatory evidence here concerns the
specified **Scenesse implant** or research-grade afamelanotide used in named
studies. A preparation marketed under the Melanotan-1 name is not thereby the
regulated medicine.

## Adult EPP evidence

One 2015 publication reports **two distinct randomized trials** of the
afamelanotide implant in erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP): 94 participants
in the US and 74 in Europe. The US trial found a median 69.4 pain-free hours
of direct sunlight over 180 days versus 40.8 with placebo. The European trial
found 6.0 versus 0.8 hours over 270 days, with fewer phototoxic reactions.
These endpoints concern light exposure in EPP, not cosmetic tanning.
[Langendonk et al., PMID
26132941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26132941/)

A German post-authorisation cohort reports outcomes among 200 treated patients
but includes no untreated comparison group. A separate Austrian cohort of 20
treated patients reported improved patient-reported light tolerance and
quality of life compared with baseline. Selection, follow-up, and measurement
differences limit causal interpretation of both cohorts; they do not add
another randomized trial. [German cohort, PMID
40082741](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11906902/) · [Austrian
cohort, PMID 41793078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41793078/)

## Approval is product- and indication-specific

The FDA lists Scenesse marketing approval on 8 October 2019 to increase
pain-free light exposure in adults with a history of phototoxic reactions from
EPP. Its label describes an afamelanotide implant administered by a trained
healthcare professional. The EMA describes its authorisation as prevention of
phototoxicity in adults with EPP. Neither record approves a cosmetic tanning
product or establishes that other formulations are equivalent. [FDA orphan
product record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=263208) ·
[current US label, DailyMed SPL v11](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94f53286-11dd-7fbb-e053-2a95a90a7c48&version=11) ·
[EMA product record](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/scenesse)

The current US Scenesse implant label warns of serious hypersensitivity,
including anaphylaxis, and recommends twice-yearly full-body skin examinations
to monitor pre-existing and new pigmentary lesions because existing nevi and
freckles may darken. These warnings concern the regulated implant used for
the adult EPP indication; they do not establish the safety or identity of
products sold as Melanotan-1. [Current US Scenesse label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94f53286-11dd-7fbb-e053-2a95a90a7c48&version=11)

## Other research and limits

A small randomized vitiligo trial compared afamelanotide implant plus
narrowband UV-B with narrowband UV-B alone in 55 adults with selected skin
phototypes. Repigmentation outcomes favored the combination in that study,
but this does not establish an approved vitiligo indication or a benefit from
afamelanotide alone. [Lim et al., PMID
25230094](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25230094/)

Melanotan-II is a different analogue; its evidence cannot be transferred to
afamelanotide. A seller's name or claim cannot establish identity,
formulation, sterility, or equivalence to Scenesse. This dossier does not
provide dosing, purchasing, or self-treatment guidance and is not medical
advice.
