  - slug: weight-loss
    name: Weight Loss
    researchSummary: >-
      Semaglutide is FDA-approved for chronic weight management (as Wegovy) and
      has demonstrated substantial efficacy in multiple Phase 3 trials. The STEP
      1 trial showed 14.9% mean weight loss versus 2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks
      (N=1,961).

      The mechanism involves GLP-1 receptor activation in appetite-regulating
      brain regions, reducing hunger and increasing satiety. Higher dose studies
      (STEP UP, 7.2mg) reported 20.7% mean weight loss under its efficacy
      estimand in adults with obesity; that estimate should not be generalized
      across formulations, populations, or estimands.

      Semaglutide represents a significant advance in pharmacological obesity
      treatment. In the STEP 1 extension subset, participants regained about
      two-thirds of their prior weight loss during one year after semaglutide
      and structured lifestyle intervention stopped. STEP 4 was a separate
      randomized withdrawal trial.

      Semaglutide received FDA approval for chronic obesity management in 2021
      based on the comprehensive STEP clinical trial program. The drug achieved
      substantial weight reduction across diverse populations including those
      with and without type 2 diabetes.

      meaningful weight loss (5% or more), with 32% achieving 20% or greater
      reduction. The STEP-HFpEF trial showed benefits in obese patients with
      heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.

      cardiovascular-event composite among adults with overweight or obesity,
      established cardiovascular disease, and no diabetes. Wegovy injection
      subsequently gained a US cardiovascular risk-reduction indication for
      adults meeting its labeled population criteria.

    name: Type 2 Diabetes
    researchSummary: >-
      Semaglutide was first FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes in 2017 (as
      Ozempic). In placebo-controlled SUSTAIN 1, HbA1c fell by 1.45 and 1.55
      percentage points in the two semaglutide groups at 30 weeks, versus 0.02
      points with placebo (PMID:28110911). Those values are trial-specific.

      The drug improves glycemic control through multiple mechanisms:
      glucose-dependent insulin secretion, glucagon suppression, and delayed
      gastric emptying. SUSTAIN-6 showed 26% reduction in major adverse
      cardiovascular events, establishing cardiovascular benefit.

      Both injectable (Ozempic, once weekly) and oral (Rybelsus, daily)
      formulations are available for diabetes. The SOUL trial (2025) confirmed
      oral semaglutide also reduces cardiovascular events by 14% in high-risk
      type 2 diabetes patients.

  - slug: appetite-control
    name: Appetite Control
    researchSummary: >-
      Reduced energy intake is one measured contributor to semaglutide-related
      weight loss. Specific POMC/CART and NPY/AgRP circuit effects are supported
      mainly by preclinical GLP-1 receptor research and should not be presented
      as a demonstrated human semaglutide mechanism.

      In one 20-week randomized mechanistic trial of 72 adults with obesity,
      ad libitum lunch intake was 35% lower with 2.4 mg injection than placebo
      (PMID:33269530). This meal-test result is not an estimate of daily intake
      across all patients. Participants also reported less hunger and greater
      fullness.

      should not be treated as the sole explanation for sustained weight loss.

      In the STEP 1 extension, weight regained after semaglutide and structured
      lifestyle intervention stopped. That follow-up does not isolate a single
      appetite mechanism or prove a permanent change in hunger signaling.

faqs:
  - question: What is semaglutide?
    answer: >-
      Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist in several FDA-approved products.
      Indications differ: Ozempic injection and Rybelsus/Ozempic tablets address
      type 2 diabetes; Wegovy injection and tablets have weight and selected CV
      risk indications, while only Wegovy injection has a MASH indication.
  - question: Is semaglutide FDA approved?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Ozempic and Rybelsus are diabetes products; Wegovy injection and
      tablets have adult weight and CV risk indications. Wegovy injection also
      has pediatric obesity and accelerated adult MASH indications. Approval
      does not extend to compounded versions.
  - question: How effective is semaglutide for weight loss?
    answer: >-
      In the STEP 1 trial, semaglutide achieved 14.9% mean weight loss versus
      2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks. Higher doses (7.2mg) achieved 20.7% weight
      loss in STEP UP.
  - question: What are common semaglutide side effects?
    answer: >-
      The most common side effects are gastrointestinal: nausea (30-45% during
      titration), vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation. These typically improve
      with continued use.
  - question: Does weight return after stopping semaglutide?
    answer: >-
      In the STEP 1 extension subset, participants regained about two-thirds
      of their prior weight loss one year after both treatment and structured
      lifestyle intervention stopped. STEP 4 was a separate randomized
      withdrawal trial.

    FDA-approved products have distinct indications (current Wegovy FDA label
    checked 2026-09-28).
    Ozempic injection (NDA 209637, approved 5 Dec 2017): type 2 diabetes,
    major adverse cardiovascular event reduction in T2D with established
    cardiovascular disease, and reduction of sustained eGFR decline, end-stage
    kidney disease and CV death in T2D with chronic kidney disease. Rybelsus
    and Ozempic tablets (NDA 213051): type 2 diabetes and MACE reduction in
    adults with T2D at high risk. Wegovy injection (NDA 215256) is labeled for
    weight reduction in adults and adolescents aged 12+, MACE reduction in
    adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity,
    and accelerated approval for adults with noncirrhotic MASH and F2-F3
    fibrosis. Wegovy tablets (NDA 218316) are labeled for adult weight
    reduction and CV risk reduction in adults with established CV disease and
    overweight or obesity; they are not labeled for pediatric use or MASH.
    Wegovy HD 7.2 mg injection is a labeled option for additional adult weight
    reduction, not for pediatric use, CV risk reduction, or MASH. Compounded
    semaglutide is not an FDA-approved product.

Semaglutide is a selective GLP-1 receptor agonist with extensive Phase 3 clinical trial data supporting its mechanisms in humans.

The displayed 31-residue backbone is a sequence shorthand, not the complete chemical structure: residue 2 is aminoisobutyric acid (Aib), residue 20 is the lysine acylated through a linker with a C18 fatty diacid, and residue 28 is arginine. NIH's [substance record](https://gsrs.ncats.nih.gov/ginas/app/beta/substances/53AXN4NNHX) and the [FDA Wegovy label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031,218316s00lbl.pdf) identify these structural features.

**Key Research:** STEP 1 randomized 1,961 adults with overweight or obesity to injectable semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo and reported 14.9% versus 2.4% mean weight reduction at 68 weeks ([PMID:33567185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185/)). A separate 72-person mechanistic study measured lower ad libitum lunch intake ([PMID:33269530](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33269530/)); it was not an outcomes trial.

### 2025-2026 Developments

**Oral Wegovy and Wegovy HD:** The current [FDA Wegovy label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031,218316s00lbl.pdf) distinguishes 25 mg oral tablets, labeled for adult weight reduction and for major cardiovascular event reduction in adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity, from the injection. Wegovy HD 7.2 mg injection is labeled for additional weight reduction in selected adults. The tablet and 7.2 mg injection have not established safety and effectiveness for pediatric use or MASH; 7.2 mg is not the labeled cardiovascular or MASH maintenance dose. In STEP UP, 7.2 mg produced 20.7% mean weight loss under the efficacy estimand; the [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031,218316s00lbl.pdf) also reports dysesthesia in 22% at 7.2 mg, 6% at 2.4 mg, and 0.3% with placebo across its higher-dose trials. These are indication- and dose-specific results.

**MASH Indication:** Wegovy **injection** received US accelerated approval for adults with noncirrhotic MASH and F2-F3 fibrosis on the basis of histologic MASH and fibrosis endpoints. The [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031,218316s00lbl.pdf) says continued approval may depend on confirmatory clinical benefit; oral Wegovy has no MASH indication.

**Acute kidney injury:** The [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031,218316s00lbl.pdf) warns of acute kidney injury due to volume depletion, including with persistent GI adverse reactions. A FAERS disproportionality signal ([PMID:41227073](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41227073/)) cannot estimate incidence or establish causation.

**Anti-doping status:** WADA's [2026 Monitoring Program](https://www.wada-ama.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/2026_list_monitoring_program_en_final_clean_september_2025.pdf) lists markers of semaglutide and tirzepatide in and out of competition. Monitoring is expressly for substances outside the Prohibited List and is not a prohibition.

**Head-to-head against a combination product (REIMAGINE 2).** REIMAGINE 2, published on 7 June 2026, put semaglutide 2.4 mg and 1.0 mg directly against cagrilintide-semaglutide and against cagrilintide alone in 2,713 adults with type 2 diabetes across 30 countries over 68 weeks ([PMID:42251859](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42251859/), NCT06065540). On the efficacy estimand, which the paper names as primary, HbA1c fell 1.91 percentage points on the combination against 1.75 on semaglutide 2.4 mg. The difference of 0.16 percentage points (95% CI 0.05 to 0.27) reached statistical significance. Read from semaglutide's side, that is the size of the gap a second mechanism opened over semaglutide alone in this population, and it came with more adverse events: 86.9% versus 81.2%.

**Indirect comparison with orforglipron.** A population-adjusted indirect treatment comparison built oral semaglutide 25 mg against orforglipron 36 mg using individual patient data from OASIS 4 and aggregate data from ATTAIN-1 ([PMID:42225305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42225305/)). It reported greater weight loss with oral semaglutide by 3.2 percentage points on the treatment-regimen estimand (95% CI -5.9 to -0.4) and 3.0 points on the efficacy estimand (95% CI -5.8 to -0.3), and higher discontinuation with orforglipron. Two caveats belong beside that result: the analysis is indirect, comparing arms from separate trials rather than randomised head-to-head, and it was funded and authored by the manufacturer of the winning compound.

**STEP 12 — semaglutide 2.4 mg in Chinese adults.** STEP 12 was a phase 3b trial at 19 sites across mainland China and Taiwan, published in Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology on 10 August 2026 ([PMID:42575111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42575111/), NCT06041217). It enrolled 242 adults at locally defined BMI thresholds — 24 to under 28 with a weight-related comorbidity, or 28 to under 30 — randomised 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 44 weeks. Body weight fell 12.1% versus 2.2%, an estimated treatment difference of 9.9 percentage points (95% CI -11.8 to -8.0), and 80.5% versus 24.4% reached at least 5% reduction. The BMI entry thresholds are lower than in the Western STEP trials, which is the point of the trial and also the limit on transferring its numbers.

**OASIS 2 — oral semaglutide 50 mg in an East Asian population.** OASIS 2 was a 68-week phase 3a trial in Japan and South Korea, published in JAMA Internal Medicine in October 2025 ([PMID:40758358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40758358/), NCT05132088). Of 201 participants, about a quarter had type 2 diabetes. Body weight fell 14.3% versus 1.3% on placebo, an estimated treatment difference of 13.07 percentage points (95% CI -15.61 to -10.52), and 84.3% versus 17.2% reached at least 5% reduction. Gastrointestinal adverse events were reported by 63.4% of the semaglutide group.

**REALYSE — registry results only.** REALYSE (NCT05035082, phase 4, n=1,018) posted results to ClinicalTrials.gov on 10 August 2026. A dateless PubMed search on 17 August 2026 for both the registration number and the trial name returned no indexed publication, so no figures are quoted from it here.

### Important Limitations

- The favourable indirect comparison against orforglipron was authored and funded by semaglutide's manufacturer, and indirect comparisons cannot substitute for a head-to-head trial
- **Common GI side effects:** Nausea (30-45%), vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation, especially during dose titration
- **Boxed warning:** Thyroid C-cell tumors observed in rodents; contraindicated in personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2
- **Gallbladder disease:** A labeled warning; trial incidence varies by product, dose, population, and event definition.
- **Pancreatitis:** A labeled warning; the FDA label directs discontinuation if suspected. Trial rates vary by product and population.
- **Pregnancy:** Wegovy is not contraindicated under label section 4 for pregnancy, but the label's instructions differ by indication: discontinue when pregnancy is recognized for weight or CV indications; for MASH, use only if the potential benefit justifies fetal risk. Its preconception language also depends on indication.
- **Weight regain:** About two-thirds of prior loss returned within one year in the STEP 1 extension subset after drug and structured lifestyle intervention stopped ([PMID:35441470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35441470/)); this estimate is not from STEP 4.
- **Body composition:** Trial lean-mass changes depend on the measurement and population; a universal percentage of weight lost as muscle is not established here.
