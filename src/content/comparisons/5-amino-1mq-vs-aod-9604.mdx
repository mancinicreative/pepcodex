## What is being compared?

5-Amino-1MQ is a small-molecule inhibitor of nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) investigated in metabolic research. AOD-9604 is a modified fragment of human growth hormone; FDA describes it as hGH residues 177–191 with an added N-terminal tyrosine. The compounds are chemically and biologically distinct. No direct trial in the evidence reviewed here compares their effects on human body weight. [Neelakantan et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29155147/) · [FDA AOD-9604 briefing, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/183584/download)

## 5-Amino-1MQ: preclinical metabolic findings

Neelakantan and colleagues identified 5-Amino-1MQ as an NNMT inhibitor and reported effects in cultured adipocytes. In diet-induced obese mice, treatment with a potent NNMT inhibitor reduced body weight and white adipose mass in that experimental setting. These cell and mouse observations do not supply a human efficacy or safety estimate. NNMT target biology cannot by itself establish a weight-loss treatment. [Neelakantan et al., 2018, PMID 29155147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29155147/)

## AOD-9604: the human obesity record

FDA's 2024 advisory briefing summarizes the sponsor's randomized, placebo-controlled OPTIONS **phase 2b** trial. It reports 536 adults enrolled and 502 randomized, with no statistically significant weight-loss difference versus placebo at the 12-week primary endpoint or at 24 weeks. FDA says the detailed study design and preliminary efficacy results do not appear to have been published in medical literature; this comparison therefore does not assign a precise percentage loss or claim an independent efficacy replication. [FDA AOD-9604 briefing, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/183584/download)

A separate 2013 paper pooled safety observations from six sponsor-funded human studies, including OPTIONS. That publication does **not** change the negative phase 2b efficacy finding or establish long-term safety for a marketed formulation. Preclinical AOD-9604 lipid-metabolism findings likewise cannot substitute for a positive human weight-loss result. [Stier et al., 2013](https://www.jofem.org/index.php/jofem/article/view/157/194) · [Heffernan et al., 2001, PMID 11713213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11713213/)

## What the evidence does not resolve

The two evidence sets cannot be converted into a comparative percentage, safety ranking, or treatment recommendation. The cited 5-Amino-1MQ experiment is preclinical. The AOD-9604 human study has a negative placebo-controlled efficacy result, while its detailed efficacy report is not published in the medical literature described by FDA. Neither result is a direct comparison with the other compound or with an approved obesity medicine. [Neelakantan et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29155147/) · [FDA AOD-9604 briefing, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/183584/download)

## Regulatory context

The [June 2026 Australian Poisons Standard](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00633/asmade/2026-05-28/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) names AOD-9604 in Schedule 4 and Appendix D clause 5. Those are substance-scheduling entries. They do not establish that an AOD-9604 product is included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG), available over the counter, or approved for weight loss. [TGA guidance on therapeutic peptide products](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medicines/monitoring-and-compliance/how-we-regulate-therapeutic-peptide-products) explains that product registration or listing is a separate question. This comparison does not determine whether any particular product has an ARTG entry.

FDA's review of AOD-9604 for a compounding advisory process is also not an approval of a weight-loss medicine. This page does not assess a product-specific regulatory status for 5-Amino-1MQ. [FDA AOD-9604 briefing, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/183584/download)

## Reading the result

5-Amino-1MQ has mechanistic and animal findings but the cited primary experiment has no human weight-loss endpoint. AOD-9604 has human obesity data, including a larger phase 2b trial that did not demonstrate significant weight loss against placebo. The distinction is about what each source actually measured; it is not a ranking of which compound is more effective or safer.
