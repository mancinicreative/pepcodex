Those four source entries and their body keys have been removed. A commercial launch, a list price, and a blended 15–17% figure are not asserted here.

What remains is the published OASIS 1 record for once-daily oral semaglutide 50 mg, plus the later OASIS 2 East Asian trial and the SNAC formulation review. The 25 mg FDA story is covered separately on this site; this page does not repeat that post's percentages.

## What We Know

OASIS 1 is a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of oral semaglutide 50 mg once per day versus matching placebo, plus lifestyle intervention, for 68 weeks in adults with overweight or obesity and without type 2 diabetes [pmid-37385278]. The registry record is [NCT05035095](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05035095): completed, phase 3, obesity/overweight, intervention named oral semaglutide, enrolment 667 [nct05035095].

The paper randomised 667 participants (334 oral semaglutide 50 mg, 333 placebo) at 50 outpatient clinics in nine countries. Coprimary endpoints were percentage change in bodyweight and the share of participants who reached at least 5% bodyweight reduction at week 68, **assessed regardless of treatment discontinuation or use of other bodyweight-lowering therapies** — an intention-to-treat, treatment-policy analysis [pmid-37385278].

On that estimand, estimated mean bodyweight change from baseline to week 68 was **−15.1%** (SE 0.5) with oral semaglutide 50 mg versus **−2.4%** (SE 0.5) with placebo (estimated treatment difference −12.7 percentage points, 95% CI −14.2 to −11.3; p&lt;0.0001) [pmid-37385278]. More participants reached at least 5% bodyweight reduction with oral semaglutide than with placebo (85% vs 26%) [pmid-37385278].

Adverse events were reported in 92% of the oral-semaglutide arm and 86% of the placebo arm. Gastrointestinal adverse events (mostly mild to moderate) occurred in 80% versus 46% [pmid-37385278].

The OASIS 1 abstract fetched for this revision does not report a separate trial-product / efficacy-estimand mean. This page therefore does not quote a higher “if everyone stayed on treatment” percentage.

OASIS 2 later tested the same 50 mg oral dose in 201 East Asian adults with overweight or obesity, with or without type 2 diabetes (68 weeks). Mean percentage bodyweight change was −14.3% with oral semaglutide versus −1.3% with placebo (estimated treatment difference −13.07 percentage points, 95% CI −15.61 to −10.52; p&lt;0.001) [pmid-40758358]. That is a second phase 3 randomised comparison, not a substitute for OASIS 1's primary figure.

### How the oral formulation was developed

Oral semaglutide was developed by co-formulating the peptide with SNAC (sodium N-(8-(2-hydroxybenzoyl)amino)caprylate), a transcellular permeation enhancer, to produce the first orally administered GLP-1 receptor agonist [pmid-35838946]. The review reports that stable steady-state concentrations could be achieved with once-daily dosing because of the peptide's long half-life, and that upper gastrointestinal disease and renal or hepatic impairment did not change the pharmacokinetic profile in the analyses it summarises [pmid-35838946]. This page does not turn that mechanism into administration instructions.

## What We Don't Know

The OASIS 1 and OASIS 2 abstracts fetched on 2026-09-01 do not include a head-to-head of 50 mg oral semaglutide versus 2.4 mg once-weekly injectable semaglutide in the same trial population. Cross-trial likeness is not a published comparison.

Those abstracts also do not report real-world adherence after the 68-week double-blind period. Trial follow-up is not the same as typical use.

OASIS 1 excluded people with type 2 diabetes; OASIS 2 enrolled a planned minority with type 2 diabetes. Neither paper is a complete picture of everyone who might later be considered for an oral GLP-1.

## How Strong Is the Evidence?

The remaining claims rest on two completed phase 3 randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials with pre-specified coprimary weight endpoints [pmid-37385278] [pmid-40758358] [nct05035095]. That is high-quality evidence for **what those trials measured** at 68 weeks under a treatment-policy estimand. It is not evidence for a launch, a price, or an unfetched efficacy-estimand headline.

Gastrointestinal adverse events were common in both OASIS 1 and OASIS 2. That is part of the published safety record, not a reason to treat the mean weight change as costless.

The SNAC paper is a formulation review in type 2 diabetes, not an obesity outcomes trial [pmid-35838946]. It supports the delivery mechanism only.
