      This is teriparatide's approved territory and the evidence is unusually
      direct: two randomized trials with radiographic fracture as the PRIMARY
      endpoint, not bone density. The 2001 pivotal trial in 1,637 postmenopausal
      women reported new vertebral fractures in 14% of the placebo group versus
      5% on the strength that was later approved, with nonvertebral fragility

      osteoporosis drugs with incident fracture as the primary outcome, and
      teriparatide beat risedronate on new vertebral fractures (5.4% versus
      12.0%) and on clinical fractures (4.8% versus 9.8%) over 24 months, while
      the nonvertebral difference did not reach significance. Independent
      network meta-analyses reach the same place from a different direction: the
      BMJ analysis of 69 trials found bone anabolic agents more effective than
      oral bisphosphonates for clinical and vertebral fractures irrespective of
      baseline risk, and the American College of Physicians living review graded
      the vertebral and clinical fracture reduction as moderate to high certainty
      while noting that both PTH1 receptor agonists increased withdrawals due to
      adverse events. Registered trials underpinning the label include
      NCT01709110 (VERO) and NCT00051558, the 428-patient glucocorticoid-induced

limitations:
  - >-
    The fracture-healing use case has a null trial behind it. Two phase 3 trials
    designed to support a femoral neck fracture-healing indication were pooled
    by prespecified agreement after both stopped early, and found 88% versus 84%
    revision-free survival at 12 months (P=0.743) — no difference in the primary
    endpoint, in radiographic healing, or in pain. The authors called the result
    exploratory because the sample was small, which is a limitation on the
    evidence, not a positive finding.
  - >-
    The 2026 osteogenesis imperfecta trial missed its primary endpoint despite
    raising bone density. Among 349 evaluable adults, 36.9% of the teriparatide
    plus zoledronic acid group had incident fractures versus 36.4% on standard
    care (hazard ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.38), while lumbar spine and total
    hip bone mineral density rose significantly more. The authors read this as
    evidence that bone quality, not density, drives fracture in that disease —
    and it is a direct demonstration that a density gain need not become a
    fracture reduction.
  - >-
    VERO's nonvertebral result did not reach significance. The headline is the
    vertebral and clinical fracture reduction versus risedronate; nonvertebral
    fragility fractures were 4.0% versus 6.1% with a hazard ratio of 0.66 whose
    confidence interval crosses 1 (95% CI 0.39 to 1.10; P=0.10). Vertebral
    fracture reduction is the well-supported claim; nonvertebral reduction
    against an active comparator is not established by this trial.
  - >-
    Most of the combination and sequencing evidence rests on bone mineral
    density, not fractures. DATA, DATA-Switch, DATA-HD and the standard-versus-
    cyclic trial enrolled 70 to 100 women each and measured density. DATA-Switch
    is explicit about the limit: its 48-month primary comparison between the two
    switch orders was not statistically significant (P=0.13), and radius density
    fell in one arm. These are mechanism-supporting results, not outcome results.
  - >-
    JOINT-05 tested a once-weekly Japanese regimen, not the product approved in
    the United States, and its comparator was alendronate rather than placebo.
    Its exploratory renal subgroup analysis then found the direction of effect
    was not uniform: vertebral fracture incidence favoured the teriparatide
    sequence in participants with preserved renal function, while in the small
    CKD 3b/4 subgroup (112 patients, 10 nonvertebral fracture events)
    nonvertebral fractures were fewer on alendronate alone.
  - >-
    Osteosarcoma is no longer boxed, but it did not disappear from the label.
    Section 5.1 of the current FORTEO label states that an increase in
    osteosarcoma was observed in male and female rats, that osteosarcoma has been
    reported in treated patients after marketing, that observational studies in
    humans have not shown an increased risk, and that data beyond 2 years of use
    are limited. Reporting the boxed warning's removal without that paragraph
    would misrepresent the record.
  - >-
    Registration is not evidence. NCT01796301 (STRUCTURE), a 436-patient Amgen
    phase 3 study using teriparatide as the open-label comparator against
    romosozumab, and NCT01611571, a 31-patient combination study in male
    osteoporosis, are both completed registrations whose publications are not
    among this page's verified citations. Their presence in a registry supports
    no claim here.
  - >-
    Nearly all of the largest trials were sponsor-run. The pivotal trial, VERO
    and both fracture-healing trials were Lilly studies; the independent work in
    this review is mostly the investigator-led DATA series (70 to 100 patients
    each) and the three network meta-analyses. Independence and size do not
    coincide in this evidence base.

Teriparatide is the compound that made osteoporosis treatment anabolic. Every drug before it worked by slowing bone loss; this one is a fragment of the body's own parathyroid hormone that, given in short daily pulses, builds bone instead. It has been approved in the United States since November 2002, and the evidence behind it is unusually direct — two randomized trials with radiographic fracture as the primary endpoint, not bone density.

It is also a drug whose most famous feature no longer exists. The osteosarcoma boxed warning was removed from the US label, and reporting that change accurately means reporting what replaced it.

This is the part most secondary sources get wrong, because the label changed and the old description stuck.

Three facts from the current FORTEO label, retrieved from the openFDA drug label endpoint on 17 August 2026 (the SPL version effective 3 June 2025):

- **There is no boxed warning.** The label's boxed-warning field is empty.
- **Section 4 lists exactly one contraindication:** hypersensitivity to teriparatide or to any of its excipients, with the label noting that hypersensitivity reactions have included angioedema and anaphylaxis. Everything else in the safety section below is a warning or precaution, not a contraindication.
- **Section 5.1 still describes osteosarcoma.** The label states an increase in osteosarcoma was observed in male and female rats given teriparatide, that osteosarcoma has been reported in treated patients in the postmarketing setting, that an increased risk has not been observed in human observational studies, and that data assessing risk beyond 2 years of use are limited.

The FDA's submission record shows an **efficacy** supplement to NDA 021318 — SUPPL 54 — approved on 16 November 2020, with a revised label and letter posted 1 December 2020 and a revised Medication Guide in February 2021. That is the submission record; it does not say what the supplement changed, so no causal link to the boxed warning's removal is asserted here. What can be stated is the current state of the label: no boxed warning, and section 2.3 framing use beyond two years as a benefit-risk judgement about continued high fracture risk rather than as a lifetime ceiling.

One more regulatory fact, and it is recent. On **3 August 2026** the FDA approved labeling supplements to FORTEO (SUPPL 59), BONSITY (SUPPL 21) *and* the abaloparatide product TYMLOS (SUPPL 18) on the same day, with labels and Medication Guides posted 11 August 2026. Three applications, three companies, one date — that pattern is a class-level labeling change rather than one sponsor's edit. **What the change says is not described on this page.** The drug-label records retrievable from openFDA still return the earlier SPL versions, so the content of the August 2026 revision could not be confirmed and is not guessed at here.

### Clinical Evidence

**The pivotal fracture trial.** 1,637 postmenopausal women with prior vertebral fractures were randomized to PTH(1-34) at one of two strengths or to placebo, with a median observation of 21 months. New vertebral fractures occurred in 14% of the placebo group and in 5% and 4% of the two active groups — relative risks of 0.35 (95% CI 0.22 to 0.55) and 0.31 (95% CI 0.19 to 0.50). New nonvertebral fragility fractures were 6% versus 3% in each active group. The paper's own conclusion was that the higher strength raised bone mineral density more but had similar effects on fracture risk and was more likely to cause side effects. The label's recommended amount is the lower of the two tested, a 20 mcg presentation. [PMID:11346808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11346808/)

**The head-to-head that osteoporosis rarely gets.** VERO enrolled 680 patients per group and stated plainly that no clinical trial had previously compared osteoporosis drugs with incident fractures as the primary outcome. Over 24 months, new vertebral fractures occurred in 5.4% on teriparatide versus 12.0% on weekly oral risedronate (risk ratio 0.44, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.68; P&lt;0.0001), and clinical fractures in 4.8% versus 9.8% (hazard ratio 0.48, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.74; P=0.0009). Nonvertebral fragility fractures were 4.0% versus 6.1%, which did not reach significance (hazard ratio 0.66, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.10; P=0.10). Funded by Lilly. [PMID:29129436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29129436/) The prospectively planned subgroup analysis found the advantage consistent across age, fracture history, glucocorticoid use and prior treatment. [PMID:29329484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29329484/)

**Effect size does not track baseline risk.** A FRAX-stratified re-analysis of the pivotal trial, merging the two active arms because their fracture effects did not differ, covered baseline 10-year major osteoporotic fracture probabilities from 2.2% to 67.2%. The reduction was 37% for all nonvertebral fractures, 56% for low-energy nonvertebral fractures and 66% for morphometric vertebral fractures, and the hazard ratios did not change significantly as baseline probability rose (P&gt;0.30). [PMID:26092063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26092063/)

**Independent syntheses agree, with caveats attached.** A BMJ network meta-analysis of 69 trials and more than 80,000 patients found bone anabolic agents more effective than oral bisphosphonates at preventing clinical and vertebral fractures, irrespective of baseline risk indicators, though the certainty in the estimates was moderate to low. [PMID:37130601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37130601/) The American College of Physicians living systematic review graded the vertebral and clinical fracture reduction as moderate to high certainty while recording that both PTH1 receptor agonists increased withdrawals due to adverse events. [PMID:36592455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36592455/) A 2025 network meta-analysis restricted to PTH1 receptor agonists put teriparatide ahead of denosumab and risedronate for vertebral fracture, but ahead of only alendronate and placebo for nonvertebral fracture. [PMID:40047881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40047881/)

**Sequencing and combination.** The investigator-led DATA series at Massachusetts General asked what happens when an anabolic agent meets a potent antiresorptive one. Combining teriparatide with denosumab raised 12-month lumbar spine density 9.1% versus 6.2% for teriparatide alone and 5.5% for denosumab alone. [PMID:23683600](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23683600/) DATA-Switch then followed 77 of those women for four years: spine density rose 18.3% in the teriparatide-then-denosumab order versus 14.0% in the reverse, a difference that was **not** statistically significant (P=0.13). The secondary hip and radius endpoints separated the orders more clearly — total hip 6.6% versus 2.8% (P=0.0002), femoral neck 8.3% versus 4.9% (P=0.0447), radius unchanged versus −1.8% — while the third arm, which took both drugs and then continued denosumab, did best of all at the hip (8.6%) and radius (2.8%). [PMID:26144908](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26144908/) DATA-HD, an open-label phase 4 trial in 76 women, found the double-strength arm reached 17.5% spine gain versus 9.5% (difference 8.1%, 95% CI 5.5 to 10.6; P&lt;0.0001). [PMID:31447409](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31447409/) Every one of these measured bone density, not fractures.

**Alternating versus continuous exposure.** In 70 postmenopausal women followed for three years, an alternating six-month sequence produced 36-month spine gains of 12% against 16% for the standard sequence (P=0.04), with hip, femoral neck and total-body results equivalent. The alternating arm avoided a decline at cortical sites seen at 18 months in the standard arm. [PMID:31419313](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31419313/)

**A combination result with a specific mechanism.** Adding raloxifene for six months produced the same rise in bone formation markers as teriparatide alone, but a significantly smaller rise in the resorption marker CTx (P=0.015). Total hip density rose 2.31% in the combination group, a significantly greater increase than in the teriparatide-alone group (P=0.04). 137 women, six months, biochemical and density endpoints only. [PMID:16234962](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16234962/)

**Osteonecrosis of the jaw.** A double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 34 participants with 47 lesions reported 45.4% of lesions resolved by 52 weeks versus 33.3% on placebo, with fewer bony defects at week 52 (odds ratio 8.1; P=0.017). Small, and not an approved indication. [PMID:32614699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32614699/)

### Where the Evidence Is Negative or Null

These results are as real as the ones above, and two of them are attempts to expand the label that failed.

- **Femoral neck fracture healing: no effect.** Two identically designed phase 3 trials (NCT01473589 and NCT01473602) were run specifically to support a fracture-healing indication. Both stopped early for very slow accrual, and their prespecified pooled analysis of 159 patients found 88% of the placebo group versus 84% of the teriparatide group free of revision surgery at 12 months (P=0.743), no difference in radiographic healing (75% versus 73%, P=0.692) and no difference in pain during ambulation (P=0.681). [PMID:26932738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26932738/)
- **Pelvic fracture healing: no radiographic or pain benefit.** A phase 2 trial in 35 patients with a mean age of 82 found 50% of teriparatide-treated and 53% of placebo-treated fractures healed at 3 months by CT, unchanged after adjustment, and no group difference in pain. Physical performance scores improved in the treated group only (group difference P&lt;0.03). [PMID:34383100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34383100/)
- **Osteogenesis imperfecta: density up, fractures unchanged.** A 2026 multicentre open-label trial randomized 350 adults across 27 referral centres to two years of teriparatide followed by zoledronic acid, or to standard care. Incident fractures occurred in 65 of 176 (36.9%) versus 63 of 173 (36.4%) — absolute risk reduction −1.57% (95% CI −9.90 to 5.89), hazard ratio 0.97 (95% CI 0.68 to 1.38) — while lumbar spine and total hip density rose significantly more in the treated group. The authors concluded that reduced bone quality rather than low density drives fracture in that disease. [PMID:42133304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42133304/)
- **The nonvertebral endpoint keeps falling short against active comparators.** VERO's nonvertebral hazard ratio was 0.66 with a confidence interval crossing 1, and the 2025 PTH1 network meta-analysis placed teriparatide above only alendronate and placebo for nonvertebral fracture while abaloparatide outperformed it at that site.
- **A subgroup where the direction reverses.** The exploratory renal analysis of JOINT-05 found vertebral fracture incidence lower on the teriparatide sequence in participants with preserved renal function (P=0.01), but in the 112 participants with CKD stage 3b/4 — carrying only 10 nonvertebral fracture events between them — nonvertebral fractures were fewer on alendronate alone. [PMID:39343826](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39343826/)
- **Registration is not evidence.** NCT01796301 (STRUCTURE), a 436-patient completed Amgen phase 3 study using teriparatide as the open-label comparator against romosozumab, and NCT01611571, a 31-patient combination study in male osteoporosis, appear here as registrations only. Their publications are not among this page's verified citations and nothing on this page rests on them.

### Safety Profile

No teriparatide product carries a boxed warning. The following is what the current FORTEO label states, retrieved from openFDA on 17 August 2026.

- **Osteosarcoma.** Section 5.1 of the label reports an increase in osteosarcoma in male and female rats, postmarketing reports of osteosarcoma in treated patients, no increased risk observed in human observational studies, and limited data beyond 2 years of use. The label directs prescribers away from use in people at raised baseline osteosarcoma risk — open epiphyses, metabolic bone diseases other than osteoporosis including Paget's disease, bone metastases or a history of skeletal malignancy, prior skeletal radiation, and hereditary predisposition.
- **Hypercalcemia and cutaneous calcification.** The label states that teriparatide may cause hypercalcemia and may worsen pre-existing hypercalcemia, and that calciphylaxis and worsening of previously stable cutaneous calcification have been reported after marketing, with underlying autoimmune disease, kidney failure and concomitant warfarin or systemic corticosteroid use listed as risk factors.
- **Urolithiasis.** The label reports that the frequency of urolithiasis in clinical trials was similar on teriparatide and placebo, but that the compound has not been studied in people with active urolithiasis, and it frames a risk-benefit weighing for anyone with active or recent stones.
- **Orthostatic hypotension.** The label describes transient orthostatic hypotension after the first several administrations, typically resolving on lying down.
- **Laboratory effects.** Per the label, at least one episode of transient hypercalcemia occurred in 11% of women and 6% of men versus 2% and 0% on placebo, verified on consecutive measurement in 3% and 1%. Serum uric acid above the upper limit of normal occurred in 3% versus 1% without an increase in gout, arthralgia or urolithiasis.
- **Common adverse events.** The label lists arthralgia, pain and nausea above 10%, and reports serious adverse events in 16% of the teriparatide group versus 19% of placebo across the two principal osteoporosis trials of 1,382 patients.
- **Interaction.** The only interaction in section 7 of the label is digoxin: transient hypercalcemia may predispose to digitalis toxicity.
- **Contraindication.** Section 4 lists hypersensitivity to teriparatide or to any of its excipients, and nothing else. Every other item above is a warning or precaution.

### How This Page Relates to Abaloparatide

The [abaloparatide](/peptides/abaloparatide) dossier covers the only other PTH1 receptor agonist approved for osteoporosis in the United States, and the two pages are deliberately built as a pair. They are **not** the same class of molecule: teriparatide is a fragment of parathyroid hormone itself, while abaloparatide is an analogue of parathyroid hormone-related protein, a different endogenous ligand at the same receptor. The TYMLOS label puts numbers on the distance — 41% homology to human PTH(1-34) against 76% homology to human PTHrP(1-34).

They met directly in one trial. ACTIVE (NCT01343004) randomized 2,463 postmenopausal women to abaloparatide, placebo, or open-label teriparatide, so the comparison between them is a between-arm comparison inside a trial powered against placebo, not a head-to-head fracture trial. The one prespecified comparison between the two active arms was a safety endpoint: hypercalcemia occurred in 3.4% on abaloparatide versus 6.4% on teriparatide (risk difference −2.96, 95% CI −5.12 to −0.87; P=0.006). [PMID:27533157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27533157/)

The 2025 network meta-analysis of PTH1 receptor agonists reports abaloparatide ahead of teriparatide for nonvertebral fractures (odds ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.80 to 0.95) and hip fractures (odds ratio 0.81, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.93), while both were superior to placebo, raloxifene and calcitonin for vertebral fracture and only teriparatide was further superior to denosumab and risedronate there. [PMID:40047881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40047881/) That is indirect evidence from pooled trials, not a head-to-head fracture result, and no such trial exists.

### Important Limitations

- Vertebral fracture reduction is the well-supported claim; the nonvertebral benefit did not reach significance against risedronate in VERO
- Every attempt to extend the compound beyond osteoporosis — femoral neck healing, pelvic healing, osteogenesis imperfecta — has missed its primary endpoint
- The combination and sequencing literature measures bone mineral density, in trials of 70 to 100 women, and DATA-Switch's own primary comparison was not significant
- JOINT-05 tested a once-weekly Japanese regimen against alendronate, and its effect size does not transfer to the once-daily US product
- Removal of the boxed warning did not remove the osteosarcoma paragraph from section 5.1 of the label, and data beyond two years of use remain limited by the label's own account
- The largest trials were sponsor-run; the independent work is small
- The class-wide labeling supplement approved 3 August 2026 is recorded here as an event only — its content could not be retrieved and is not described

This page is an evidence review, not medical guidance. It does not describe how any product is used, and nothing here should be read as a recommendation.
