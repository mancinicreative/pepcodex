**Correction notice.** This article previously reported the discovery of a mitochondrial-derived peptide, described as "SHLP-8," said to be elevated in centenarians enrolled in the New England Centenarian Study. No such study is retrievable, and a PubMed search returns no peptide called SHLP-8. The specific figures previously published here — an 850-participant cohort, a 2.5-fold elevation, and associations with executive function and gait speed — have been removed as unverifiable. What follows is what the field does support.

## What We Know

Mitochondrial-derived peptides (MDPs) are short peptides encoded within mitochondrial DNA that appear to have signalling functions beyond the mitochondrion's traditional role in energy production.

**Humanin**, described in 2001, was the first identified and has been linked to neuroprotection and insulin sensitivity.

**MOTS-c** regulates metabolic homeostasis and is sometimes described as an "exercise mimetic" for its effects on muscle.

**The SHLP family** (small humanin-like peptides) comprises SHLP1 through SHLP6, characterised as a group in work published in *Aging* in 2016. Individual members have since been studied for specific roles — SHLP2, for instance, in macular degeneration and as a candidate prostate cancer biomarker, and SHLP6 in computational modelling of neurodegeneration and cellular aging published in 2025.

There is no SHLP7 or SHLP8 in the published literature.

The evolutionary conservation of these peptides across species suggests biological importance. Researchers hypothesise that MDPs may serve as signals of mitochondrial health, communicating cellular energy-producing capacity to other tissues.

## What It Means

The MDP field is genuinely interesting and genuinely early.

**Scientific understanding:** MDPs are consistent with the mitochondrial theory of aging, which posits that accumulated mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to age-related decline. Whether any MDP preserves mitochondrial function in a way that matters at the organism level is unresolved.

**Biomarker potential:** Circulating MDP levels change with age, which makes them candidate biomarkers. Association with an outcome does not establish that the peptide causes it — an important caution given how often MDP findings are reported as longevity breakthroughs.

**Therapeutic development:** Humanin analogs are in preclinical development for conditions ranging from Alzheimer's disease to metabolic syndrome. None has an approved therapeutic indication.

## What's Next

Progress in this field depends on the unglamorous work: mechanistic studies of how specific MDPs affect autophagy, senescence and stress resistance; longitudinal cohorts to test whether levels predict outcomes; genetic analyses to probe causality; and lifespan or healthspan studies in model organisms.

Readers should be sceptical of any claim that a named mitochondrial peptide has been shown to drive human longevity. That claim has not been established for any member of this family.

This information is provided for educational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice. Longevity research is in early stages, and no therapies based on these findings are currently available.
