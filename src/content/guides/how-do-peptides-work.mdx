## Start with a specific molecule

To understand a peptide mechanism claim, identify the molecule and ask what was actually measured. The example below describes semaglutide; use it to practice reading a mechanism, not as a general account of every peptide.

## Receptor activation: a documented example

Semaglutide's prescribing information describes it as a GLP-1 receptor agonist: it binds to and activates that receptor. The label describes glucose-dependent stimulation of insulin secretion and reduction of glucagon secretion. [Semaglutide tablet labeling, section 12.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=27f15fac-7d98-4114-a2ec-92494a91da98).

When checking another peptide's proposed action, ask which target was tested and whether the report describes binding, activation, or a measured downstream response. Keep the report's experimental setting attached to the claim.

## Structure and formulation in this example

The same label describes albumin binding and resistance to DPP-4 degradation as contributors to semaglutide's prolonged exposure. Its tablet formulation contains SNAC, an absorption enhancer. These details concern the named molecule and formulation. [Semaglutide tablet labeling, sections 11-12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=27f15fac-7d98-4114-a2ec-92494a91da98).

Do not use this example to infer how another peptide is absorbed, distributed, or cleared. Check its own supporting evidence rather than transferring a half-life, route, or formulation assumption from semaglutide.

## Separate a mechanism claim from an outcome claim

For each claim you read, record:

- **Identity:** the exact molecule and formulation studied.
- **Setting:** the experimental model or study population.
- **Measurement:** the target interaction or outcome actually measured.
- **Limits:** what the source leaves uncertain.

When a claim concerns a benefit or harm in people, look for research on that outcome and population rather than treating a proposed mechanism as the answer.

*This example is educational, not a recommendation to use a product or a statement about the safety or effectiveness of other peptides.*
