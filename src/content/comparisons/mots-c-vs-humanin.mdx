## Two peptides, different evidence streams

MOTS-c is a 16-residue peptide encoded in the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region. Humanin is a 24-residue peptide encoded in the 16S rRNA region. Both have laboratory and animal effects, but endogenous concentrations, native administration and modified analogs are separate evidence types. [MOTS-c discovery, PMID 25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) · [humanin cell discovery, PMID 11717357](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11717357/)

| Question | MOTS-c | Humanin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selected human work | Endogenous exercise, sleep-apnea and diabetes observations; no cited native administration result | Endogenous circulating or tissue associations; no cited controlled native administration result |
| Intervention models | Mouse metabolic, physical-capacity and cachexia experiments | Cell anti-apoptotic work and animal experiments; HNG is a modified analog |
| Therapeutic-claim grade | **Low** for human benefit | **Low** for human benefit |

MOTS-c's 2015 mouse study reported metabolic effects. A 2021 paper measured MOTS-c around exercise in people but administered peptide to mice for physical-capacity experiments. A 2026 human physiology study did not observe an exercise-related arterio-venous difference, so the claim that it consistently rises with exercise is too broad. [PMID 25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) · [PMID 33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/) · [PMID 41520850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41520850/)

A separate 2026 C26 mouse study found partial skeletal-muscle preservation with preventive MOTS-c exposure, but no prevention of overall body or fat loss and no human treatment arm. It cannot establish a cachexia or exercise-capacity treatment in people. [PMID 42266945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42266945/)

Native humanin reduced apoptosis in early cell models; BAX interaction was studied in cells. A mouse healthspan experiment used the stronger **HNG analog**, while the same research reported human endogenous-level associations. Humanin biomarkers show different age or disease relationships across populations. Neither the HNG mouse result nor human biomarker patterns prove native-humanin anti-aging efficacy. [PMID 12732850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12732850/) · [PMID 32575074](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32575074/) · [PMID 33131010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33131010/) · [PMID 39102184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39102184/)

[NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) registers native MOTS-c administration without a cited outcome. [NCT03998514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) tested the separate CB4211 analog. A kidney-transplant anesthesia registry measures MOTS-c and humanin but administers neither peptide. [NCT07678073](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07678073)

The selected sources contain no direct comparison or combination result. Endogenous nature does not establish administration safety; FDA reports no identified exposure data for compounded MOTS-c products. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

This page describes selected research rather than a longevity intervention.
