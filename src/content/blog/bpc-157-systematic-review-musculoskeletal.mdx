**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article described a systematic review
said to have screened over 400 records and included 67 studies under PRISMA guidelines.
That review, and those figures, could not be located in the published literature and
have been removed. The verified systematic review of this space — published in 2025 in
*HSS Journal* — is summarised below, together with the primary studies that can actually
be checked.

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound-157) is a synthetic pentadecapeptide derived from a
sequence found in human gastric juice proteins. Originally investigated for
gastrointestinal applications, it has attracted sustained interest for musculoskeletal
healing on the strength of its animal literature. A 2025 systematic review in *HSS
Journal* examined its emerging use in orthopaedic sports medicine
[hss-systematic-review] — and its central finding is the same one this site's
[BPC-157 dossier](/peptides/bpc-157) reaches: the preclinical data is consistent, and
the human data is nearly nonexistent.

## What the Preclinical Literature Shows

The animal studies most often cited for musculoskeletal claims are individually
verifiable, and several are methodologically substantial:

**Tendon healing.** In a rat Achilles tendon transection model, BPC-157 promoted tendon
outgrowth, cell survival, and cell migration [tendon-healing-study]. Follow-up work by
the same group found that BPC-157 enhances growth hormone receptor expression in tendon
fibroblasts, proposing a mechanism by which the peptide could amplify local growth
hormone signalling at the injury site [tendon-ghr-study].

**Angiogenesis.** BPC-157's pro-angiogenic activity has been associated with VEGFR2
activation and upregulation [vegfr2-study] — relevant because new blood vessel
formation is rate-limiting for tendon and ligament repair, tissues with poor baseline
blood supply.

**Breadth across models.** The wider literature reports positive findings in muscle
crush and transection models, ligament injury models, and fracture healing. That
breadth is documented at review level [hss-systematic-review] — and it cuts both ways,
as discussed below.

## What the Systematic Review Could Not Find

The 2025 review's human-evidence findings are quickly stated. The only human efficacy
data in the orthopaedic literature is a retrospective, uncontrolled case series in knee
pain — self-reported improvement in 7 of 12 patients after intra-articular
administration [hss-systematic-review]. A Phase 2 programme (PL-14736) was halted
before completion. No randomised controlled trial of BPC-157 for any musculoskeletal
indication has been completed and published.

A 2026 biopharmaceutical review fills in the development picture: fewer than 30 human
subjects across three uncontrolled pilot studies, no characterised human
pharmacokinetic profile, and no approved formulation [bpc-157-review].

This means none of the fundamental clinical questions have answers:

- Does BPC-157 work in humans as it does in rats?
- What exposure produces effects in humans, by which route?
- What is the human safety profile?
- How would it compare to standard care for any specific injury?

## Reading the Evidence Honestly

**Single-group provenance.** The literature is dominated by one research group
(Sikirić and colleagues, Zagreb), with limited independent replication — a structural
weakness no quantity of studies from the same laboratory can fix.

**Breadth as a warning sign.** A compound reported to heal tendon, ligament, muscle,
bone, and gut, and to counteract toxins and organ injury besides, is either acting on
something very fundamental or being tested in ways that generate positive results.
Distinguishing those possibilities requires exactly the independent, controlled work
that is missing.

**Preclinical consistency is real but insufficient.** The animal evidence base is
stronger than for many compounds attracting similar interest. The history of drug
development is nonetheless full of preclinically consistent compounds that failed in
controlled human trials.

## What's Next

One development since the earlier version of this article is genuinely new: a
placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial of BPC-157 for acute hamstring muscle strain
(NCT07437547) is registered and was recruiting as of an August 2026 registry check —
the first controlled human test of the musculoskeletal claims. Its status and context
are covered in [BPC-157 human safety data: what actually exists](/blog/bpc-157-first-human-safety-data).

Meanwhile, BPC-157 remains unapproved, classified by the FDA as ineligible for
compounding, and sold only through unregulated channels. The safety questions that
follow from that are examined in [Is BPC-157 safe?](/blog/is-bpc-157-safe), and the
same fragment-versus-evidence pattern in a related repair peptide is covered in the
[TB-500 dossier](/peptides/tb-500).

The BPC-157 story remains a case study in the gap between preclinical promise and
clinical validation. The systematic review provides the most rigorous published
assessment of what the evidence shows — and, equally important, what it does not.
