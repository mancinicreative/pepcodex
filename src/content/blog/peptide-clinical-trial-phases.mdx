## A phase label is a starting point

“Phase 2” identifies a study designed to gather preliminary effectiveness and further safety information. It does not say whether the trial met its outcome, establish a treatment for every population, or indicate FDA approval ([FDA clinical research](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-3-clinical-research); [FDA drug review](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-4-fda-drug-review)). For the definitions of each stage, see our [trial-phase guide](/guide/peptide-clinical-trial-phases).

**Evidence grade: moderate for this single-trial interpretation.** The published study supports findings for its participants and measured outcomes. The phase designation alone supports no efficacy claim.

## Example: read the study behind the label

The registered [NCT04881760 study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04881760) and its [2023 peer-reviewed report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/) identify a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial of retatrutide in adults with obesity, or overweight with a weight-related condition. The **phase belongs to this study**; it should not be read as a current status label for every retatrutide program.

| Question | What this study reports | Careful interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who and compared with what? | The trial enrolled 338 adults and compared retatrutide groups with placebo ([Jastreboff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)). | Its estimates concern the studied population and comparison. |
| What was measured first? | The primary endpoint was percentage change in body weight at 24 weeks; 48-week weight measures were secondary endpoints ([Jastreboff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)). | Read the endpoint and time point before repeating a result. |
| What did the report find? | Weight change favored retatrutide groups over placebo; gastrointestinal adverse events were the most common in those groups ([Jastreboff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)). | A benefit signal and observed harms both belong in the summary. |
| What remains outside its scope? | The reported comparison followed participants for 48 weeks ([Jastreboff et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37366315/)). | This trial cannot settle outcomes beyond that observation window or in unstudied populations. |

This is why a headline based only on “Phase 2” loses the crucial evidence: comparator, endpoint, population, and duration. The 2023 paper is a record of **that trial at that time**, not a live inventory of later research or regulatory decisions. Check the dated [registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04881760) for this study and any separate studies before describing a product's present development program.

## Trial results and approval answer different questions

FDA explains that Phase 3 studies are designed to provide expanded evidence about effectiveness and safety in a specified population. A later marketing application is reviewed separately using the full submitted record, including study reports and other information. Neither a Phase 2 finding nor the words “Phase 3” amount to an approval decision ([FDA clinical research](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-3-clinical-research); [FDA drug review](https://www.fda.gov/patients/drug-development-process/step-4-fda-drug-review)).
