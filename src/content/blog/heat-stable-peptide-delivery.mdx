Peptide therapeutics depend on refrigeration, and that dependence is one of the real constraints on who can access them. Thermostabilisation — reformulating a biologic so it survives without a cold chain — is an active research field with genuine successes in vaccines. It is worth being clear that those successes have not yet produced a heat-stable peptide therapeutic.

## The Cold Chain Challenge

Peptide therapeutics like semaglutide and tirzepatide require refrigeration at 2–8°C through much of their supply chain. This creates several linked problems:

- **Infrastructure costs:** Cold storage facilities and refrigerated transport add substantial expense
- **Geographic limitations:** Regions without reliable electricity struggle to maintain cold chains
- **Waste:** Product exposed to out-of-range temperatures may have to be discarded
- **Patient burden:** Home storage requires dedicated refrigerator space, and travel becomes harder

The World Health Organization treats rigorous temperature control as a precondition for immunisation programmes working at all, and identifies strengthening supply chains — so that products are stored and distributed under the right conditions — as an explicit goal of the Immunization Agenda 2030. WHO also promotes the Controlled Temperature Chain approach, which permits limited excursions outside standard cold-chain conditions for products qualified for it [who-cold-chain].

## What Thermostabilisation Research Has Achieved

The most instructive published results come from vaccines rather than peptide drugs.

Researchers formulated inactivated polio vaccine (IPV) as a dried thin film in silk fibroin, a protein derived from silk. The results were substantial: silk fibroin IPV films maintained 70% D-antigen potency after storage for nearly three years at room temperature, and retained greater than 50% potency for the IPV-2 and IPV-3 serotypes at 45°C for one year. After two weeks of storage at elevated temperature, the stressed films produced immune responses equivalent to commercial vaccine for two of the three serotypes tested [silk-fibroin-ipv].

That is a real demonstration that a biologic requiring 2–8°C storage can be reformatted into something that tolerates 45°C for extended periods, with function preserved.

### Why This Does Not Transfer Automatically to Peptides

Several things separate that result from a heat-stable semaglutide:

**Different molecule, different failure mode.** A vaccine antigen's requirement is that enough epitope structure survives to provoke an immune response. A therapeutic peptide has to retain receptor-binding activity and stay free of degradation products at injectable purity — a stricter and different bar.

**Dried formats need reconstitution.** A dried film or powder is not a pre-filled pen. Adding a reconstitution step changes the product's usability, especially for self-administered weekly injections, and changes the sterility assurance requirements.

**Excipient approval.** Novel matrix materials require their own safety assessment as excipients in an injectable product.

**Regulatory stability requirements.** Any claim about storage conditions has to be established through formal stability testing. The ICH Q1A(R2) framework governs how stability data for new drug substances and products is generated and what storage-condition claims it can support [stability-testing-guidelines]. A laboratory finding is not a labelled storage condition until that work is done.

## What Would Change If It Worked

If peptide therapeutics could be made cold-chain-independent, the consequences would be significant:

- Distribution through existing non-refrigerated pharmaceutical supply chains
- Reach into rural health facilities without reliable power
- Reduced waste from temperature excursions
- Easier travel and simpler home storage for patients in every market

These are the reasons the research is worth doing. They are not, however, evidence that it has been done.

## What This Means

Cold-chain dependence is a genuine and consequential limitation on global access to peptide medicines, and thermostabilisation is a legitimate research direction with demonstrated results in adjacent product classes.

But no heat-stable formulation of a peptide therapeutic has been established in humans, and there is no approved product with a room-temperature storage claim in this class. Anyone encountering marketing that implies otherwise — particularly for products sold outside regulated supply chains — should treat the claim as unsupported, since improper storage of a peptide product is a quality problem that is invisible until it is a potency problem.
