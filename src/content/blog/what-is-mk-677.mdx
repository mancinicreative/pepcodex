## What Two Decades of Human Studies Actually Show

The beginner definition is the [MK-677 guide](/guide/what-is-mk-677). The compound record is [MK-677](/peptides/mk-677). This page is the evidence and discontinuation story — not a second "what is it" article.

MK-677 (ibutamoren) is a small molecule that stimulates growth hormone release by mimicking the hormone ghrelin. Unlike most compounds in this category, it is orally active — it survives digestion, which injectable growth hormone peptides do not. It is **not FDA-approved** for any use, and its clinical development was discontinued.

MK-677 is technically not a peptide, but it targets the same receptor as peptide secretagogues such as [ipamorelin](/peptides/ipamorelin), so it appears throughout the [GH secretagogue](/blog/what-are-gh-secretagogues) research literature. The full evidence-graded record lives in the [MK-677 dossier](/peptides/mk-677).

## How Does It Work?

MK-677 activates the ghrelin receptor (GHSR), which signals the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Because it mimics ghrelin — sometimes called the hunger hormone — increased appetite is a commonly reported effect in studies.

Unlike injected growth hormone, MK-677 works through the body's own regulatory circuits, producing pulsatile GH release rather than constant elevation. In healthy older adults, daily oral administration restored GH and IGF-1 levels toward the range seen in young adults [chapman-1996].

## What Do the Human Studies Show?

MK-677 was developed by Merck in the 1990s and has more published human data than most unapproved compounds:

- **GH and IGF-1 elevation.** Daily administration raised GH and IGF-1 consistently across trials in elderly, obese, and healthy volunteers [chapman-1996].
- **Body composition.** A two-month randomized trial in obese men reported increased fat-free mass without a significant change in total or visceral fat [svensson-1998].
- **Catabolism.** In a short-term calorie-restriction model, MK-677 reversed diet-induced protein loss, suggesting an anti-catabolic effect [murphy-1998].
- **Sleep.** A small crossover study reported improved sleep quality, including increased deep (stage IV) and REM sleep [copinschi-1997].
- **Bone turnover.** Treatment increased biochemical markers of both bone formation and bone resorption [svensson-bone-1998].

### The trials that ended development

Two longer studies are the main reason MK-677 never became a medicine:

1. **Hip fracture recovery.** A randomized trial of MK-0677 in patients recovering from hip fracture found IGF-1 increases but did not establish a convincing functional recovery benefit [bach-2004].
2. **Healthy aging.** A 12-month double-blind randomized trial in healthy older adults (with extensions to two years) found that MK-677 increased GH and IGF-1 and modestly increased fat-free mass — but did not significantly improve strength or function, and adverse effects included increased appetite, transient lower-extremity edema, and muscle pain. Fasting glucose and insulin sensitivity moved unfavorably [nass-2008].

The pattern across the program: the hormone levels move, the clinically meaningful outcomes largely do not. No Phase III program was completed.

## Known Concerns

- **Insulin sensitivity.** Reduced insulin sensitivity and increased fasting glucose were observed in the longest trial [nass-2008]
- **Water retention and increased appetite** are commonly reported
- **Long-term safety is not established** — as of the studies verified for this article (2026-08-17), no published trial has followed users beyond two years
- **Unregulated products.** Because it was never approved, everything sold as MK-677 online is outside pharmaceutical quality control — see our review of [growth hormone peptide safety](/blog/growth-hormone-peptides-safety)

## Important Warnings

- Not approved for medical use by any regulatory agency
- Banned in competitive sport under WADA rules
- May raise blood glucose; the published trials monitored this closely
