## What is Wegovy?

Wegovy is a brand name for [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide), a GLP-1 receptor agonist FDA-approved for chronic weight management. It contains the same active ingredient as Ozempic, but at a higher maintenance strength (2.4 mg) and with a different approved use: Ozempic is labeled for type 2 diabetes, while Wegovy is labeled for obesity and overweight [wegovy-review].

## How is Wegovy Different from Ozempic?

| Feature | Wegovy | Ozempic |
|---------|--------|---------|
| **Approved for** | Weight management; cardiovascular risk reduction | Type 2 diabetes; cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction |
| **Maximum strength** | 2.4 mg weekly | 2 mg weekly |
| **First FDA approval** | June 2021 | December 2017 |

For a fuller side-by-side, see the [Ozempic vs Wegovy comparison](/compare/ozempic-vs-wegovy).

## How Does Wegovy Work?

Semaglutide mimics GLP-1, a gut hormone released after eating. It:

1. **Reduces hunger signaling** in appetite centers of the brain
2. **Increases satiety**, so smaller meals feel sufficient
3. **Slows gastric emptying**, extending fullness after meals
4. **Improves blood sugar control** as a secondary effect

## Who is Wegovy Approved For?

### FDA-approved indications

Adults with:
- **Obesity** (BMI of 30 or higher), OR
- **Overweight** (BMI of 27 or higher) with at least one weight-related condition (high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol)

Also approved for:
- **Cardiovascular risk reduction** in adults with obesity or overweight and established cardiovascular disease, based on the SELECT trial [select-nejm]

### Pediatric use

Approved for adolescents aged 12 and older with obesity.

## What Does the Research Show?

The STEP program is the core clinical evidence behind Wegovy. Reported figures below are the published treatment-policy results:

| Trial | Population | Weight change (2.4 mg vs placebo) |
|-------|------------|-----------------------------------|
| STEP 1 [step1-nejm] | Obesity, no diabetes | 14.9% vs 2.4% at 68 weeks |
| STEP 2 [step2-lancet] | Obesity with type 2 diabetes | 9.6% vs 3.4% |
| STEP 3 [step3-jama] | With intensive behavioral therapy | 16.0% vs 5.7% |

### Does the weight loss last?

STEP 5 followed participants for two years and found weight loss was largely maintained while treatment continued [step5-natmed]. Trials that withdrew treatment have generally observed weight regain, which is why the label frames semaglutide as a chronic therapy rather than a short course.

### Cardiovascular outcomes

The SELECT trial (N=17,604) tested semaglutide 2.4 mg in adults with obesity or overweight and established cardiovascular disease but no diabetes. It reported a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events versus placebo, which led to the additional FDA indication [select-nejm].

## How Does Wegovy Compare to Tirzepatide?

The SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial compared semaglutide 2.4 mg with [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide), the dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist sold as Zepbound. At 72 weeks, tirzepatide produced 20.2% average weight loss versus 13.7% with semaglutide [surmount5-nejm]. Both are highly effective by historical standards; our [SURMOUNT-5 analysis](/blog/surmount-5-tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide) covers the trade-offs in detail.

## Is There a Wegovy Pill?

Yes. The FDA approved an oral semaglutide 25 mg tablet under the Wegovy brand in December 2025 — the first oral GLP-1 formulation approved for chronic weight management. See our coverage of the [oral Wegovy launch](/blog/wegovy-pill-launches-us).

## Common Side Effects

Gastrointestinal effects dominate the safety profile:

- Nausea (typically improves over time)
- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Constipation
- Abdominal pain

## Important Considerations

Wegovy carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors observed in rodents. The label states it should not be used by people with:

- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2
