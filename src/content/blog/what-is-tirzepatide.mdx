## Introduction

Tirzepatide is an injectable medication that helps control blood sugar levels and promotes weight loss. It is sold under the brand names **Mounjaro** (for type 2 diabetes) and **Zepbound** (for weight management and obstructive sleep apnea).

What makes tirzepatide different from similar medications is that it activates two hormone receptors instead of one — and in the trials run so far, that has translated into the largest weight-loss effects of any approved medication in its class [acp-living-nma].

---

## How Does Tirzepatide Work?

Tirzepatide works by mimicking two natural hormones your body produces after eating:

### GLP-1 (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1)
- Signals your pancreas to release insulin when blood sugar rises
- Slows down how quickly food leaves your stomach
- Helps you feel full longer

### GIP (Glucose-Dependent Insulinotropic Polypeptide)
- Also helps release insulin when blood sugar is elevated
- May have additional effects on fat metabolism

By activating both of these pathways, tirzepatide is called a **dual incretin agonist**. Our explainer on [how GLP-1 agonists work](/blog/how-do-glp-1-agonists-work) covers the underlying biology.

## What is Tirzepatide Used For?

### FDA-approved uses

| Condition | Brand name | Approved |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Type 2 diabetes** | Mounjaro | May 2022 |
| **Chronic weight management** | Zepbound | November 2023 |
| **Obstructive sleep apnea with obesity** | Zepbound | December 2024 |

### For type 2 diabetes
Tirzepatide is approved for adults with type 2 diabetes to improve blood sugar control, alone or combined with other diabetes medications, as an addition to diet and exercise.

### For weight management
Tirzepatide (as Zepbound) is approved for adults with obesity (BMI of 30 or higher), or overweight (BMI of 27 or higher) with at least one weight-related health condition.

### For obstructive sleep apnea
In December 2024, Zepbound became the first medication approved specifically for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity, after the SURMOUNT-OSA trials showed large reductions in breathing interruptions during sleep [surmount-osa-nejm]. See our full coverage of the [Zepbound sleep apnea approval](/blog/zepbound-sleep-apnea-approval).

### How is it administered?
Tirzepatide is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection from a pre-filled pen. The label specifies a stepwise titration from a low starting strength to the maintenance strength, which reduces gastrointestinal side effects.

## What Does the Research Show?

### For diabetes control

Clinical trials (called SURPASS) showed tirzepatide significantly lowered HbA1c (a measure of average blood sugar):

| Trial | Result |
|-------|--------|
| SURPASS-1 | 1.9-2.1% reduction vs placebo |
| SURPASS-2 | 2.0-2.3% reduction (vs semaglutide 1mg: 1.9%) |

Many participants achieved an HbA1c below 7%, the typical treatment goal.

### For weight loss

Clinical trials (called SURMOUNT) showed substantial weight loss. Figures are the published treatment-policy results:

| Trial | Population | Weight loss (15mg) |
|-------|------------|--------------------|
| SURMOUNT-1 [pmid-35658024] | Obesity, no diabetes | 20.9% of body weight |
| SURMOUNT-2 | Obesity with diabetes | 14.7% of body weight |

In SURMOUNT-4, participants who continued tirzepatide after an initial treatment period kept losing weight, while those switched to placebo regained much of it — evidence that the effect depends on continued treatment [pmid-38078870].

### Head-to-head against semaglutide

SURMOUNT-5 compared tirzepatide directly with semaglutide 2.4 mg in adults with obesity. At 72 weeks, tirzepatide produced 20.2% average weight loss versus 13.7% with semaglutide [surmount5-nejm]. Our [SURMOUNT-5 analysis](/blog/surmount-5-tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide) and the [tirzepatide vs semaglutide comparison](/compare/tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide) break down what that difference means in practice.

A 2026 living systematic review and network meta-analysis for the American College of Physicians, covering 69 studies and 112,511 participants, ranked tirzepatide and semaglutide as the two most effective medications for weight loss [acp-living-nma].

### Cardiovascular outcomes — noninferior, not superior

Tirzepatide's dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial, SURPASS-CVOT (n=13,299), compared it against dulaglutide — an active GLP-1 comparator with proven cardiovascular benefit — on major adverse cardiovascular events. The primary results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 18 December 2025: tirzepatide was **noninferior** to dulaglutide (hazard ratio 0.92, 95.3% CI 0.83–1.01), but **superiority was not established** (P=0.09) [surpass-cvot-nejm]. Full outcome tables were posted to the ClinicalTrials.gov registry on 2026-07-08 [surpass-cvot-registry].

That published result aligns with a 2026 meta-analysis of mostly observational comparisons, which also found tirzepatide was **not** significantly better than GLP-1 receptor agonists on major cardiovascular events (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.70-1.04) [mace-meta-2026]. Tirzepatide's superior weight loss has not been shown to translate into superior cardiovascular protection.

## Is Tirzepatide Safe?

Like all medications, tirzepatide has potential side effects. The most common are related to the digestive system:

### Common side effects
- **Nausea** (12-18% of people)
- **Diarrhea** (12-17%)
- **Decreased appetite** (5-10%)
- **Vomiting** (5-9%)
- **Constipation** (6-7%)

These side effects are usually mild to moderate and tend to improve over time, especially with the label's gradual titration.

### Serious considerations

Tirzepatide carries a boxed warning about thyroid tumors observed in animal studies. While this has not been confirmed in humans, the label states it should not be used by:

- People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer
- People with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)
- People with known hypersensitivity to tirzepatide

For complete safety information, see our [GLP-1 Agonist Safety Overview](/blog/glp1-safety-overview).

## Key Takeaways

1. **What it is:** A once-weekly injectable medication that activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors

2. **What it's for:** FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes (Mounjaro), weight management, and obstructive sleep apnea with obesity (Zepbound)

3. **How well it works:** 20.9% average weight loss in SURMOUNT-1, and greater weight loss than semaglutide in the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial

4. **What is resolved — and what isn't:** The published SURPASS-CVOT trial showed tirzepatide is noninferior to dulaglutide on cardiovascular events, but superiority over existing GLP-1 drugs was not established

5. **Common side effects:** Mostly digestive (nausea, diarrhea) that often improve over time

6. **Important note:** This is a prescription medication that should only be used under medical supervision
