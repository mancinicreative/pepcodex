## Research comparison

MOTS-c is a 16-residue mitochondrial peptide whose original study examined metabolism and AMPK-related signaling in cells and mice. SHLP-2 is a humanin-like peptide from the mitochondrial 16S region; its model research has investigated cytoprotection and energy regulation. These are different sequences, not interchangeable formulations. [MOTS-c discovery, PMID 25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) · [SHLP-2 receptor study, PMID 37468558](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468558/)

| Question | MOTS-c | SHLP-2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human evidence cited | Four endogenous biomarker/physiology papers; one registered native intervention with no cited result | A naturally occurring SHLP-2 K4R variant was associated with lower Parkinson disease risk; no demonstrated administered-peptide benefit ([PMID 38167865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38167865/)) |
| Mechanistic evidence | Mouse metabolic and physical-capacity studies | CXCR7-mediated hypothalamic signaling in experimental models |
| Therapeutic-claim grade | **Low** | **Low** for human benefit |

Human MOTS-c studies measured circulating peptide in sleep apnea, diabetes and exercise settings, while administration experiments were in mice. The 2026 physiology study did not find an exercise-related human arterio-venous difference. A 2026 C26 mouse/cell cachexia paper reported partial muscle preservation with preventive exposure, without preserving overall body or fat mass; it is not a human outcome. [PMID 40538389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40538389/) · [PMID 40855115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40855115/) · [PMID 41520850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41520850/) · [PMID 42266945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42266945/)

SHLP-2's 2023 CXCR7 study is a model mechanism. A separate primary study reported that the naturally occurring mitochondrial m.2158 T>C variant, which produces K4R SHLP-2, was associated with lower Parkinson disease risk; its cell and mouse experiments tested mechanisms, not human SHLP-2 treatment. Genotype association is not an administered-peptide result and cannot establish that giving SHLP-2 improves metabolism, protects cells clinically or extends life. [CXCR7 model, PMID 37468558](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468558/) · [K4R association, PMID 38167865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38167865/)

[NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) is a native MOTS-c Phase 2 registration with no cited efficacy result. The completed [CB4211 registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) concerns an analog. Neither record supplies a SHLP-2 or MOTS-c-plus-SHLP-2 outcome.

FDA reports insufficient safety information for compounded MOTS-c products; no combination-safety inference follows from mitochondrial origin. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)
