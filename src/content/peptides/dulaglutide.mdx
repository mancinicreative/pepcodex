Dulaglutide is not a new entrant. It is the drug the new entrants are measured against — the active comparator arm in trials this site already cites, including PIONEER 10 (NCT03015220), SUSTAIN 7, SURPASS J-mono, and the 13,299-participant SURPASS-CVOT (NCT04255433). Reading it as "another GLP-1" misses the point. The honest framing is an incumbent that generally loses on weight to the agents that came after it, while owning a cardiovascular outcomes result that most of them do not have.

### How It Works (Simplified)

Per the FDA label, dulaglutide is a human GLP-1 receptor agonist whose GLP-1 analogue portion is 90% homologous to endogenous human GLP-1 (7-37). It activates the GLP-1 receptor, a membrane-bound cell-surface receptor coupled to adenylyl cyclase in pancreatic beta cells, raising intracellular cAMP and producing glucose-dependent insulin release. It also decreases glucagon secretion and slows gastric emptying.

### Molecular Identity

Dulaglutide is structurally unlike the small GLP-1 analogues on this site. The FDA label describes a fusion protein of two identical, disulfide-linked chains, each containing an N-terminal GLP-1 analogue sequence covalently joined to the Fc portion of a modified human IgG4 heavy chain by a small peptide linker. The molecule is produced in mammalian (Chinese hamster ovary) cell culture and has an overall molecular weight of approximately 63 kilodaltons.

The label records three deliberate engineering changes: modifications in the GLP-1 region responsible for interaction with dipeptidyl-peptidase-IV, modifications in an area with a potential T-cell epitope, and modifications in the IgG4 Fc regions responsible for binding high-affinity Fc receptors and for half-antibody formation. No amino-acid sequence is published on this page: at this size the sequence display used for short peptides would not be meaningful, and inventing one would be worse than omitting it.

### Clinical Evidence

**REWIND — the distinguishing trial.** REWIND (NCT01394952, PMID 31189511) randomised 9,901 people with type 2 diabetes at 371 sites in 24 countries to dulaglutide or placebo, double-blind, with a median follow-up of 5.4 years. The population is what makes it unusual: participants qualified with either a previous cardiovascular event *or* cardiovascular risk factors, and the median baseline glycated hemoglobin was 7.2%. The post hoc arrhythmia analysis reports that 31% of the trial's participants had previous cardiovascular disease (PMID 34984808), meaning roughly two-thirds of REWIND was a primary-prevention population — the feature that distinguishes it from most cardiovascular outcomes trials in this class. The primary composite of non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 594 participants (12.0%) versus 663 (13.4%), hazard ratio 0.88 (95% CI 0.79-0.99, p=0.026), assessed in the intention-to-treat population. All-cause mortality did not differ significantly (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.80-1.01, p=0.067).

**REWIND's secondary and exploratory analyses.** An exploratory stroke analysis (PMID 31924562) reported 158 strokes on dulaglutide versus 205 on placebo, HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.62-0.94, p=0.010), driven by ischaemic stroke with no effect on haemorrhagic stroke. An exploratory renal analysis (PMID 31189509) reported a composite renal outcome HR of 0.85 (95% CI 0.77-0.93, p=0.0004), clearest for new macroalbuminuria. Both papers describe themselves as exploratory. A separate post hoc analysis (PMID 34984808) found no effect on incident atrial fibrillation or flutter.

**The AWARD program.** AWARD is not one trial but a numbered series with regional and pediatric variants. AWARD-3 (PMID 24842985) tested dulaglutide against metformin as monotherapy in 807 patients. AWARD-5 (PMID 24742660 at 52 weeks, PMID 25912221 at 104 weeks) tested it against sitagliptin in 1,098 metformin-treated patients. AWARD-7 (PMID 29910024) tested it against insulin glargine in 577 patients with stage 3-4 chronic kidney disease — relevant because dulaglutide is not cleared by the kidneys. AWARD-10 (PMID 29483060) added it to an SGLT2 inhibitor in 424 patients. AWARD-11 (NCT03495102, PMID 33397768) compared higher strengths against 1.5 mg in 1,842 patients. AWARD-PEDS (PMID 35658022) studied 154 youths aged 10 to under 18 years.

**Estimand discipline, illustrated.** AWARD-11 is the cleanest example on this site of why the estimand matters. It pre-specified two: the treatment-regimen estimand (regardless of treatment discontinuation or rescue medication) and the efficacy estimand (on treatment, without rescue). Dulaglutide 4.5 mg beat 1.5 mg on both. Dulaglutide 3.0 mg beat 1.5 mg on the efficacy estimand (ETD -0.17 percentage points, P=0.003) but **not** on the treatment-regimen estimand (ETD -0.10 percentage points, P=0.096). Same trial, same comparison, opposite conclusions depending on which estimand is quoted. Figures on this page are the treatment-regimen or intention-to-treat values wherever a paper reports both.

### Where Dulaglutide Loses

This is the most useful section for a reader choosing between agents, and it is not close.

- **Against semaglutide (SUSTAIN 7, PMID 29397376).** Open-label, 1,201 patients, 40 weeks. Semaglutide 1.0 mg reduced bodyweight 6.5 kg versus 3.0 kg for dulaglutide 1.5 mg (estimated treatment difference -3.55 kg, p&lt;0.0001) and glycated hemoglobin 1.8 versus 1.4 percentage points (ETD -0.41, p&lt;0.0001). The same pattern held at the lower strength pair. These are the paper's primary analysis population values — on treatment and before rescue medication, not a treatment-policy estimand.
- **Against tirzepatide, Japan (SURPASS J-mono, PMID 35914543).** Double-blind, 636 patients, 52 weeks, modified intention-to-treat. Tirzepatide reduced bodyweight by 5.8, 8.5 and 10.7 kg across its three strengths versus 0.5 kg for dulaglutide 0.75 mg. Read that comparison with its caveat: the dulaglutide arm was the 0.75 mg strength, not the highest strength the AWARD program later studied.
- **Against tirzepatide, at dulaglutide's own ceiling (SURPASS-SWITCH, PMID 40183678).** Phase 4, 282 adults, 40 weeks. Escalating dulaglutide to 4.5 mg or maximum tolerated gave -0.67% glycated hemoglobin and -3.6 kg; switching to tirzepatide gave -1.44% and -10.5 kg (both P&lt;0.001). This is the comparison that removes the "wrong strength" objection, and dulaglutide still loses. The authors list the open-label design as a limitation.
- **Null and non-significant results within its own trials.** No reduction in atrial fibrillation or flutter (PMID 34984808). No significant reduction in all-cause mortality, and no effect on haemorrhagic stroke (PMID 31189511, PMID 31924562). Sustained eGFR decline and renal replacement therapy were not individually significant in the REWIND renal analysis (PMID 31189509). No effect on BMI in youths (PMID 35658022). Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio reduction not significantly different from insulin glargine in AWARD-7 (PMID 29910024).

### Safety Profile

The FDA label carries a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors. In male and female rats, dulaglutide causes an increase in the incidence of thyroid C-cell tumors (adenomas and carcinomas) after lifetime exposure, related to both the amount given and the duration of treatment. The label states that it is unknown whether Trulicity causes thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma, in humans, because the human relevance of the rodent finding has not been determined. It also notes one case of medullary thyroid carcinoma in a Trulicity clinical trial in a patient whose pretreatment calcitonin was approximately eight times the upper limit of normal, and an additional case of C-cell hyperplasia with elevated calcitonin in REWIND.

Contraindications per the label: personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma, Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2, and serious hypersensitivity reaction to dulaglutide or any product component.

Labelled warnings and precautions beyond the boxed warning: acute pancreatitis, hypoglycemia when combined with an insulin secretagogue or insulin, serious hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis and angioedema, acute kidney injury from volume depletion, severe gastrointestinal reactions (the label states it is not recommended in severe gastroparesis), diabetic retinopathy complications reported in a cardiovascular outcomes trial, acute gallbladder disease, and pulmonary aspiration during general anesthesia or deep sedation. The most common adverse reactions at an incidence of 5% or more are nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, abdominal pain and decreased appetite.

Trial-level corroboration: in REWIND, 47.4% of dulaglutide participants reported a gastrointestinal adverse event during follow-up versus 34.1% on placebo (p&lt;0.0001) (PMID 31189511). In AWARD-7, nausea and diarrhoea were more common than with insulin glargine while symptomatic hypoglycaemia was roughly half as frequent (PMID 29910024).

The label's drug-interaction section notes that because dulaglutide slows gastric emptying it can reduce the rate of absorption of concomitantly administered oral medicines, and advises monitoring drug levels of oral medicines with a narrow therapeutic index such as warfarin.

### Registered But Not Yet Evidence

Registration of a trial is not evidence of efficacy. These are on the register and are named here so readers know what is coming, not what has been shown:

- **SURPASS-CVOT** (NCT04255433) — tirzepatide versus dulaglutide on major cardiovascular events, 13,299 participants, sponsor Eli Lilly, listed as completed with a primary completion date of 12 June 2025. No publication for it appears among the sources verified for this page.
- **ACHIEVE-PEDS** (NCT07668336) — orforglipron versus dulaglutide in 170 pediatric participants with type 2 diabetes, listed as not yet recruiting with a September 2026 start.
- **RADIANT** (NCT07282041) — dulaglutide in severe intracranial atherosclerosis, 130 participants, sponsor National University of Singapore, recruiting, primary completion December 2030. An investigator-initiated trial outside the diabetes indication.
- **AWARD-6** (NCT01624259) — dulaglutide versus liraglutide, 599 participants, completed in 2013. The registration is verified; its publication is not among the sources verified for this page, so no result is quoted here.
- **PIONEER 10** (NCT03015220) — oral semaglutide versus dulaglutide in 458 Japanese patients, completed. Cited elsewhere on this site; its publication is not among the sources verified for this page.

### Important Limitations

- Weight reduction is small relative to semaglutide and tirzepatide in direct comparison (PMID 29397376, PMID 35914543, PMID 40183678).
- REWIND's primary hazard ratio has a 95% CI upper bound of 0.99 — significant, but not far from the null (PMID 31189511).
- The stroke and renal REWIND results are exploratory analyses by their authors' own description, not confirmatory endpoints.
- The AWARD and REWIND programs share a single sponsor. The strongest independent evidence in this dossier is a competitor-sponsored trial in which dulaglutide was the losing arm.
- Two of the three head-to-head trials cited here were open-label.
- Boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors, based on rodent carcinogenicity of undetermined human relevance.
- Not approved for weight management; the approved indications are glycemic control in type 2 diabetes and reduction of major adverse cardiovascular events.
- Gastrointestinal adverse events are common and were the leading reason for discontinuation in SUSTAIN 7.
