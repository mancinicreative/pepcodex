## Why the Fragment Is Not the Protein

The beginner definition is the [TB-500 guide](/guide/what-is-tb-500). The compound record is [TB-500](/peptides/tb-500). This page is the fragment-vs-parent distinction — not a second "what is it" article.

TB-500 is a synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4), a naturally occurring 43-amino-acid protein involved in actin binding, cell migration and wound healing. It is **not approved** by any regulatory agency.

The single most useful fact about TB-500 is that it is not thymosin beta-4. Analytical work characterising the material sold under this name identified it as the **N-terminal acetylated 17–23 fragment** of thymosin beta-4 — seven residues out of forty-three [pmid-22962027]. Nearly every claim made for TB-500 cites research performed on the full protein.

| Fact | Detail |

## TB-500 vs Thymosin Beta-4

| Aspect | TB-500 | Thymosin beta-4 |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Structure | 7-residue acetylated fragment [pmid-22962027] | Full 43-residue protein |
| Human trials | None identified for the fragment | Clinical work in dermal healing [pmid-23050815] |
| Preclinical breadth | Narrow; mostly analytical and doping-control studies | Wound healing, corneal repair, anti-fibrotic, cardiac [pmid-22074294] |
| Regulatory status | Unapproved research chemical | Investigational; not an approved drug either |

The full compound record is in the [TB-500 dossier](/peptides/tb-500). Readers comparing repair-associated peptides may also want the [BPC-157](/peptides/bpc-157) and [LL-37](/peptides/ll-37) dossiers.

## What the Parent Protein Research Shows

Thymosin beta-4 has a substantial and genuinely interesting literature. It was reported to accelerate wound healing in early work [pmid-10469335], and a later review describes accelerated dermal healing in preclinical animal models **and in patients** [pmid-23050815]. A broader review sets out its properties as a multi-functional regenerative peptide across several tissue types [pmid-22074294].

None of that is evidence about TB-500. A seven-residue fragment can retain, lose, or alter the activity of its parent — that is an empirical question, and for this fragment it has not been answered in humans.

## What Has Actually Been Studied on TB-500 Itself

The TB-500 literature is dominated by analytical chemistry rather than efficacy, which tells you what the research community has mostly been interested in: detecting it.

- **Doping control.** Methods have been developed to detect TB-500 in equine urine and plasma by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry [pmid-23084823] — the horse-racing context in which the compound first drew attention.
- **Metabolism.** A 2024 study quantified TB-500 and its metabolites in in-vitro experiments and in rats, and screened them for wound-healing activity in vitro [pmid-38382158]. In-vitro screening is a starting point, not an efficacy result.
- **Animal tendon models.** A 2026 rat study examined BPC-157 and TB-500 in Achilles tendon healing using histopathological and biomechanical endpoints [pmid-42542926]. A rodent tendon model is the closest published analogue to the use TB-500 is marketed for, and it is still a rodent.

A 2026 review in *Sports Medicine* covering approved and unapproved peptide therapies for musculoskeletal injury and athletic performance places TB-500 in that landscape [pmid-41966639] — the useful framing being that the compound sits firmly on the unapproved side.

## The Evidence Problem, Stated Precisely

- TB-500 specifically has **no identified human clinical trials**
- Research on the parent compound cannot be transferred to a seven-residue fragment without testing
- The fragment's metabolism differs from the intact protein [pmid-38382158]
- Most published TB-500 work exists to *detect* the compound, not to evaluate whether it works

This is a common shape in the research-peptide market: a real protein with real science behind it, a fragment sold under a different name, and the citation list of the former attached to the latter.

## Important Warnings

- Products are unregulated with unknown purity and composition
- Safety in humans is not established
- Prohibited in competitive sport, with validated detection methods in place [pmid-23084823]
- Gained public attention through horse-racing doping cases
