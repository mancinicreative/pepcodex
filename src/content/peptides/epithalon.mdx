Epitalon, also called Epithalon, is the synthetic **Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly (AEDG)** tetrapeptide. It is distinct from pineal extracts and from other peptide bioregulators. Its best-cited telomere results come from cultured cells, not from treated people.

### Telomere experiments

A 2003 study reported telomerase activity and telomere elongation after adding AEDG to telomerase-negative human fetal fibroblast cultures. A 2025 independent study reported hTERT/telomerase-associated telomere lengthening in normal cultured human cells and **alternative-lengthening-of-telomeres activity in breast-cancer cell lines**. The latter observation raises a mechanistic safety question; it does not prove an increase in cancer risk in humans. A published correction replaced incorrect Figures 1–3 in the 2025 paper; the corrected figures should be used when appraising its results. Neither study establishes longer lifespan, improved sleep or clinical benefit. [PMID:12937682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12937682/) · [PMID:40908429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40908429/) · [Correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12619744/)

Earlier animal and experimental work on chromatin, pineal-related and antioxidant measures is hypothesis-generating. It cannot be turned into a human anti-aging timeline or a claim that AEDG safely activates telomerase in a whole person. Studies of cultured **human** cells are preclinical studies, not human treatment trials.

### Human evidence and safety boundary

No controlled human efficacy study showing improved lifespan, healthspan or sleep was identified. FDA's compounding review reports insufficient safety-related information for the proposed administration route and cites potential immunogenicity from aggregation and peptide-related impurities. Combination effects with other peptides are unknown. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)
