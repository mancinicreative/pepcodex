## Different clinical status

MOTS-c is a mitochondrial 12S-region peptide examined in metabolic and physical-capacity models. SS-31, also called **elamipretide**, is a synthetic cardiolipin-binding tetrapeptide with indication-specific clinical trials. A product approval for one condition does not validate either peptide for longevity. [MOTS-c discovery, PMID 25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) · [SS-31 mechanism, PMID 23813215](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23813215/)

| Evidence question | MOTS-c | SS-31 / elamipretide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human intervention | Native Phase 2 registration; no cited efficacy or safety result | Published controlled trials in defined diseases |
| US regulatory status | No FDA-approved native MOTS-c medicine verified | **Forzinity** received accelerated approval for Barth syndrome patients weighing at least 30 kg |
| Human-evidence status | Endogenous observations and a native Phase 2 registration without cited results | Forzinity has product-specific accelerated Barth approval, with patient benefit awaiting confirmation; the primary-mitochondrial-myopathy trial missed both primary endpoints |

Human MOTS-c exercise, sleep-apnea and diabetes studies measured endogenous peptide; mouse experiments tested administered MOTS-c. A 2026 C26 mouse/cell cachexia paper found partial preservation of skeletal muscle after preventive exposure, without preventing overall body or fat loss and without a human treatment arm. [PMID 33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/) · [PMID 40538389](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40538389/) · [PMID 40855115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40855115/) · [PMID 42266945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42266945/)

[NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745) registers native MOTS-c for adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity, but no outcome is cited in the accepted review. [NCT03998514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) concerns **CB4211**, a separate analog, and cannot supply native MOTS-c results.

FDA granted **accelerated approval on September 19, 2025** to the specific Forzinity product to improve muscle strength in adult and pediatric Barth syndrome patients weighing at least 30 kg. Its intermediate endpoint was knee-extensor strength and a confirmatory trial is required to verify patient benefit. The 12-person randomized Barth crossover phase missed its two prespecified primary endpoints; FDA says the knee-strength increase was seen in the later open-label extension, not during the randomized phase. The extension and natural-history comparisons have design limits. This is regulatory and study status, not a confirmed general efficacy grade. [FDA announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-first-treatment-barth-syndrome) · [FDA trial snapshot](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-trials-snapshots/drug-trials-snapshots-forzinity) · [Barth trial, PMID 33077895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33077895/)

The 218-person MMPOWER-3 randomized trial in **primary mitochondrial myopathy** missed both primary endpoints at 24 weeks. Thus the Barth approval cannot be generalized to that disease, heart failure, retinal disease, or exercise enhancement. [MMPOWER-3, PMID 37268435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37268435/)

The Forzinity label describes product-specific hypersensitivity and injection-site risks; it does not establish safety of unrelated preparations. FDA says compounded MOTS-c exposure information is insufficient. No head-to-head MOTS-c/elamipretide study is cited. [FDA Forzinity label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/215244Orig1s000Lbl.pdf) · [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

This comparison is for research literacy, not use outside an approved indication.
