Novo Nordisk reported positive results from the REDEFINE 2 phase 3 trial evaluating [CagriSema](/peptides/cagrisema), the company's combination of [cagrilintide](/peptides/cagrilintide) (a long-acting amylin analog) and [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) (a GLP-1 receptor agonist), in adults living with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes. The trial met its primary endpoint, with participants losing 13.7% of body weight at 68 weeks versus 3.4% with placebo.

## What We Know

The REDEFINE 2 trial (NCT05394519) randomised 1,206 adults with a BMI of 27 kg/m² or above, type 2 diabetes and a glycated haemoglobin of 7-10%, in a 3:1 ratio to once-weekly subcutaneous CagriSema (cagrilintide 2.4 mg / semaglutide 2.4 mg) or placebo, both alongside lifestyle intervention, for 68 weeks. The co-primary endpoints were percent change in body weight and the proportion achieving at least 5% weight reduction [redefine-2-trial].

Participants receiving CagriSema lost 13.7% of body weight versus 3.4% with placebo under the treatment-policy estimand — a difference of 10.4 percentage points (95% CI -11.2 to -9.5; P&lt;0.001) [redefine-2-trial]. The PubMed abstract fetched 2026-09-02 does not report a trial-product percent; this page does not quote one. Glycaemic control also improved markedly: 73.5% of the CagriSema group reached an HbA1c of 6.5% or below, compared with 15.9% on placebo [redefine-2-trial].

Note that REDEFINE 2 compared CagriSema against placebo, not against semaglutide monotherapy. The head-to-head against its own components came later: REIMAGINE 2, published 7 June 2026, put CagriSema against semaglutide 2.4 mg and 1.0 mg and against cagrilintide 2.4 mg alone in 2,713 adults with type 2 diabetes over 68 weeks. On the primary comparison, HbA1c fell 1.91 percentage points with CagriSema against 1.75 with semaglutide 2.4 mg — a statistically significant but small margin of 0.16 percentage points (95% CI 0.05 to 0.27) [reimagine2-lancet].

Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most commonly reported side effects, occurring in 72.5% of the CagriSema group versus 34.4% on placebo, and were mostly transient and mild or moderate in severity [redefine-2-trial].

### The Science Behind CagriSema

CagriSema combines two distinct mechanisms targeting appetite and metabolism. Semaglutide, already established as an effective obesity treatment, activates GLP-1 receptors in the brain and pancreas, reducing appetite and improving glucose metabolism.

Cagrilintide is a long-acting analog of amylin, a hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells. Amylin contributes to satiety through effects on the area postrema in the brainstem and slows gastric emptying, complementing GLP-1's effects.

Combining the two mechanisms is intended to engage more of the pathways that regulate appetite and energy balance than either component does alone. This aligns with the emerging understanding that obesity involves multiple regulatory pathways, and targeting multiple mechanisms may be necessary for optimal outcomes.

## What It Means

The REDEFINE 2 results establish CagriSema's efficacy in a population that is typically harder to treat: people with both obesity and type 2 diabetes generally lose less weight on incretin-based therapy than those without diabetes. A 10.4 percentage point placebo-adjusted reduction alongside substantial HbA1c improvement is a clinically meaningful result in that group.

For Novo Nordisk, the results strengthen the company's competitive position against Eli Lilly's retatrutide and other emerging therapies. Having both the current market-leading semaglutide products and a next-generation combination therapy provides strategic flexibility.

The implications for patients are significant. For individuals with type 2 diabetes and severe obesity, a therapy that delivers double-digit weight loss while pushing most participants below an HbA1c of 6.5% addresses two problems at once, and does so with a single weekly injection.

However, CagriSema is not approved as of 17 August 2026. Whether a reviewer can obtain it after a decision is a separate question from what REDEFINE 2 measured.

## What's Next

Novo Nordisk filed a New Drug Application for CagriSema with the FDA on 18 December 2025, on the basis of the REDEFINE programme; a first FDA decision is expected in late 2026 — our [NDA filing coverage](/blog/cagrisema-nda-filed) has the details. As of 17 August 2026, CagriSema is not approved by any regulator.

The diabetes evidence has also widened: three phase 3 trials from the separate REIMAGINE programme published together on 7 June 2026, covering CagriSema in diet-and-exercise-managed diabetes, head-to-head against its own components [reimagine2-lancet], and as an add-on to basal insulin. The REDEFINE cardiovascular outcomes trial is ongoing. For the combination's role in validating the amylin pathway, see our [REDEFINE-1 amylin analysis](/blog/cagrilintide-redefine-1-amylin-validated).

Manufacturing preparations are underway to support commercial launch. Given the supply constraints that have affected semaglutide products, ensuring adequate production capacity will be critical for successful market entry.

The obesity treatment landscape continues to evolve rapidly. Within the next two to three years, healthcare providers and patients may have several options producing double-digit weight loss, changing the approach to obesity management.
