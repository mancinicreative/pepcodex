**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article stated that FDA had
finalized 2025/2026 guidance restricting compounding of BPC-157 and TB-500.
As of 2026-09-01, that specific finalization document was not located on
fda.gov. Claims that treat a 2026 “final guidance” as the source of a
BPC-157/TB-500 ban have been removed.

What *is* on the official pages is Category 2 history plus the 503A statutory
test.

## What 503A actually requires

Under section 503A, state-licensed physicians and pharmacists may compound
using a bulk drug substance only if the substance [fda-503a-bulks]:

1. Complies with an applicable USP or NF monograph (and the USP compounding
   chapter), or
2. Is a component of an FDA-approved drug if no such monograph exists, or
3. Appears on FDA’s 503A bulks list if neither of the first two applies.

Bulk substances must also come with a valid certificate of analysis and be
manufactured by an establishment registered under section 510 [fda-503a-bulks].
FDA’s July 2014 503A compounding guidance describes how those provisions are
applied while list-making and advisory-committee consultation continue
[fda-503a-guidance-2014].

Neither BPC-157 nor TB-500 is placed on the 503A bulks list by the 503A page
fetched on 2026-09-01 [fda-503a-bulks]. This article does not inventory USP
monographs or approved-drug components beyond that page.

## The interim Category 1 / 2 / 3 policy

While FDA evaluates nominated bulks, an interim enforcement policy sorts
nominations [fda-503a-bulks]:

- **Category 1:** nominated with enough information to evaluate; FDA does not
  intend to take action against compounding that meets the guidance conditions.
- **Category 2:** nominated with enough information, but FDA has identified
  significant safety risks. Category 1 discretion does not apply. FDA publishes
  the risk summary and would consider action under general enforcement
  policies.
- **Category 3:** nominated without enough supporting information.

FDA’s January 2025 final guidance, *Interim Policy on Compounding Using Bulk
Drug Substances Under Section 503A*, finalizes the December 2023 draft of the
same title and replaces the January 2017 final [fda-503a-interim-2025]. That
is a real finalized *interim-policy* document. It is not a peptide-by-peptide
restriction order naming BPC-157 or TB-500.

The 503A page also states that FDA does not intend to place substances
nominated on or after 7 January 2025 into these three categories
[fda-503a-bulks].

## What the Category 2 page says about these two peptides

FDA’s Category 2 safety page, fetched 2026-09-01, has two tables
[fda-category-2].

**Current Category 2 table:** BPC-157 and TB-500 are not listed. The live
Category 2 rows as of that fetch include other substances (for example,
cesium chloride, ibutamoren mesylate, kisspeptin-10, and several 503B-only
peptides such as GHRP-2 and GHRP-6).

**“Nominated but withdrawn” table:** the page says these substances were
previously in Category 2 and that nominators withdrew them. Both peptides
appear there with FDA’s recorded concerns [fda-category-2]:

- **BPC-157:** possible immunogenicity for certain routes; peptide-related
  impurities and API-characterization complexity; no or limited
  safety-related information for the proposed routes, so FDA lacks sufficient
  information to know whether the drug would cause harm in humans.
- **Thymosin beta-4 fragment (LKKTETQ), also known as TB-500:** possible
  immunogenicity for certain routes from aggregation and peptide-related
  impurities; no identified human-exposure data; FDA lacks information on
  whether the drug would cause harm in humans.

Those are FDA’s words on a current agency page. They are not a 2026 final
guidance that “removed these substances from the legal compounding pathway.”
Withdrawal from the interim Category 2 grouping is not the same as placement
on the 503A bulks list [fda-503a-bulks] [fda-category-2].

## What the literature on the page actually covers

A 2025 narrative review of BPC-157 for musculoskeletal healing describes a
large preclinical literature and a thin human record [pmc-12446177]. That
supports the “limited human data” half of FDA’s Category 2 note. It is not a
regulatory document.

A 2025 commentary on compounded weight-loss medicines documents a separate
compounding-quality problem in the GLP-1 shortage period [pmc-12164287]. It
does not decide BPC-157 or TB-500 status.

## What we do not know (dated 2026-09-01)

The fetched FDA pages do not state an enforcement calendar for pharmacies that
still handle these substances, do not record a 2025/2026 final guidance
restricted to BPC-157 or TB-500, and do not, on the pages opened this day,
publish a completed 503A-list rulemaking for either peptide [fda-category-2]
[fda-503a-bulks] [fda-503a-interim-2025].

Advisory-committee votes, state-board interpretations, and news reports are
outside this article unless they are those FDA pages.

**Dated absence (scanned 2026-09-01):** no FDA final guidance titled as a
2025 or 2026 restriction of BPC-157 or TB-500 compounding was located.
