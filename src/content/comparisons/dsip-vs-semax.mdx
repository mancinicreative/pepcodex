## Different populations and outcomes

**DSIP (emideltide)** was tested intravenously in small human insomnia studies. Early reports described improved sleep measures in some participants ([PMID 7028502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7028502/); [PMID 3622582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3622582/)). A later crossover study found little clinical significance, and a 16-person placebo-controlled study concluded that short-term DSIP was unlikely to provide a major therapeutic benefit ([PMID 3583493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3583493/); [PMID 1299794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1299794/)). FDA's 2026 review found the intravenous evidence inconclusive and no effectiveness study for the proposed subcutaneous route ([FDA emideltide briefing, pp. 24–31](https://www.fda.gov/media/193344/download)). The evidence for human insomnia benefit is **very low**.

**Semax** was examined in different clinical settings. A 1997 Russian-language report compared 30 people given Semax during acute ischemic-stroke care with 80 receiving conventional care and reported differences in neurological recovery measures; the abstract does not establish a modern randomized efficacy estimate ([PMID 11517472](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11517472/)). A 2018 report followed 110 people during stroke rehabilitation in groups with or without Semax and measured plasma BDNF, motor function, and Barthel index; its group and rehabilitation context matter when interpreting the reported associations ([PMID 29798983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29798983/)). A separate rat study measured hippocampal BDNF/trkB expression after Semax, which is not human evidence of improved memory or sleep ([PMID 16996037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16996037/)).

## Limits and safety context

The cited papers provide no DSIP-versus-Semax trial and no evidence that their combination is safe or beneficial. A hormone-marker study of DSIP in 11 healthy men found lower ACTH-like immunoreactivity but **no change in plasma or urinary cortisol**, so “DSIP reduces cortisol” is inaccurate ([PMID 2554357](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2554357/)). FDA's 2026 emideltide briefing proposed against including its free-base and acetate forms on the 503A bulks list; that proposal is not a final approval decision ([FDA briefing, pp. 52–55](https://www.fda.gov/media/193344/download)). This page makes no current-country approval claim for Semax without a verified primary regulatory record.
