## Different mechanisms and patient outcomes

Tesamorelin is a GHRH analog approved to reduce **excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy**. The pivotal trial assessed VAT by imaging. FDA labeling states it is not for weight-loss management and that long-term cardiovascular safety has not been established. [FDA prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=839334d3-8c1d-4c26-9036-2ab524a6ea75); [Falutz et al., 2007, PMID 18057338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18057338/).

MK-677 (ibutamoren) is a **nonpeptide** ghrelin-receptor agonist. In a randomized trial of 65 healthy older adults, it increased GH, IGF-1 and fat-free mass. The study found **no significant improvement in strength or function and no reduction in abdominal visceral fat**; fasting glucose rose and insulin sensitivity decreased. These results cannot be transferred to tesamorelin's indicated population or called general anti-aging benefit. [Nass et al., 2008, PMID 18981485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18981485/).

FDA's 2024 review says Merck discontinued its MK-0677 program in 1999 **and** describes a later licensed pediatric development program under the LUM-201 name. It therefore does not support a blanket claim that development of ibutamoren has ended. The status of any individual program requires a dated registry check; a compounding assessment does not confer drug approval. [FDA 2024 ibutamoren review](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download).

No cited head-to-head trial establishes relative efficacy or safety, and no evidence here validates combining these agents.
