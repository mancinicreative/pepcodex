## Why Peptide Market Numbers Vary So Much

Published forecasts for "the peptide therapeutics market" differ by tens of billions of dollars for the same year. That is not primarily a disagreement about growth; it is a disagreement about definitions:

- **What counts as a peptide.** Insulin is a peptide hormone. Whether insulin sales are included changes the base by an enormous margin. So does the treatment of peptide-derived and peptide-adjacent modalities such as monoclonal antibodies (usually excluded) and peptide-drug conjugates (usually included).
- **Which GLP-1 products count.** Semaglutide and tirzepatide dominate any peptide market total. Whether a forecast includes the diabetes indication, the obesity indication, or both moves the number dramatically.
- **Gross versus net.** List-price-based totals and net-of-rebate totals differ substantially in the US market in particular.
- **Forecast assumptions.** Coverage decisions for obesity medications, generic entry timing, and supply constraints each swing the out-year projection by a wide margin.

Because of this, a headline forecast figure is close to meaningless without its methodology. Treat any single number quoted without its definition as marketing rather than analysis.

## What Is Structurally True

Several features of the category are not seriously disputed and do not require a proprietary forecast to state:

**Incretin therapies dominate the category by value.** GLP-1 receptor agonists and dual agonists account for the large majority of peptide therapeutic revenue, and the gap between them and every other peptide segment is wide.

**Manufacturing capacity has been a genuine constraint.** Supply shortages of GLP-1 products were widely reported and acknowledged by manufacturers, and both Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk publicly announced large capital investments in production capacity. The direction is well documented even where specific capacity figures are not.

**Oncology is the most active area of non-metabolic peptide development.** Peptide-drug conjugates, radiolabeled peptides for theranostics, and peptide-based cancer vaccines are all in clinical development, though from a much smaller commercial base.

**Oral delivery is the pivotal technical variable.** Peptides are conventionally injected. The commercial ceiling for the category depends substantially on how well oral formulations perform, since patient preference and adherence differ sharply between injection and tablet.

## How to Read a Market Projection

If you encounter a peptide market forecast, three questions determine whether it is worth anything:

1. **Does it state whether insulin is included?** If not, the number is uninterpretable.
2. **Does it separate metabolic from non-metabolic peptides?** A blended growth rate mostly reflects GLP-1 dynamics and says little about the rest.
3. **Is the source retrievable?** A named report you can actually locate is worth more than an impressive figure attributed to "a leading industry research firm."
