## Mechanism is not an outcome

MK-677 (ibutamoren) is an **oral, nonpeptide** ghrelin-receptor agonist. Ipamorelin is a **pentapeptide** growth hormone secretagogue. The original ipamorelin selectivity study used rat pituitary cells and animal models, including swine; those results cannot establish a cleaner clinical safety profile or human muscle benefit. [FDA ibutamoren review](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download); [Raun et al., PMID 9849822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9849822/).

Ipamorelin also has **human hormone-response data**. A randomized, placebo-controlled pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic study enrolled 48 healthy men and measured plasma ipamorelin and GH after intravenous infusion. It characterized GH release, not treatment of growth hormone deficiency, body composition or functional benefit. [Gobburu et al., PMID 10496658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10496658/); [FDA ipamorelin assessment](https://www.fda.gov/media/182088/download).

| Functional outcome | No significant strength/function improvement detected in one older-adult trial; trial underpowered for this endpoint | Postoperative meal-tolerance endpoint did not improve significantly; muscle/function benefit was not tested |
| FDA approval | No approved ibutamoren product | No approved ipamorelin product identified |

The MK-677 older-adult trial included **65 healthy adults**. GH, IGF-1 and fat-free mass increased, but abdominal visceral fat, strength and function did not improve significantly. The trial's size and duration were insufficient to assess functional endpoints definitively. Fasting glucose increased and insulin sensitivity decreased. These are population-specific results, not a direct comparison with ipamorelin. [Nass et al., PMID 18981485](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18981485/).

Ipamorelin was also tested in a **randomized, double-blind Phase 2 human trial** for postoperative ileus after bowel resection ([NCT00672074](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00672074)). Of **117 enrolled patients, 114** were included in the safety and modified intent-to-treat populations. Median time to tolerating a solid meal was 25.3 hours with intravenous ipamorelin and 32.6 hours with placebo (**p = 0.15**); the primary and secondary efficacy analyses found no significant difference. This small proof-of-concept trial assessed gastrointestinal recovery, not body composition, strength or anti-aging benefit. [Beck et al., PMID 25331030](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25331030/).

FDA's 2024 assessment identifies **ibutamoren/MK-677/LUM-201** as the same molecule and no FDA-approved product. FDA separately states that ipamorelin is not a component of an approved drug. The earlier Merck ibutamoren program ended, yet pediatric LUM-201 development continued; [NCT06948214](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06948214) was marked recruiting in the September 29, 2026 official registry archive. These facts do not establish approval or efficacy for aging, muscle gain or recovery. [FDA ibutamoren assessment](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download); [FDA ipamorelin assessment](https://www.fda.gov/media/182088/download).

There is no cited head-to-head trial supporting a clinical winner or combination. This page does not make a use recommendation.
