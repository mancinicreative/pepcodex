figures have been removed. This version was rebuilt on named compounds with published papers.

Peptide-drug conjugates (PDCs) are a targeted-delivery approach with potential advantages
over antibody-drug conjugates, chiefly better tumour penetration and simpler manufacturing.
Whether those advantages convert into clinical benefit is unsettled as of 2026-08-17.

## What We Know

### The Architecture

A peptide-drug conjugate has three components:

**Targeting peptide** — a short sequence, typically 10–20 amino acids, that binds a receptor
or surface structure enriched on tumour cells.

**Linker** — chemistry designed to stay intact in circulation and release the payload at or
inside the target cell.

**Cytotoxic payload** — an agent too potent to administer systemically on its own.

The comparison class is well characterised. A reference treatment of typical antibody-drug
conjugates sets out the antibody format's own architecture and constraints [adc-reference],
and a 2026 review traces how the conjugate field has developed beyond the antibody format
[conjugate-journey].

### Clinical Status — Named Compounds Only

Two bicyclic peptide-drug conjugates have published first-in-human results.
[BT5528](/peptides/bt5528), targeting EphA2 with a monomethyl auristatin E payload, has a
published phase I dose-escalation study in advanced solid tumours [bt5528-phase1].
[Zelenectide pevedotin](/peptides/zelenectide-pevedotin), targeting nectin-4, has a published
phase I/II dose escalation and expansion study [zelenectide-phase1].

Separately, radiolabelled [somatostatin analogues](/peptides/octreotide) are established in
neuroendocrine tumour care. They deliver a radioisotope rather than a cytotoxic drug, but
they are the existing proof that peptide-directed delivery works in humans.

PepCodex does not publish per-tumour-type response rates, disease control rates, or adverse
event percentages for "the PDC platform," because there is no single platform trial those
numbers could come from. Any efficacy figure for a peptide-drug conjugate should be tied to a
named compound and a specific NCT number before it is relied on.

### Where PDCs Differ From ADCs

**Size and penetration.** A targeting peptide is roughly 1–3 kDa against an antibody's
roughly 150 kDa. Smaller agents distribute into tumour tissue more readily, which is the
central mechanistic argument for the format [conjugate-journey].

**Manufacturing.** Peptides are made by chemical synthesis; antibodies require biologic
production. Synthesis is the more straightforward and more scalable of the two.

**Immunogenicity.** Short synthetic peptides generally provoke less of an immune response
against the carrier than a full antibody does.

**[Half-life](/glossary/half-life).** This one runs the other way. Peptides clear in hours;
antibodies persist for days to weeks. Short exposure is the format's main pharmacological
disadvantage and drives its dosing schedules.

## What It Means

The honest summary is that the mechanistic case for PDCs is coherent and the clinical case is
young.

**Target breadth.** Many tumours lack a surface antigen suitable for an antibody. Peptide
targets may extend targeted delivery to some of them — an argument, not yet a result.

**Later-line setting.** Both published PDC studies enrolled heavily pretreated patients.
That is where new agents start; it is not evidence of where they will end up.

**Neuroendocrine precedent.** Somatostatin receptor targeting remains the best-established
peptide-targeting approach in oncology, and it is approved.

PepCodex does not publish a target-and-stage table for PDC programmes. Stated targets for
individual compounds are frequently reported incorrectly in secondary coverage, and each
should be confirmed against the sponsor's own materials or the compound's ClinicalTrials.gov
record.

## What's Next

The directions under active investigation are bi-specific targeting peptides, payloads beyond
traditional cytotoxics such as immunomodulators and targeted protein degraders, conditional
activation confined to tumour-microenvironment conditions, and radiopeptide theranostics
[conjugate-journey]. A separate briefing covers
[peptide-based targeted protein degraders](/blog/peptide-protacs-emerge-cancer-treatment).

The questions that will decide the class are unchanged: which targets balance tumour
selectivity against expression breadth, how resistance emerges, where PDCs sit in a treatment
sequence, and whether a biomarker can select patients. None has an answer supported by a
randomised [clinical trial](/glossary/clinical-trial) as of 2026-08-17. For the CBX-12
programme specifically and what could not be verified about it, see
[the PDC oncology digest](/blog/peptide-drug-conjugates-cbx-12-oncology).
