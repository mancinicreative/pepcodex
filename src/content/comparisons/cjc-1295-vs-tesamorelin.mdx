## What the evidence compares

Both compounds act through the growth hormone-releasing hormone pathway, but shared mechanism does not make their clinical evidence interchangeable. Tesamorelin has an FDA-approved indication for **reducing excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV and lipodystrophy**. Its pivotal randomized trial measured visceral adipose tissue (VAT) by imaging. The label says it is **not indicated for weight-loss management**, and long-term cardiovascular safety is unestablished. [FDA prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=839334d3-8c1d-4c26-9036-2ab524a6ea75); [Falutz et al., 2007, PMID 18057338](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18057338/).

CJC-1295 was studied in two short randomized, placebo-controlled ascending-dose studies in **healthy adults**. Growth hormone and IGF-1 rose, but these are hormone biomarkers, not evidence of a patient benefit or an approved indication. The investigators reported no serious adverse reactions in those small, brief studies; that does not establish long-term safety. [Teichman et al., 2006, PMID 16352683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683/).

FDA's CJC-1295 safety review describes limited clinical data and reports of increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction with compounded products. A compounding review is **not** a drug approval. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks).

No cited trial directly compares the two compounds or validates their combined use. Relative hormone changes cannot establish relative clinical efficacy or safety.
