A randomized, double-blind, multicentre trial published in The Lancet HIV demonstrated that [tesamorelin](/peptides/tesamorelin), a synthetic growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog, significantly reduces liver fat content in people living with HIV who have developed non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). The findings expand the evidence supporting tesamorelin's role in managing metabolic complications of HIV treatment.

## What We Know

The trial randomized 61 participants with HIV on stable antiretroviral therapy and documented hepatic steatosis to daily subcutaneous tesamorelin 2 mg or placebo for 12 months, followed by a 6-month open-label phase [tesamorelin-nafld-trial].

At 12 months, participants receiving tesamorelin showed an approximately 37% relative reduction in hepatic fat fraction (an absolute effect size of -4.1% versus placebo). A hepatic fat fraction below 5% was reached by 35% of tesamorelin recipients versus 4% of placebo recipients.

Secondary outcomes also favored tesamorelin, including less progression of fibrosis. HIV viral suppression was maintained, with no evidence of adverse interaction with antiretroviral therapy [tesamorelin-nafld-trial].

### HIV and Metabolic Disease

People living with HIV face elevated risks of metabolic complications including lipodystrophy, insulin resistance, and fatty liver disease. While modern antiretroviral regimens are metabolically more favorable than older treatments, these issues persist and contribute to liver disease, cardiovascular disease, and reduced quality of life.

Tesamorelin is currently approved for reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy, on the basis of two multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trials whose pooled analysis covered 806 antiretroviral-treated patients [tesamorelin-phase3-pooled]. In those trials the effect was selective: visceral adipose tissue fell while subcutaneous fat was largely unaffected [tesamorelin-drugs-review]. The liver-fat study extends evidence to a related but distinct metabolic abnormality.

The proposed mechanism involves restoration of physiologic growth hormone pulsatility. GHRH stimulates the pituitary to release the body's own growth hormone in a pattern mimicking natural secretion, and growth hormone promotes lipolysis — the same secretagogue logic behind [sermorelin](/peptides/sermorelin), though tesamorelin is the only member of the class with an FDA approval.

## What It Means

For HIV patients with NAFLD, tesamorelin offers a treatment option specifically studied in their population. General NAFLD treatments may not account for the unique metabolic perturbations associated with HIV and its treatment; tesamorelin has demonstrated efficacy in this specific context.

The finding also has implications for the broader NAFLD/NASH field. If tesamorelin's liver effects translate to non-HIV populations, the drug could have wider application. However, HIV-specific factors may contribute to the observed benefits, and generalization requires caution.

Clinical implementation faces practical considerations. Tesamorelin requires daily injections and has a high cost, limiting accessibility. Insurance coverage varies, and prior authorization requirements can delay treatment initiation.

The relationship between liver fat reduction and clinical outcomes remains under investigation. While reducing hepatic steatosis is associated with reduced progression to cirrhosis and liver cancer, the trial did not run long enough to demonstrate these outcomes directly.

## What's Next

Longer-term follow-up would be needed to examine whether liver fat reduction translates to prevented progression of liver disease; the published trial was not long enough to answer that question.

Combination approaches are a logical area for future research. GLP-1 agonists have also shown benefit for fatty liver disease, and whether adding tesamorelin provides additive benefit remains untested.

Tesamorelin's US labelling remains limited to reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy; it is not approved for NAFLD.

Two later developments are worth recording. A phase 4 tesamorelin study (NCT03226821) was terminated with the stated reason "lack of availability of the study drug", with 6 participants enrolled — a supply problem, not a safety or efficacy finding, and it should be read as neither [nct03226821]. And the TRIUMPH trial is examining tesamorelin as an adjunct to exercise for physical function in people with HIV; its protocol paper appeared in *BMJ Open* in July 2026 and contains no results [triumph-protocol].

For the GH secretagogue field more broadly, the liver-fat results support continued investigation of this mechanism, though each related compound requires independent evaluation.
