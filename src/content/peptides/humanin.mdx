Humanin is a 24-amino-acid peptide first identified in cell models of Alzheimer-related toxicity. **Native humanin, the stronger S14G-humanin/HNG analogue and colivelin are distinct research interventions.** Findings from one should not be silently credited to another.

### Mechanism and intervention studies

In cell experiments, humanin suppressed apoptosis induced by Alzheimer-related insults. Guo and colleagues later showed interaction with BAX and reduced BAX movement to mitochondria, with knockdown experiments supporting a role for endogenous humanin in that model. These are mechanistic findings, not evidence of neuroprotection in patients. [PMID:11717357](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11717357/) · [PMID:12732850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12732850/)

Animal work includes studies of native humanin and modified analogues. A 2020 study reported healthspan-related readouts after HNG treatment in middle-aged mice, alongside correlations of endogenous humanin with longevity-related phenotypes in animals and higher levels in children of centenarians. The mouse intervention and human observational findings answer different questions. They do not show that administering humanin extends human life. [PMID:32575074](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32575074/)

### Human associations and clinical gap

Human studies have measured circulating or muscle humanin after disease, aging or exercise exposures. Their directions vary by population: one study reported positive correlations with age in healthy subjects, and a hemodialysis cohort reported a U-shaped association with adverse outcomes. Such biomarker associations cannot establish a therapeutic effect from giving humanin. No controlled human treatment trial or approved humanin product was identified; administration safety and peptide-combination effects remain unknown. [PMID:33131010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33131010/) · [PMID:39102184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39102184/)
