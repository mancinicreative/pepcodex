Pemvidutide is a GLP-1/glucagon receptor dual agonist in development for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). Its Phase 2b trial, IMPACT, has been published, and the results are worth reading precisely — because the headline endpoint and the fibrosis endpoint pointed in different directions.

**Pemvidutide is not approved by the FDA, the EMA, or any other regulator.** It remains investigational.

## The IMPACT Trial

IMPACT screened 1,557 patients and randomised 212 across 83 sites in the United States and Australia. Participants received once-weekly subcutaneous pemvidutide at 1.2 mg or 1.8 mg, or placebo. The published results report the 24-week outcomes from a planned 48-week study [impact-trial-results].

### MASH Resolution Without Worsening of Fibrosis

| Group | Achieved endpoint |
|---|---|
| Placebo | 18/86 (20%) |
| Pemvidutide 1.2 mg | 24/41 (58%) |
| Pemvidutide 1.8 mg | 45/85 (52%) |

Both doses were statistically significant versus placebo (p&lt;0.0001) [impact-trial-results].

### Fibrosis Improvement Without Worsening of MASH

| Group | Achieved endpoint | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Placebo | 24/86 (28%) | — |
| Pemvidutide 1.2 mg | 13/41 (33%) | 0.59 |
| Pemvidutide 1.8 mg | 30/85 (36%) | 0.27 |

**Neither dose significantly improved fibrosis at this timepoint** [impact-trial-results].

This distinction matters more than it might appear. Fibrosis stage is the histological feature most strongly linked to long-term liver outcomes. A drug that resolves steatohepatitis without a demonstrated effect on fibrosis has shown something real but incomplete.

Note also that the lower 1.2 mg dose produced the numerically higher MASH resolution rate, in a small subgroup — a pattern that argues for caution in reading dose-response into these numbers.

### Safety

Adverse events were reported in 78% of the 1.2 mg group, 81% of the 1.8 mg group and 67% of placebo, predominantly mild to moderate. Discontinuations due to adverse events were minimal: none in the 1.2 mg group, one in the 1.8 mg group, and two on placebo [impact-trial-results].

## The Dual Agonist Mechanism

Pemvidutide activates both GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, which distinguishes it from pure GLP-1 agonists such as semaglutide. Earlier work in MASLD describes the rationale: the glucagon component acts directly on the liver to stimulate fatty acid oxidation and inhibit lipogenesis, offering a route to reducing liver fat that does not depend solely on weight loss [mash-market-2025].

In that earlier study, at 12 weeks the 1.8 mg dose produced liver fat content reductions of at least 30% in 94.4% of patients and at least 50% in 72.2%, with 55.6% normalising liver fat content, against 4.4% for placebo. Weight loss at that dose and timepoint was −4.3% [mash-market-2025].

The balance between GLP-1 and glucagon activity has to be tuned: excess glucagon signalling risks raising glucose, while the GLP-1 component counteracts that.

This differs from tirzepatide, which pairs GLP-1 with GIP rather than glucagon.

## What It Means

MASH has historically lacked effective pharmacotherapy, and a mechanism that targets hepatic lipid handling directly is a genuinely different approach from weight-loss-mediated benefit alone.

But the appropriate reading of IMPACT is a positive Phase 2b on the resolution endpoint, without a demonstrated fibrosis benefit at 24 weeks, in 212 patients. That is a reason to run a larger and longer trial — which is what is happening — not a basis for treatment decisions.

For patients with MASH, pemvidutide is available only through clinical trials. Treatment decisions should be made with a hepatologist, taking into account approved options and evidence-based lifestyle intervention.
