FOXO4-DRI is an experimental peptide designed to interfere with the interaction between FOXO4 and p53. Its evidence concerns biochemical experiments, cells, cultured tissue and animal models. **Evidence for a treatment benefit in people remains very low:** none of the publications included in this September 5, 2026 review establishes safety or efficacy after FOXO4-DRI administration to people.

### Molecular identity and mechanism

Baar and colleagues published the peptide's sequence in 2017. Their reported D-isoform construct has 46 residues and a molecular weight of 5,358.2 Da. The sequence shown above refers to that published experimental construct. The original study found competition with FOXO4 for p53 binding, p53 movement out of the nucleus, and caspase-dependent death in senescent fibroblasts. Its selectivity comparisons used particular cell models and experimental conditions; they do not establish that every healthy cell is unaffected. [PMID:28340339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28340339/)

A 2025 structural study identified the disordered p53 transactivation domain as a binding site for FOXO4-DRI. Both the FOXO4-derived region and the cell-penetrating tag contributed to the interaction. These biochemical findings refine the proposed mechanism; they do not establish clinical benefit or a completely resolved downstream pathway in every tissue. [PMID:40593617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40593617/)

### What the experimental studies found

| Research setting | Reported finding | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Fast-aging and naturally aged mice | The original study reported improved fitness, fur density and renal-function measures. [PMID:28340339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28340339/) | Animal outcomes do not establish longer life or improved health in people. |
| Human chondrocytes expanded outside the body | Senescent-cell removal did not improve the cells' chondrogenic potential. [PMID:33996787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33996787/) | Selective cell killing did not establish the intended functional benefit in this experiment. |
| Cultured keloid tissue and fibroblasts | FOXO4-DRI promoted apoptosis alongside nuclear exclusion of phosphorylated p53. [PMID:39994346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39994346/) | This was tissue and cell research, not treatment of people with keloids. |
| Leydig-cell experiments and naturally aged mice | Separate papers reported effects on testosterone secretion and sperm quality or spermatogenesis. [PMID:31959736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31959736/), [PMID:39025385](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39025385/) | Human tissue observations in the earlier paper were separate from peptide intervention experiments. Neither establishes a human fertility benefit. |
| Bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis in mice | Two publications reported reduced fibrotic changes or collagen deposition. [PMID:35510614](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35510614/), [PMID:37074394](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37074394/) | These disease-model results are not clinical evidence for pulmonary fibrosis. |
| Mouse models of vascular aging | A paper published electronically in January 2026 reported improved aortic function after FOXO4-DRI exposure. [PMID:41625068](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41625068/) | Aortic findings cannot be generalized to every vascular condition. |

A 2024 correction to the first bleomycin paper reports that Figure 6B duplicated/misplaced a FACS panel: PBS + FOXO4-DRI data had been placed in the TGF-beta + FOXO4-DRI group. The authors state that the scientific conclusion is unchanged; this review does not independently validate the corrected results. [Correction, DOI:10.1111/jcmm.18502](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcmm.18502), [PMID:39166551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39166551/)

The correction notice is disclosed separately and excluded from the 13 research/review publications counted above.

### A pulmonary vascular counter-signal

A separate study found unfavorable pulmonary hemodynamic changes and loss of pulmonary endothelial cells in mouse models exposed to FOXO4-DRI or another senolytic. The paper also examined other interventions and a rat model; its monocrotaline rat experiment used ABT263. These results show why favorable findings in one setting cannot establish that senescent-cell clearance is beneficial in another. Human lung observations in this publication were not a human FOXO4-DRI intervention. [PMID:36515093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36515093/)

### Findings included in the September 2026 update

An older bronchopulmonary dysplasia paper was retrieved because its PubMed record was revised in June 2026. It was indexed in 2023 and appeared in print in 2024. Its human component examined autopsy lungs; FOXO4-DRI was administered to hyperoxia-exposed rat pups. The abstract reports improved alveolar complexity and partial preservation of type 2 alveolar cells. This is not evidence of treatment benefit in human infants. [PMID:37874230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37874230/)

A July 2026 glioblastoma paper examined AGK, mitochondrial stress and temozolomide resistance. The abstract reports FOXO4-DRI effects in cell experiments. Its analyses of patient AGK expression, senescence and prognosis are observational and do not establish that patients received the peptide or benefited from it. [PMID:42391447](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391447/)

A July 2026 narrative review distinguishes direct FOXO4 evidence from findings about other FOXO proteins. It describes FOXO4-DRI as preclinical and identifies pharmacokinetics, delivery and long-term toxicology as translation gaps. It is a review, not another intervention experiment. [PMID:42510573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42510573/)

### Evidence boundaries

The source inventory contains 13 unique publications, assigned once to a primary category: one paper with patient biomarker/prognosis observations, 11 preclinical papers and one review. The patient-observation paper also includes cell experiments. Human tissue studies remain in the preclinical category; they are not studies administering the peptide to people. None in this inventory reports administering FOXO4-DRI to people. Publication counts are not counts of independent trials or a complete literature census.

The original and structural papers were reviewed in full text, and the earlier Leydig paper's human-tissue methods were checked in its published full text. Other findings above were checked against PubMed abstracts; detailed methods, effect estimates and risk of bias were not comprehensively assessed. The June 7 to September 5, 2026 discovery window included publication, indexing and record-modification dates, so a match in that window does not necessarily mean a new study.

The reviewed sources do not establish human pharmacokinetics, clinical safety, or safety of combinations with other peptides. Numerical evidence and effectiveness scores are withheld pending a source-backed reassessment. This page summarizes research and does not provide medical advice.
