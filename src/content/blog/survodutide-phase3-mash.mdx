**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this briefing reported Phase 3 efficacy results for survodutide in MASH from a trial called "ACHIEVE." No such survodutide trial exists, and those figures were removed. A subsequent version correctly stated that no phase 3 results existed — which was true at the time. That has now changed: survodutide's first phase 3 liver readout published in June 2026, and this article reports it.

## The First Phase 3 Result: SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD

SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD, published in Nature Medicine on 7 June 2026, is the first phase 3 trial of [survodutide](/peptides/survodutide) to report ([PMID 42252333](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42252333/); NCT06309992). It enrolled 216 adults with obesity and at-risk MASLD — defined by liver inflammation or fibrosis on non-invasive tests, or biopsy-confirmed MASH — and randomised them 2:1 to once-weekly subcutaneous survodutide 6.0 mg (n=146) or placebo (n=70) for 48 weeks. Both co-primary endpoints were met [synchronize-masld].

The paper reports each co-primary endpoint under two estimands, and the difference between them is large:

| Co-primary endpoint at week 48 | Treatment-regimen estimand | Efficacy estimand |
|---|---|---|
| ≥30% reduction in MRI-PDFF liver fat | **68.5%** vs 28.6% placebo | 84.2% vs 24.3% placebo |
| Mean change in body weight | **−8.7%** vs −1.4% placebo | −12.2% vs −1.0% placebo |

The treatment-regimen figures lead here. That estimand keeps every randomised participant in the analysis, including those who stopped treatment, so it describes the result of being assigned the drug. The efficacy estimand describes only what happened while participants remained on treatment, which is why its numbers are larger. Both are reported in the paper; neither is wrong, but they answer different questions and are not interchangeable [synchronize-masld].

Note what this trial is and is not: it used imaging-based liver-fat reduction and weight change as co-primary endpoints in an at-risk MASLD population. It is not a biopsy-endpoint MASH fibrosis trial — that remains the job of the larger LIVERAGE trials registered under the same programme, targeting approximately 1,800 participants with moderate or advanced fibrosis [liverage-phase3] and approximately 1,590 with cirrhosis [liverage-cirrhosis]. As of 17 August 2026, no biopsy-endpoint phase 3 results for survodutide are in the verified registry.

## The Obesity Result Landed Too

The same week, SYNCHRONIZE-1 — the phase 3 obesity trial ([PMID 42253238](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42253238/); NCT06066515) — published in the New England Journal of Medicine. In 725 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes over 76 weeks, mean body weight fell 12.2% at 3.6 mg and 13.0% at 6.0 mg versus 5.4% with placebo, on the treatment-regimen estimand, which the paper names as its primary analysis [synchronize-1-obesity].

## What Phase 2 Showed

The biopsy-based MASH evidence remains the Phase 2 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine. It randomized 293 patients with biopsy-confirmed MASH and stage F1-F3 fibrosis to once-weekly subcutaneous survodutide at 2.4 mg, 4.8 mg, or 6.0 mg, or placebo, over 48 weeks [survodutide-phase2].

**MASH improvement with no worsening of fibrosis:** 47% (2.4 mg), 62% (4.8 mg), and 43% (6.0 mg) versus 14% with placebo.

**Fibrosis improvement by at least one stage:** 34%, 36%, and 34% across the three strengths versus 22% with placebo.

**Liver fat reduction of at least 30%:** 63%, 67%, and 57% versus 14% with placebo.

Gastrointestinal adverse events were considerably more frequent with survodutide than placebo (nausea 66% vs. 23%; diarrhoea 49% vs. 23%; vomiting 41% vs. 4%). Serious adverse events were similar between groups (8% vs. 7%).

## The Glucagon Rationale

Survodutide's differentiated mechanism is its dual agonism. Unlike GLP-1-only agonists, survodutide also activates glucagon receptors, which are expressed in the liver. The hypothesis is that glucagon receptor activation contributes directly to hepatic lipid metabolism, in addition to the indirect benefit that follows weight loss.

SYNCHRONIZE-MASLD is consistent with that hypothesis at the level of liver fat and weight. Whether the dual mechanism produces durable *histological* benefit — fibrosis improvement on biopsy — and whether that translates into fewer liver-related clinical events is what the LIVERAGE trials are designed to test. The same mechanism is being pursued by [pemvidutide](/peptides/pemvidutide), whose phase 2b MASH results we cover in our [IMPACT analysis](/blog/pemvidutide-impact-mash-resolution).

## What to Watch

- Completion and readout of the biopsy-endpoint LIVERAGE and LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis trials
- Whether fibrosis improvement, which was modest relative to placebo in Phase 2, holds up in a larger population
- Long-term tolerability, given the high rate of gastrointestinal adverse events in Phase 2

Cross-trial comparisons against resmetirom, [semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide), or [tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) remain unreliable: MASH trials differ in fibrosis stage at entry, biopsy reading methodology, and endpoint definition.
