    somatostatin (grade B/C fistula 22.9% vs 24.1%, P=0.73, intention-to-treat).

    question — whether either helps — unanswered.
  - >-
    Registration is not evidence. Two Novartis phase 3 extension studies of
    octreotide acetate microspheres in diabetic retinopathy (NCT00248131,
    NCT00248157) were terminated, and a lanreotide-versus-Sandostatin LAR
    carcinoid comparison (NCT00092287) was terminated with 4 patients enrolled.
    None of these produced usable efficacy evidence, and their existence in a
    registry should not be read as support for those uses.
  - >-
    The four approved formulations are not interchangeable and their evidence
    does not transfer. Mycapssa is approved only for maintenance in patients
    already controlled on octreotide or lanreotide, the LAR depot only in
    patients already shown to tolerate the immediate-release form, and CAM2029
    (ACROINNOVA 1) is an investigational subcutaneous depot rather than any
    approved product. Trial results attach to a specific formulation.
  - >-
    Biliary toxicity is common rather than rare. The Sandostatin label reports a
    63% incidence of biliary tract abnormalities in clinical trials (27%
    gallstones, 24% sludge without stones, 12% biliary duct dilatation), rising

    The compound is on-target for glucose and thyroid disturbance. Because it
    inhibits insulin, glucagon and growth hormone together, the labels warn that
    either hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia can result, and TSH suppression can
    cause hypothyroidism.

    Much of the gastrointestinal-bleeding evidence pools octreotide with native
    somatostatin. The 1997 non-variceal meta-analysis explicitly combined the
    two agents, so the octreotide-specific effect size cannot be separated from

Octreotide is the compound most other somatostatin analogues are measured against. It has been approved in the United States since 1988, which means the evidence base is unusually long — and unusually mixed. Several of the largest, best-run trials involving octreotide are trials it lost.

### How It Works (Simplified)

Octreotide is a synthetic cyclic octapeptide that mimics the natural hormone somatostatin, but resists breakdown long enough to be useful as a medicine. Two of its eight residues are D-amino acids and a disulfide bridge closes the ring — both features slow enzymatic degradation.

### Four Products, Not One Drug

This is the single easiest thing to get wrong about octreotide. The United States has four distinct approved product families, each a separate FDA application with its own approval date and its own indication wording, plus an investigational fifth formulation now in phase 3.

| Product | Application | Earliest approval | Approved for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sandostatin, immediate-release | NDA 019667 (Novartis) | 21 Oct 1988 | Acromegaly; carcinoid diarrhea/flushing; VIPoma diarrhea |
| Sandostatin LAR Depot | NDA 021008 (Novartis) | 25 Nov 1998 | Same three, but only in patients already shown to respond to and tolerate the immediate-release form |
| Mycapssa, oral capsules | NDA 208232 (Chiesi) | 26 Jun 2020 | Acromegaly maintenance only, in patients already responding to octreotide or lanreotide |
| Bynfezia Pen | NDA 213224 (Sun Pharmaceutical) | 27 Sep 2024 | Acromegaly; carcinoid diarrhea/flushing; VIPoma diarrhea |
| CAM2029, subcutaneous depot | investigational | — | Not approved; tested in ACROINNOVA 1 and POSITANO |

More than a dozen generic applications also exist, from Fresenius Kabi, Mylan, Teva, Gland, Heritage, Meitheal and others, spanning 2005 to 2025. A result obtained with one of these products does not automatically describe another: the LAR depot and the oral capsule differ in absorption, in the population they are approved for, and in the trials that produced them.

### Clinical Evidence

**Neuroendocrine tumors — the strongest positive result.** The PROMID trial randomized treatment-naive patients with well-differentiated metastatic midgut neuroendocrine tumors to octreotide LAR or placebo. Median time to tumor progression was 14.3 months versus 6.0 (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.20 to 0.59; P=.000072). The reported analysis was a planned interim look at 67 progressions and 16 deaths among 85 patients. [PMID:19704057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19704057/)

A post hoc quality-of-life analysis of the same cohort found longer time to definitive deterioration for fatigue (18.5 vs 6.8 months, p=0.0006), pain and insomnia. [PMID:30852564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30852564/)

**Acromegaly.** The approved indication rests on the biochemical claim in the label: octreotide substantially reduces GH and IGF-1. The most recent randomized acromegaly data in this review are from ACROINNOVA 1, a 24-week phase 3 trial in 72 patients across 10 countries — but of **CAM2029**, a subcutaneous octreotide depot using FluidCrystal technology, not of Sandostatin LAR. In the intention-to-treat analysis, 72.2% versus 37.5% reached IGF-1 at or below the upper limit of normal (risk difference 34.6, 95% CI 11.3 to 57.9; P=.0018), with participants whose amount was reduced counted as non-responders. [PMID:39378125](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39378125/)

**Gastrointestinal bleeding.** A meta-analysis of randomized trials in acute esophageal variceal hemorrhage favoured octreotide over all alternatives combined (RR 0.63, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.77) and over vasopressin/terlipressin (RR 0.58, 95% CI 0.42 to 0.81), with efficacy comparable to immediate sclerotherapy. No alternative therapy showed a mortality benefit. [PMID:11231948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11231948/) A 2026 Thai multicenter trial in 220 cirrhotic patients then showed that one day of continuous infusion was non-inferior to five days after successful band ligation. [PMID:41090987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41090987/)

**Angiodysplasia-related anemia.** The OCEAN trial randomized 62 transfusion-dependent patients across 17 Dutch hospitals to octreotide LAR or standard of care for one year. Total transfusion units were 11.0 versus 21.2, a mean reduction of 10.2 units (95% CI 2.4 to 18.1; P=.012), in the intention-to-treat analysis. Open-label, single-country, and small. [PMID:38158089](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38158089/)

**Polycystic liver disease (POSITANO).** A second CAM2029 trial, POSITANO (NCT05281328), tested the subcutaneous depot against placebo in 71 patients with symptomatic polycystic liver disease. Its results were first posted to ClinicalTrials.gov on 1 June 2026, and the study is listed as active, not recruiting. On the posted primary outcome, height-adjusted total liver volume changed by -0.7% (95% CI -2.9 to 1.6) with CAM2029 against +3.9% (95% CI 0.1 to 7.7) with placebo. A dateless PubMed search on 17 August 2026 for the registration number, the trial name and the compound-plus-indication returned no publication of this trial, so those figures come from the registry posting rather than a peer-reviewed report — and the confidence interval on the CAM2029 arm crosses zero. As with ACROINNOVA 1, this is CAM2029 — an investigational formulation, not any approved octreotide product — and polycystic liver disease is not an approved indication for any of them.

### Where the Evidence Is Negative or Null

A dossier that lists only supportive trials would misrepresent this compound. These results are as real as the ones above.

- **Beaten in modern neuroendocrine oncology.** In NETTER-2, the higher-strength octreotide LAR control group had median progression-free survival of 8.5 months against 22.8 months for 177Lu-Dotatate plus octreotide (stratified HR 0.276, 95% CI 0.182 to 0.418; P&lt;0.0001). [PMID:38851203](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38851203/) In the phase 3 XT-XTR008-3-01 trial the octreotide control group had median progression-free survival of 5.8 months and an objective response rate of 1.0%, versus 43.4% for the radioligand. [PMID:41111031](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41111031/)
- **NETTER-1's survival endpoint was not met — in either direction.** At final analysis, median overall survival was 48.0 months with 177Lu-Dotatate versus 36.3 months in the octreotide comparator group (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.17; two-sided p=0.30). The comparison failed statistically; that is a null result about the radioligand and tells us nothing positive about octreotide either. [PMID:34793718](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34793718/)
- **Acute pancreatitis: no benefit.** In 58 patients with moderate or severe acute pancreatitis randomized against placebo, complications occurred in 54% versus 40% and mortality was 18% versus 20%. The authors concluded octreotide was not of benefit. [PMID:9127169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9127169/)
- **Hepatorenal syndrome: inferior.** Renal function recovered in 70.4% (19/27) of patients given terlipressin plus albumin versus 28.6% (6/21) given midodrine and octreotide plus albumin (P=0.01). [PMID:25644760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25644760/)
- **Pancreatic fistula prevention: no difference.** PREFIPS randomized 651 patients after pancreatectomy; clinically significant grade B/C fistula occurred in 22.9% of the octreotide arm and 24.1% of the somatostatin arm (P=0.73, intention-to-treat); the absence of a difference persisted after adjustment for the stratification variables. [PMID:38662619](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38662619/)
- **A combination that missed its primary endpoint.** AXINET added axitinib or placebo to octreotide LAR in 256 patients with advanced extrapancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. Investigator-assessed progression-free survival, the primary endpoint, was 17.2 versus 13.1 months (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.65 to 1.15) — not met, although blinded central review was positive (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.54 to 0.94, P=.017). [PMID:41719493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41719493/)
- **Registration is not evidence.** Two Novartis phase 3 diabetic-retinopathy extension studies of octreotide acetate microspheres were terminated (NCT00248131, 260 enrolled; NCT00248157, 105 enrolled), and a comparison of lanreotide Autogel against Sandostatin LAR Depot in carcinoid syndrome was terminated with 4 patients enrolled (NCT00092287). Registry entries in these indications should not be read as support for them.

### Safety Profile

No octreotide product carries a boxed warning. The labels share a consistent set of warnings and precautions, verified against the current openFDA drug labels on 6 August 2026.

- **Gallbladder.** Octreotide inhibits gallbladder contractility and decreases bile secretion. The Sandostatin label reports a 63% incidence of biliary tract abnormalities in clinical trials (27% gallstones, 24% sludge without stones, 12% biliary duct dilatation), with 52% developing stones or sludge among those treated 12 months or longer. Postmarketing reports include cholecystitis, cholangitis, pancreatitis and cholecystectomy.
- **Glucose.** Because it shifts the balance between insulin, glucagon and growth hormone, either hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia can occur. The Mycapssa label reports increased blood glucose in 7%, hypoglycemia in 4% and diabetes mellitus in 1% of its trial participants.
- **Thyroid.** TSH suppression can cause hypothyroidism; the labels recommend baseline and periodic thyroid assessment during chronic therapy.
- **Cardiac.** Bradycardia, arrhythmias and conduction abnormalities are described. The Sandostatin label reports bradycardia below 50 bpm in 25% of acromegalic patients, conduction abnormalities in 10% and arrhythmias in 9%, and warns of higher-degree atrioventricular block with intravenous use — complete atrioventricular block has been reported postmarketing during surgical procedures.
- **Gastrointestinal and nutritional.** New-onset steatorrhea, stool discoloration, loose stools, bloating and weight loss may signal pancreatic exocrine insufficiency. Decreased vitamin B12 levels and abnormal Schilling's tests have been observed; the Mycapssa label recommends monitoring B12.
- **Contraindications differ by product.** The Sandostatin LAR Depot label lists none. The immediate-release, Bynfezia and Mycapssa labels contraindicate hypersensitivity to octreotide or product components; the Mycapssa label notes anaphylactoid reactions including anaphylactic shock have been reported.

### How This Page Relates to Pasireotide

The [pasireotide](/peptides/pasireotide) dossier describes octreotide as the first-generation comparator pasireotide was designed to improve on, and reports pasireotide binding SSTR1 and SSTR5 with 30–40 fold higher affinity. Nothing in the octreotide evidence reviewed here contradicts that framing. Note that the two dossiers are built on different citation sets: the pasireotide page carries its own verified sources for the head-to-head acromegaly comparisons, and those studies are not re-cited here.

### Important Limitations

- Approval age is not the same as strength of effect — several of the largest modern trials involving octreotide are ones it lost as the control arm
- Trial results attach to a specific formulation; the immediate-release, LAR depot, oral and pen products are not interchangeable, and CAM2029 is not approved at all
- The FDA labels state explicitly that effects on tumor size, growth rate and metastasis have not been determined in carcinoid syndrome and VIPomas
- PROMID's survival analysis was described by its own authors as non-confirmatory
- Biliary abnormalities affect a majority of long-term recipients
- Several off-label uses reviewed here (variceal bleeding, angiodysplasia, hepatorenal syndrome, pancreatic fistula) are not approved indications, and two of them produced null or inferior results

This page is an evidence review, not medical guidance. It does not describe how any product is used, and nothing here should be read as a recommendation.
