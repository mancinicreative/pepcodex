## How Subcutaneous Injection Works

1. **Needle penetrates skin** - Short, thin needle (typically 27-31 gauge)
2. **Enters subcutaneous tissue** - Fat layer beneath dermis
3. **Medication deposited** - Creates a depot in fatty tissue
4. **Gradual absorption** - Enters bloodstream via capillaries

## Common Injection Sites

| Site | Advantages | Considerations |
|------|------------|----------------|
| Abdomen | Easy access, consistent absorption | Absorption differs near the navel |
| Thigh (outer) | Good for self-injection | May have variable absorption |
| Upper arm (back) | Less nerve endings | May need assistance |
| Buttocks (upper outer) | Large area | Harder to self-inject |

## Subcutaneous vs Intramuscular

| Feature | Subcutaneous (SC) | Intramuscular (IM) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Depth | 1/4" - 5/8" | 1" - 1.5" |
| Tissue | Fat layer | Muscle tissue |
| Absorption | Slower, sustained | Faster |
| Pain | Generally less | Can be more |
| Needle gauge | 27-31 (thin) | 21-25 (thicker) |

## Why Technique Is Not Described Here

This entry previously carried a numbered step-by-step injection procedure. It has been removed.

Administration instructions are outside what this site publishes, for two reasons. The first is
editorial: PepCodex documents what the evidence shows about a compound, and a procedure is not an
evidence claim — there is no study to cite for "insert at 45 degrees". The second matters more.
Technique is specific to the product, the device and the person. Every approved injectable carries
an Instructions for Use written and tested for that particular presentation, and a generic
procedure read off a reference page can conflict with it.

For any approved medicine, the Instructions for Use supplied with the product and the prescribing
clinician are the sources for how it is given. This page describes what the subcutaneous route
**is** and why peptides tend to use it.

## Why Peptides Use Subcutaneous Route

### Advantages
- Bypasses digestive system (peptides survive)
- High bioavailability (70-100%)
- Relatively consistent absorption
- Self-administration possible
- Less painful than IM

### Considerations
- Requires injection (barrier for some)
- Site reactions possible
- Storage/handling requirements
- Need for proper technique

## Injection Site Rotation

Site rotation is a standing feature of subcutaneous product instructions because repeated injection
into one location changes the tissue:
- Lipohypertrophy (fat buildup) and lipoatrophy (fat loss) both develop at over-used sites
- Those tissue changes alter how much drug is absorbed there
- Absorption therefore becomes less consistent over time

The spacing and pattern a given product calls for are stated in its Instructions for Use, not here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does subcutaneous injection hurt?

Modern insulin needles are very thin (29-31 gauge) and short. Most people describe the sensation as a brief pinch. Proper technique and rotating sites minimizes discomfort.

### What determines how deep a subcutaneous injection goes?

By definition the subcutaneous route deposits a drug in the fat layer beneath the skin rather than
in muscle, which is what distinguishes it from an intramuscular injection and gives it a slower,
steadier absorption profile.

Needle length and angle are what a product's Instructions for Use specifies to achieve that, and
they differ by presentation and by body habitus. This page does not state them; the Instructions for
Use supplied with a given product does.

### Why does site rotation come up so often?

Repeated injection into one location can cause lipohypertrophy or lipoatrophy — localised changes
in the fat tissue — and those changes alter how much drug is absorbed from that site. That is why
site rotation appears in the Instructions for Use of essentially every subcutaneous product.
