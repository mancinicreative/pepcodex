## What each study question was

**DSIP (emideltide)** was studied mainly for sleep. Small intravenous studies in people with insomnia reported some improvements ([PMID 7028502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7028502/); [PMID 3622582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3622582/)). Later placebo-controlled studies found effects that were weak or of little clinical significance ([PMID 3583493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3583493/); [PMID 1299794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1299794/)). FDA's 2026 review called the human intravenous evidence inconclusive and found no effectiveness study for the proposed subcutaneous route ([FDA emideltide briefing, pp. 24–31](https://www.fda.gov/media/193344/download)). The evidence for a human insomnia benefit is **very low**.

**Selank** was studied for anxiety-related symptoms in a Russian-language report. In one randomized comparative study, 30 participants received Selank and 32 received medazepam; the 62 participants had generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia. The authors reported similar anxiolytic effects on their measures, but this active-comparator study was small and did not answer whether Selank treats insomnia ([PMID 18454096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18454096/)). Its results cannot be translated into a head-to-head ranking against DSIP.

| Reader question | DSIP evidence | Selank evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep in people with insomnia | Small intravenous studies, with conflicting results and uncertain clinical benefit | The Selank anxiety study did not test this outcome |
| Anxiety-related symptoms | The cited DSIP sleep studies do not establish an anxiety treatment effect | One small randomized comparison with medazepam in a mixed anxiety/neurasthenia group |
| Using both together | No combination result in these cited studies | No combination result in these cited studies |

## What the evidence cannot settle

The compounds were studied in different populations for different outcomes. A publication count, mechanism hypothesis, or response in a different condition cannot rank their clinical effectiveness. The reviewed studies also provide no basis for calling a DSIP–Selank combination compatible or safe. FDA's 2026 emideltide briefing is a regulatory **proposal and evidence assessment**, not a final approval decision; it proposed against adding emideltide free base and acetate to the 503A bulks list ([FDA briefing, pp. 52–55](https://www.fda.gov/media/193344/download)). This comparison makes no current-country approval claim for Selank because no primary regulatory record was verified for that claim.
