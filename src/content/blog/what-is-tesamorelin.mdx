## What the Approved Indication Covers — and What It Does Not

The beginner definition is the [Tesamorelin guide](/guide/what-is-tesamorelin). The compound record is [tesamorelin](/peptides/tesamorelin). Safety tables live on [tesamorelin safety](/safety/tesamorelin-safety). This page is the label-vs-off-label split — not a second "what is it" article.

Tesamorelin (brand name Egrifta) is an FDA-approved medication that stimulates the body's own growth hormone release. It is approved specifically for reducing excess abdominal fat in people with HIV-associated lipodystrophy.

That approval makes it unusual on this site. Most peptides covered here are investigational or unapproved; tesamorelin cleared two phase 3 trials and carries a label. Understanding exactly what that label covers — and what it does not — is the most useful thing to take from this page. The full compound record is in the [tesamorelin dossier](/peptides/tesamorelin).

## Key Facts

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **FDA approved** | Yes (2010) |
| **Brand names** | Egrifta, Egrifta SV |
| **Indication** | Reduction of excess abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy |
| **Class** | GHRH (growth hormone-releasing hormone) analogue |
| **Molecule** | Synthetic analogue of human GHRH(1-44) [pmid-20101189] |

## How Does It Work?

Tesamorelin is an analogue of growth hormone-releasing hormone. It acts on the pituitary to stimulate synthesis and release of **endogenous** growth hormone [pmid-21668043], rather than supplying growth hormone directly.

That distinction matters pharmacologically. Because release remains pituitary-mediated, it stays subject to the body's own feedback regulation, which is a different exposure profile from administering recombinant growth hormone. It is also why tesamorelin is grouped with [sermorelin](/peptides/sermorelin) and [CJC-1295](/peptides/cjc-1295) as secretagogues rather than with growth hormone itself.

## What the Trials Actually Measured

Two multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 studies form the basis of the approval, and their design is worth knowing.

| Study | Design | Participants | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 3 trial with safety extension [pmid-20101189] | 12 months, randomised 2:1 | 404 HIV patients with excess abdominal fat | 6-month primary efficacy phase, then a 6-month extension |
| Pooled analysis of two phase 3 trials [pmid-20554713] | 26-week randomised phase plus 26-week safety extension | **806** ART-treated HIV patients, randomised 2:1 | Re-randomisation at week 26 to continue or switch to placebo |

The pooled analysis is the more informative of the two, both for its size and for its re-randomisation design: patients initially on tesamorelin were re-assigned at week 26 either to continue or to move to placebo [pmid-20554713]. That structure directly addresses what happens when treatment stops — a question most obesity and body-composition trials leave open.

**The key efficacy finding, and its limit.** Tesamorelin reduced **visceral** adipose tissue but did not affect **subcutaneous** adipose tissue to a clinically significant extent across the two 26-week trials [pmid-21668043]. The effect is selective for the fat compartment that drives the clinical problem, and it is not a general fat-loss effect.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis with GRADE assessment revisited the randomised evidence in this population, searching to February 2026 [pmid-42538058]. It is the current synthesis-level reference for the indication.

## What It Is Not Approved For

The label covers HIV-associated lipodystrophy. It does not cover:

- General weight loss or body recomposition
- Anti-aging or longevity use
- Athletic performance
- Visceral fat reduction in people without HIV

Tesamorelin appears frequently in discussions of growth-hormone-axis peptides for performance and aesthetics. Those uses are outside the approved indication and outside the trial populations described above — the participants in every pivotal trial were people receiving antiretroviral therapy with excess abdominal fat.

## Where Research Is Going

Two developments are worth recording, because both correct impressions that a 2010-vintage summary would leave.

**A phase 4 study was terminated for supply reasons.** Registry record NCT03226821 shows the study terminated with the stated reason "lack of availability of the study drug", with 6 participants enrolled and results posted [nct03226821]. That is a supply problem, not a safety or efficacy finding, and it should not be read as either.

**A new trial is underway on a different question.** TRIUMPH is examining tesamorelin as an adjunct to exercise for improving physical function in people with HIV; its design paper appeared in *BMJ Open* in July 2026 [pmid-42419889]. A design paper records an ongoing trial — it contains no results, and should never be cited as evidence of effect.

## Common Side Effects

Reported in the trial programme:

- Injection site reactions
- Joint pain
- Swelling (fluid retention)
- Numbness or tingling

Fluid retention and joint symptoms are characteristic of growth-hormone-axis stimulation generally, not unique to this compound.
