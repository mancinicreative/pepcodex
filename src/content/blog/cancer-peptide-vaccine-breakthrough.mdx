Personalized neoantigen cancer vaccines are frequently described in peptide-industry coverage as a peptide success story. The specific product that holds FDA breakthrough therapy designation in melanoma is **not** a synthetic peptide vaccine: it is mRNA-4157/V940 (intismeran autogene), an mRNA-encoded individualised neoantigen therapy from Moderna and Merck. The distinction matters when reading claims about what peptide vaccines have achieved.

## What We Know

On 22 February 2023, the FDA granted breakthrough therapy designation to mRNA-4157/V940 in combination with pembrolizumab (KEYTRUDA) for the adjuvant treatment of patients with high-risk melanoma following complete resection [fda-breakthrough-2025].

The designation rested on the randomised phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 trial, which enrolled 157 patients with resected stage III/IV melanoma and assigned them to the vaccine plus pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab alone. The combination reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 44% compared with pembrolizumab monotherapy [neoantigen-trial].

### How It Works

The development process begins with sequencing both tumor tissue and normal tissue from each patient. Computational algorithms identify mutations present only in cancer cells and predict which resulting peptide fragments (neoantigens) will be most effectively presented to the immune system.

Here the two platforms diverge. A **peptide** vaccine synthesises the selected neoantigen sequences directly as peptides — typically 15-30 amino acids — and formulates them with an adjuvant. An **mRNA** vaccine instead encodes the neoantigens in a single synthetic mRNA, which the patient's own cells translate. mRNA-4157 takes the second route: one mRNA molecule encoding up to 34 patient-specific neoantigens. In both cases the aim is a T cell response against the patient's tumor while sparing normal tissue [nature-cancer-vaccines].

This personalized approach contrasts with traditional cancer vaccines that target shared tumor antigens. By focusing on neoantigens unique to each tumor, the risk of immune tolerance and autoimmune side effects is theoretically reduced.

### What this means for peptide vaccines

Synthetic peptide neoantigen vaccines are an active clinical field, but as of this writing none holds FDA breakthrough therapy designation in melanoma. Claims that "a peptide cancer vaccine received breakthrough designation" generally trace back to the mRNA-4157 announcement and should be treated with care.

## What It Means

The breakthrough designation accelerates the path toward potential FDA approval, which could come within the next two to three years if ongoing trials continue showing benefit. For melanoma patients at high risk of recurrence after surgery, an additional treatment option that works synergistically with existing immunotherapies represents meaningful progress.

The broader implications extend beyond melanoma. Personalized neoantigen approaches — both mRNA and peptide — are under investigation across other tumor types. Success in melanoma would validate the individualised-neoantigen concept and likely accelerate development timelines elsewhere.

Manufacturing complexity remains a significant challenge for any individualised product. Each dose must be produced for one patient, requiring sequencing, neoantigen selection, synthesis and quality control before the first administration — a timeline that has to compress substantially for the approach to work in rapidly progressing cancers.

Cost is another consideration. Personalized manufacturing inherently lacks the economies of scale of traditional pharmaceuticals, raising questions about accessibility and insurance coverage that neither platform has yet answered.

## What's Next

The programme has moved into phase 3 (the INTerpath studies) in resected high-risk melanoma and other tumour types. Those trials, not the phase 2b data, will determine whether the approach reaches approval.

Parallel work is exploring earlier-stage disease and combinations with other immunotherapy modalities, along with faster manufacturing, including AI-driven neoantigen prediction.

For readers of a peptide reference site, the useful takeaway is a definitional one: individualised neoantigen therapy is a *target-selection* strategy that can be delivered as peptides, as mRNA, or by other means. The regulatory milestone described here belongs to the mRNA implementation.
