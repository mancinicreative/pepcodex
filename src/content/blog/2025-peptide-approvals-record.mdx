Peptide therapeutics have moved from a niche modality to a routine one at the FDA, and the trend line matters more than any single year's tally. This piece looks at the structural reasons for that shift and the candidates positioned to extend it.

**A note on counting.** An earlier version of this article stated that the FDA approved 12 peptide-based therapeutics in 2025, surpassing a previous record of 9 in 2023, and provided a breakdown by therapeutic area. Neither the totals nor the breakdown could be traced to a published source — the FDA's novel drug approvals list does not classify approvals by modality, so any such count is a derived figure that depends on how "peptide" is defined. Those numbers have been removed.

## What We Know

### Where peptides are reaching approval

Rather than a count, the useful observation is which therapeutic areas peptides now routinely reach:

**Metabolic diseases:** The GLP-1 and incretin class remains the centre of gravity, with new formulations, combinations and indication expansions.

**Oncology:** Peptide-drug conjugates and radioligand therapies continue to gain traction.

**Rare diseases:** Orphan designations facilitate approvals for peptides addressing conditions with few alternatives.

**Cardiovascular and infectious disease:** Smaller but active areas for peptide-based agents.

### Driving Factors

Several factors underpin the growth in peptide approvals [drug-development-trends]:

**Platform maturation:** Peptide synthesis, formulation, and delivery technologies have advanced significantly, enabling more reliable drug development.

**Regulatory experience:** Increased FDA familiarity with peptide therapeutics has streamlined review processes and clarified development pathways.

**Investment growth:** Venture capital and pharmaceutical company investment in peptide programs has increased substantially, funding larger clinical development programs.

**Clinical success:** High success rates in clinical trials for peptide candidates have encouraged continued investment.

**Manufacturing scale-up:** Expanded manufacturing capacity has enabled larger trials and commercial launches.

## What It Means

### For the Industry

A steady flow of approvals validates continued investment in peptide drug development. Pharmaceutical companies and biotechnology firms are likely to expand peptide portfolios on that basis. Key implications include:

- Increased competition in established peptide therapeutic areas
- Greater interest in novel peptide targets and mechanisms
- Continued investment in peptide platform technologies
- Expansion of peptide-focused companies and research programs

### For Patients

More approved peptide therapeutics mean more treatment options across multiple conditions, including some therapeutic areas that have been underserved. Access and affordability remain challenges for many new peptide therapeutics.

### For Drug Development

Peptides carry some structural advantages as a modality:

**Target selectivity:** Peptides often achieve high selectivity for their targets, reducing off-target effects.

**Predictable pharmacology:** Peptide mechanisms based on endogenous pathways often have predictable effects.

**Established development paths:** Regulatory pathways for peptide therapeutics are well-characterized.

**Manufacturing advances:** Modern peptide synthesis enables high-quality, scalable production.

## What's Next

### 2026 Pipeline

Notable candidates in late-stage development include:

- [Retatrutide](/peptides/retatrutide/) for obesity (phase 3 completion expected)
- [Survodutide](/peptides/survodutide/) for MASH and obesity
- [Orforglipron](/peptides/orforglipron/) and other oral GLP-1 agonists
- Next-generation peptide-drug conjugates
- Novel antimicrobial peptides

### Emerging Trends

Several trends are likely to shape peptide approvals in coming years:

**Multi-agonist peptides:** Following tirzepatide's success, dual and triple agonists are advancing across multiple targets.

**Oral peptides:** Improved oral bioavailability technologies are enabling peptide delivery without injection.

**Long-acting formulations:** Extended-release formulations are reducing dosing frequency and improving convenience.

**Peptide combinations:** Fixed-dose combinations are being developed to enhance efficacy.

**Novel targets:** Peptide approaches are being applied to previously undruggable targets.

Peptide therapeutics have moved decisively out of niche status. The reliable signal is not any single year's approval count but the breadth of therapeutic areas in which peptides now reach registration, and the depth of the late-stage pipeline behind them.
