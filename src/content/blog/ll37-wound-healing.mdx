LL-37 is frequently described as a wound-healing peptide. The underlying biology is real and well characterised — and, unlike most peptides discussed this way, it has been tested in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled human trial that has published.

The result is positive on its primary tissue endpoint and small enough that it settles very little. Both facts belong in the summary.

## What Is LL-37?

LL-37 is the only human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, a 37-amino-acid peptide cleaved from its precursor protein hCAP18 [ll37-structures-review]. It plays multiple roles in innate immunity. The full compound record is in the [LL-37 dossier](/peptides/ll-37); the other repair-associated peptides discussed alongside it are [BPC-157](/peptides/bpc-157) and [TB-500](/peptides/tb-500). The antimicrobial-peptide pair already on this site is [LL-37 vs lactoferricin](/compare/ll-37-vs-lactoferricin).

**Antimicrobial Activity:**
- Kills bacteria by membrane disruption
- Active against gram-positive and gram-negative organisms
- Antifungal properties
- Antiviral effects against some pathogens

**Immunomodulation:**
- Recruits immune cells to infection sites
- Modulates inflammatory responses

**Tissue Repair:**
- Effects on epithelial cell migration and proliferation
- Involvement in angiogenesis and tissue remodelling

This combination of properties is why LL-37 has been investigated for wound healing applications.

## The Diabetic Wound Problem

Diabetic foot ulcers represent a major clinical challenge:

- **Prevalence:** 15-25% of diabetics develop foot ulcers
- **Healing failure:** 30-40% fail to heal with standard care
- **Infection risk:** Compromised immunity increases infection
- **Amputation:** Leading cause of non-traumatic amputation
- **Mortality:** 5-year mortality after amputation approaches 50%

Current treatments (debridement, offloading, dressings) have limited efficacy, and new approaches are needed [chronic-wound-treatment].

## What Has Actually Been Studied in Humans

One interventional trial of topical LL-37 in diabetic foot ulcers is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov: **NCT04098562**, *Efficacy of LL-37 Cream on Bacteria Colonization, Inflammation Response and Healing Rate of Diabetic Foot Ulcers*, sponsored by Fakultas Kedokteran Universitas Indonesia, phase 2, 40 participants [ll37-diabetic-wounds]. **No results have been posted to the registry.**

The registry is not the whole story. A **randomised, double-blind, controlled trial** of LL-37 cream in diabetic foot ulcer published in *Archives of Dermatological Research* in 2023, from the same institution [ll37-dfu-rct]. This is the human evidence, and it deserves reading closely rather than summarising as "it works".

**What the trial did.** Conducted in Jakarta from January 2020 to June 2021, in patients with diabetic foot ulcer and mild infection. Participants applied LL-37 cream or placebo cream twice weekly for four weeks. Wounds were measured on days 7, 14, 21 and 28 and analysed with ImageJ; LL-37, IL-1α and TNF-α were measured in wound fluid by ELISA; aerobic bacterial colonisation was counted from culture isolates [ll37-dfu-rct].

**What it found.** Baseline LL-37 levels in the ulcers were equally low in both arms — 1.07 ng/mg protein (range 0.37–4.96) in the LL-37 group and 1.11 (0.24–2.09) in placebo — which establishes that the wounds were genuinely deficient in the peptide being supplied. The **granulation index increased more in the LL-37 group at every timepoint**: days 7, 14, 21 and 28, with p = 0.031, 0.009, 0.006 and lower [ll37-dfu-rct].

**What that is and is not.** Granulation index is a tissue-formation measure, not wound closure, and not amputation avoidance. The trial is single-centre, small, four weeks long, and enrolled only mildly infected ulcers. A consistent p-value across four sequential measurements on the same patients is not four independent confirmations — the measurements are correlated by construction.

So: a real randomised result, in the right direction, on an intermediate endpoint, in a study too small to change practice. That is a meaningfully better position than most peptides in this category occupy, and it is not evidence of clinical benefit.

## Mechanism: What the Preclinical Work Shows

The most concrete recent mechanistic evidence in the diabetic context comes from a mouse study. LL-37 accelerated wound healing in diabetic mice by activating TFEB-dependent autophagy in keratinocytes under hyperglycaemic conditions, with increased expression of the autophagy-related proteins ATG5, ATG7 and beclin 1 [ll37-mechanisms-review].

This is a coherent mechanistic story — it proposes how an antimicrobial peptide could act on the host cell repair programme rather than only on bacteria. It is also a mouse study, and the gap between accelerated healing in diabetic mice and closure rates in human ulcers is exactly the gap that trials exist to test.

### Why LL-37 Is a Plausible Candidate

Several features make LL-37 attractive for this indication in principle:

- **Dual action:** antimicrobial activity plus effects on host repair pathways, where most wound therapies address only one
- **Human origin:** an endogenous human peptide, which may limit immunogenicity concerns
- **Topical route:** local application to the wound bed rather than systemic exposure

None of these are efficacy findings. They are reasons the hypothesis is worth testing.

## What This Means

LL-37 sits one step further along than most peptides discussed for tissue repair: strong mechanistic rationale, real preclinical data, and a published randomised human trial with a positive result on a tissue endpoint [ll37-dfu-rct].

It is still not a treatment option. One small single-centre trial on granulation index does not establish that ulcers close faster, that amputations are avoided, or that the effect replicates elsewhere. Diabetic foot ulcers carry serious consequences, and the evidence-based interventions that exist — debridement, offloading, infection control and specialist wound care — remain the standard for good reason.

The wider literature is moving toward LL-37 analogues rather than the peptide itself. A 2026 review discusses ceragenins — non-peptide mimics of antimicrobial peptides that resist proteolysis, carry anti-biofilm activity and remain stable across environmental conditions [ll37-ceragenins-review]. Proteolytic instability is LL-37's central formulation problem, and the analogue route is the field's answer to it.

For readers evaluating claims about LL-37 products, the useful question remains which human study any quoted number comes from. There is now exactly one, it measured granulation rather than healing, and it enrolled a few dozen people at a single centre in Jakarta.
