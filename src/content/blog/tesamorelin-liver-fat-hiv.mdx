## What did the study test?

The 2019 randomized, double-blind study enrolled **61 adults with HIV and fatty liver**; 30 received tesamorelin and 30 received placebo. Its primary endpoint was change in hepatic fat fraction measured with proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy over 12 months. This was a liver-fat study in a specific population. ([Stanley et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31611038/))

## Results and their limits

Tesamorelin reduced hepatic fat fraction by **4.1 percentage points more than placebo** (95% CI, 0.7 to 7.6 points greater reduction). At 12 months, 35% of the tesamorelin group versus 4% of the placebo group had hepatic fat fraction below 5%. Glucose measures did not differ significantly between groups at that time point. These results do not establish improved survival, prevention of liver failure or long-term safety. ([Trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31611038/))

**Evidence grade: low for the measured liver-fat endpoint in this HIV population.** The small study does not establish a broadly effective treatment for fatty liver, and its authors called for further study of long-term liver histology. ([Trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31611038/))

## How this differs from the approved use

The FDA indication concerns excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy. It is not a fatty-liver indication or general weight-loss approval. The label also identifies glucose intolerance, elevated IGF-1 and other risks; long-term cardiovascular safety remains unestablished. ([FDA prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/022505s020lbl.pdf), [current label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=839334d3-8c1d-4c26-9036-2ab524a6ea75))
