Tirzepatide is a "twincretin" - the first dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist designed to harness the full incretin effect.

The [current Mounjaro label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=d2d7da5d-ad07-4228-955f-cf7e355c8cc0) includes type 2 diabetes in patients aged 10 years and older, based in part on the 99-participant SURPASS-PEDS trial ([PMID:40975112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40975112/)). The [Zepbound label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b&version=38) covers adult weight reduction and moderate-to-severe OSA in adults with obesity; pediatric safety and effectiveness for Zepbound have not been established. These indications are not interchangeable.

### SURPASS-CVOT — noninferior to dulaglutide, not superior

SURPASS-CVOT is the cardiovascular outcomes trial for tirzepatide, and it was published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 18 December 2025 ([PMID:41406444](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41406444/), NCT04255433). It was an active-comparator-controlled, double-blind **noninferiority** trial: 13,299 adults with type 2 diabetes and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease were randomised 1:1 to weekly tirzepatide or dulaglutide 1.5 mg, an agent already shown to reduce cardiovascular events. The modified intention-to-treat population was 6,586 versus 6,579 after 134 exclusions. Mean age was 64.1 years, mean HbA1c 8.4%, and mean diabetes duration 14.7 years.

The primary endpoint — a composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction or stroke — occurred in **801 patients (12.2%) on tirzepatide and 862 (13.1%) on dulaglutide, hazard ratio 0.92 (95.3% CI 0.83 to 1.01)**. The prespecified noninferiority margin was an upper confidence limit of 1.05, which was met (P=0.003). Superiority required an upper limit below 1.00, and that was **not** achieved (P=0.09).

The distinction carries the whole result. Tirzepatide was shown not to be meaningfully worse than an agent with proven cardiovascular benefit; it was not shown to be better. Adverse events were broadly similar between groups, with more gastrointestinal events on tirzepatide.

The trial's registry results were separately posted to ClinicalTrials.gov on 8 July 2026, and add detail the abstract does not carry: all-cause death in 566 versus 669 participants, cardiovascular death in 367 versus 408, and at 36 months HbA1c down 1.66 percentage points versus 0.88 and body weight down 11.6% versus 4.8%. A cardiorenal post hoc analysis was published in JAMA Cardiology in June 2026 ([PMID:41903177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903177/)).

The active-comparator result does not establish superiority to placebo or confer a US cardiovascular risk-reduction indication on Mounjaro or Zepbound. The [Zepbound label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b&version=38) also warns that delayed gastric emptying can alter oral-drug absorption. It specifically notes reduced efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives and recommends a non-oral or added barrier method for four weeks after initiation and after each dose escalation; the same issue is in the Mounjaro label. This is a labeled interaction, not a result inferred from a different peptide's mechanism.

WADA's [2026 Monitoring Program](https://www.wada-ama.org/sites/default/files/2025-09/2026_list_monitoring_program_en_final_clean_september_2025.pdf) includes markers of tirzepatide and semaglutide in and out of competition. The program explicitly monitors substances outside the Prohibited List; inclusion is not prohibition.

### Important Limitations

- SURPASS-CVOT established noninferiority to dulaglutide, not superiority (P=0.09 for superiority) — it does not show that tirzepatide reduces cardiovascular events more than an existing GLP-1 receptor agonist
- A 2026 meta-analysis of eight studies found no statistically significant MACE reduction for tirzepatide against GLP-1 receptor agonists (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.70-1.04), with heterogeneity of 90.4% and seven of the eight studies observational
- **Gastrointestinal side effects** remain common (nausea 12-18%, diarrhea 12-17%), especially during titration
- **Thyroid C-cell tumor warning** (boxed warning based on rodent studies; clinical relevance in humans uncertain)
- **Supply constraints** have affected availability since launch; demand continues to exceed supply
- **Cost and insurance coverage** may limit access for many patients
- **Weight regain occurs** after discontinuation (SURMOUNT-4 demonstrated this clearly)
- **Not studied in combination** with other GLP-1 receptor agonists; these should not be combined
- **Contraindicated** in patients with personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2
- **Oral hormonal contraceptives:** Zepbound and Mounjaro labels identify reduced efficacy after initiation and dose escalation because of delayed gastric emptying.
