**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article attributed biopsy-confirmed MASH results — including a 74% MASH resolution rate — to the MOMENTUM trial, and included cross-trial resolution rates for competing drugs that could not be traced to verified sources. MOMENTUM is pemvidutide's phase 2 *obesity* trial; the MASH evidence comes from the IMPACT phase 2b trial. The figures below reflect the verified record.

The FDA has granted Breakthrough Therapy Designation (BTD) to pemvidutide for the treatment of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis. This regulatory milestone reflects the agency's recognition of pemvidutide's potential in a condition with limited treatment options. The full evidence-graded record is in the [pemvidutide dossier](/peptides/pemvidutide).

## What We Know

### Understanding Breakthrough Therapy Designation

Breakthrough Therapy Designation is an FDA program designed to expedite development of drugs that may offer substantial improvement over existing therapies for serious conditions.

**BTD provides:**
- More intensive FDA guidance on efficient drug development
- Eligibility for rolling review
- Organizational commitment involving senior FDA managers
- Eligibility for accelerated approval and priority review

**BTD does not guarantee approval.** It is a development-stage designation, not an efficacy finding. Pemvidutide also holds Fast Track designation for MASH — and, as of 17 August 2026, no marketing application has been filed for pemvidutide anywhere.

### Pemvidutide: The Drug

Pemvidutide is a dual GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist developed by Altimmune. Like [survodutide](/peptides/survodutide) (Boehringer Ingelheim), pemvidutide activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Mechanism:** Dual GLP-1R/GCGR agonist
- **Administration:** Once-weekly subcutaneous injection
- **Developer:** Altimmune, Inc.
- **Development stage:** Phase 2b completed in MASH; registrational Phase 3 planned

### The Clinical Data Behind the Designation

The MASH evidence comes from the IMPACT phase 2b trial (NCT05989711), which randomised 212 patients across 83 sites in the United States and Australia to once-weekly pemvidutide at 1.2 mg or 1.8 mg, or placebo [impact-phase2b].

At 24 weeks:

| Endpoint | Pemvidutide 1.2 mg | Pemvidutide 1.8 mg | Placebo |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|---------|
| MASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis | 58% | 52% | 20% |
| Fibrosis improvement without worsening of MASH | 33% | 36% | 28% |

The resolution result was statistically significant at both strengths (p\<0.0001). The fibrosis result was not significant at either (p=0.59 and p=0.27) — an important caveat, because fibrosis stage is what links most strongly to long-term clinical outcomes [impact-phase2b].

Separately, pemvidutide's phase 2 *obesity* trial, MOMENTUM (NCT05295875), reported 15.6% weight loss at 48 weeks versus 2.3% with placebo [momentum-obesity-trial]. That result speaks to the metabolic side of the mechanism, not to liver histology.

### The Dual Agonist Rationale

The combination of GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonism appears well-suited to liver disease:

**GLP-1 receptor activation provides:**
- Appetite suppression and weight loss
- Improved glycemic control
- Indirect hepatic benefit through metabolic improvement

**Glucagon receptor activation adds:**
- Direct hepatic effects — the liver expresses glucagon receptors but not GLP-1 receptors
- Increased hepatic fat oxidation
- Reduced de novo lipogenesis

The closest published comparator with the same mechanism is survodutide, whose phase 2 MASH trial reported improvement in MASH with no worsening of fibrosis in 47%, 62% and 43% across its three strengths versus 14% with placebo [survodutide-phase2-mash]. Cross-trial comparison between MASH programs is unreliable — entry criteria, fibrosis-stage distribution, biopsy reading methodology and trial duration all differ — so these figures contextualise the class rather than rank the drugs.

## What We Don't Know

**Phase 3 outcomes.** The BTD rests on phase 2b data. Whether the resolution result replicates at scale, whether the fibrosis endpoint reaches significance with more patients and longer treatment, and whether histological improvement translates into fewer liver-related clinical events all remain open.

**Safety in broader populations.** Phase 2 populations are selected. Longer exposure and more diverse patients — advanced fibrosis, older adults, renal impairment, polypharmacy — are what a phase 3 safety database exists to characterise.

**Comparative position.** No head-to-head trials compare pemvidutide to survodutide, semaglutide, or other MASH candidates.

## What's Next

Altimmune has stated that it completed an end-of-Phase-2 meeting with the FDA and reached alignment on a registrational Phase 3 trial in MASH patients with moderate to advanced fibrosis, evaluating multiple strengths over a 52-week treatment period with biopsy-based endpoints intended to support potential accelerated approval.

As of 17 August 2026, pemvidutide has designations, not applications: no NDA or BLA has been filed, and no approval decision of any kind exists. Our companion pieces on the [IMPACT trial results](/blog/pemvidutide-impact-mash-resolution) and pemvidutide's [regulatory position](/blog/pemvidutide-crl-more-data) track both threads.

## How Strong Is the Evidence?

The IMPACT trial was randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled with histological endpoints — appropriate methodology for its stage. But it is a single phase 2b trial: the resolution endpoint was met, the fibrosis endpoint was not, and no long-term or clinical-outcome data exist. The FDA's designation signals that the agency found the preliminary evidence compelling; it is not a finding that the drug works. Phase 3 will decide pemvidutide's place.
