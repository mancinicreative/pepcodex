## Overview

[5-Amino-1MQ](/peptides/5-amino-1mq) is the research name used on this site for 5-amino-1-methylquinolinium, a small-molecule nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) inhibitor. [AOD-9604](/peptides/aod-9604) (also written AOD9604) is a synthetic peptide corresponding to the C-terminal fragment of human growth hormone from amino acids 177-191 with an additional N-terminal tyrosine [aod-9604-identity-pmid-25208511]. The mouse NNMT-inhibitor papers and the AOD-9604 rodent papers fetched this run are separate programmes. This page does not collapse them into one efficacy ratio.

Related pages: [5-Amino-1MQ vs semaglutide](/compare/5-amino-1mq-vs-semaglutide) and [amycretin vs AOD-9604](/compare/amycretin-vs-aod-9604).

PMID 39067875 title-matched as an NNMT / urothelial bladder cancer CAF paper. It is not an obesity RCT and is not quoted as a weight-change result. PMID 33645410 title did not name 5-amino-1-methylquinolinium; it is not quoted.

A PubMed search for `"AOD-9604" AND (randomized OR "clinical trial" OR "phase 3" OR "phase III" OR "phase 2")` on 2026-09-02 returned 1 record (PMID 16625817, a 2006 development review). `"AOD9604"` with the same clinical-trial/phase-3 terms returned 3 records (PMIDs 42395176, 16931496, 16625817), all reviews. PMID 15134286 (2004 drug profile) states that Metabolic was developing AOD-9604 for potential obesity treatment and that by February 2002, phase IIa trials were underway. The abstract does not publish enrollment, duration, estimand, or a percent [aod-9604-profile-pmid-15134286].

PMID 16625817 (2006 review) lists AOD-9604 among then-in-development obesity drugs as a human growth hormone fragment said to increase adipose-tissue breakdown. Of **that set**, the review states that only rimonabant had completed phase III. That is not an AOD-9604 Phase 3 result. This page does not invent an obesity Phase 3 readout or a human weight-change percent that is not in a fetched abstract [aod-9604-review-pmid-16625817].

PMID 16931496 (2006 review) mentions AOD9604 among compounds then said to be in clinical trials. The abstract publishes **no** enrollment and **no** weight-change percent. PMID 17971763 and PMID 22435392 also name AOD9604 in development/patent reviews without a human RCT percent. Those reviews are not quoted as trial results.

### AOD-9604 rodent papers (not human RCT percents)

PMID 11146367 studied AOD9604 in obese Zucker rats for 19 days. Body-weight gain was 15.8 ± 0.6 g versus 35.6 ± 0.8 g in controls. That is a rodent gram figure, not a human RCT percent. The abstract's treatment amount is not copied here [aod-9604-rat-pmid-11146367].

PMID 11673763 treated obese and lean mice for 14 days. Both hGH and AOD9604 reduced body-weight gain in obese mice and increased fat oxidation. The abstract does not publish a human percent. AOD9604 did not compete for the hGH receptor in the in-vitro assay reported there [aod-9604-mice-pmid-11673763].

PMID 11713213 is a related mouse / beta(3)-adrenergic-receptor knock-out paper. It is not quoted as an obesity RCT. PMID 26275694 is a rabbit osteoarthritis model and is not quoted as a weight-change result. PMID 25208511 describes peptide identity and in-vitro metabolites for anti-doping detection; it publishes no efficacy percent [aod-9604-identity-pmid-25208511].
