**Correction (September 29, 2026):** An earlier version said that University of Southern California researchers had launched a Phase 1 MOTS-c trial and gave a participant count, doses, schedule, outcomes, and projected approval dates. The cited trial identifier does not resolve in ClinicalTrials.gov. Those unsupported claims have been removed. The registered study described below is a different record, first posted in 2026.

## What has been studied?

The [2015 discovery paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/) identified MOTS-c as a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded in the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region. Administered MOTS-c reduced diet-related obesity and insulin resistance **in mice**. These animal findings do not establish a clinical benefit in people.

A [2021 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/) reported changes in the body's own MOTS-c in human muscle and blood after exercise. The same paper tested administered MOTS-c for physical performance and aging outcomes in **mice**. Measuring a peptide in people is different from giving it to them; that study did not test MOTS-c treatment in humans.

## What does the trial registry show?

[ClinicalTrials.gov record NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745), first posted April 1, 2026, lists a Phase 2 study of MOTS-c and placebo in adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity. The record names Hudson Biotech as sponsor, reports a "Recruiting" status, and gives an **estimated** enrollment of 120. Its listed primary outcomes are change in insulin sensitivity and treatment-emergent adverse events. No results are posted in the registry as of this update.

A registry entry reports information submitted by the study's responsible party. Its status and planned enrollment do not independently confirm that participants received MOTS-c, or show whether it is safe or effective. This record does not support the earlier claim about a USC Phase 1 study.

## What remains unknown?

The [FDA's compounding safety page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks) says it has not identified human exposure data for drug products containing MOTS-c administered by any route. The agency also identifies potential immunogenicity and peptide-impurity concerns for compounded MOTS-c products. That statement concerns administered products; it does not erase studies measuring naturally occurring MOTS-c in people.

**Evidence level: very low for clinical use.** The published papers above support biological and animal findings, while the listed trial has no posted results. Human treatment benefits and safety remain unestablished.
