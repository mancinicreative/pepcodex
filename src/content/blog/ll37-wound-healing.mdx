LL-37 is frequently described as a wound-healing peptide. The underlying biology is real and well characterised, and there is a registered clinical trial in diabetic foot ulcers. What does not exist is published human efficacy data.

## What Is LL-37?

LL-37 is the only human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, a 37-amino-acid peptide cleaved from its precursor protein hCAP18. It plays multiple roles in innate immunity:

**Antimicrobial Activity:**
- Kills bacteria by membrane disruption
- Active against gram-positive and gram-negative organisms
- Antifungal properties
- Antiviral effects against some pathogens

**Immunomodulation:**
- Recruits immune cells to infection sites
- Modulates inflammatory responses

**Tissue Repair:**
- Effects on epithelial cell migration and proliferation
- Involvement in angiogenesis and tissue remodelling

This combination of properties is why LL-37 has been investigated for wound healing applications.

## The Diabetic Wound Problem

Diabetic foot ulcers represent a major clinical challenge:

- **Prevalence:** 15-25% of diabetics develop foot ulcers
- **Healing failure:** 30-40% fail to heal with standard care
- **Infection risk:** Compromised immunity increases infection
- **Amputation:** Leading cause of non-traumatic amputation
- **Mortality:** 5-year mortality after amputation approaches 50%

Current treatments (debridement, offloading, dressings) have limited efficacy, and new approaches are needed [chronic-wound-treatment].

## What Has Actually Been Studied in Humans

One interventional trial of topical LL-37 in diabetic foot ulcers is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov: **NCT04098562**, *Efficacy of LL-37 Cream on Bacteria Colonization, Inflammation Response and Healing Rate of Diabetic Foot Ulcers* [ll37-diabetic-wounds].

Its registered characteristics are modest and worth stating plainly:

- **Phase:** 2
- **Enrolment:** 40 participants
- **Sponsor:** Fakultas Kedokteran Universitas Indonesia
- **Start date:** October 2019
- **Status:** Unknown
- **Comparator:** LL-37 cream versus standard wound care
- **Primary outcomes:** healing rate, aerobic bacterial count and species, and inflammation markers, measured over the first weeks of treatment

**No results have been posted for this trial**, and its status has not been updated to completed. A registration establishes that a study was planned; it is not evidence of an effect. Any figure quoted anywhere for LL-37's healing rate in humans does not come from published trial data.

## Mechanism: What the Preclinical Work Shows

The most concrete recent mechanistic evidence in the diabetic context comes from a mouse study. LL-37 accelerated wound healing in diabetic mice by activating TFEB-dependent autophagy in keratinocytes under hyperglycaemic conditions, with increased expression of the autophagy-related proteins ATG5, ATG7 and beclin 1 [ll37-mechanisms-review].

This is a coherent mechanistic story — it proposes how an antimicrobial peptide could act on the host cell repair programme rather than only on bacteria. It is also a mouse study, and the gap between accelerated healing in diabetic mice and closure rates in human ulcers is exactly the gap that trials exist to test.

### Why LL-37 Is a Plausible Candidate

Several features make LL-37 attractive for this indication in principle:

- **Dual action:** antimicrobial activity plus effects on host repair pathways, where most wound therapies address only one
- **Human origin:** an endogenous human peptide, which may limit immunogenicity concerns
- **Topical route:** local application to the wound bed rather than systemic exposure

None of these are efficacy findings. They are reasons the hypothesis is worth testing.

## What This Means

LL-37 sits in a common and easily misread position: strong mechanistic rationale, real preclinical data, a registered trial — and no human efficacy results.

For patients with chronic wounds, that means LL-37 is not a treatment option. Diabetic foot ulcers carry serious consequences, and the evidence-based interventions that do exist — debridement, offloading, infection control and specialist wound care — remain the standard for good reason.

For readers evaluating claims about LL-37 products, the useful question is which human study any quoted number comes from. At present, for wound healing, there is no published one.
