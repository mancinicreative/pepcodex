  - >-
    One pivotal fracture trial, not two. Everything about the fracture claim
    traces back to ACTIVE and its extension. There is no independent
    replication of the fracture result by a second sponsor or a second
    population, and the network meta-analyses that rank abaloparatide favourably
    are pooling that same trial.
  - >-
    The male indication rests on bone density, not fractures. ATOM randomized 228
    men for 12 months with lumbar spine bone mineral density as its primary
    endpoint. That is an appropriate design for its size, but it means the
    approved use in men is supported by a surrogate, and the 2026 systematic
    review states plainly that evidence in men and long-term safety remain
    limited.
  - >-
    The nonvertebral fracture result sits on the significance threshold. The
    label reports a 43% relative reduction with a log-rank P of 0.049 at 19
    months. A result that close to the boundary is not the same strength of
    finding as the vertebral one, where the confidence interval on the relative
    reduction runs from 61% to 95%.
  - >-
    The transdermal formulation failed its noninferiority test. In a 511-patient
    phase 3 study the microstructured transdermal system produced a 12-month
    lumbar spine gain of 7.14% against 10.86% for the subcutaneous form — a
    treatment difference of −3.72% (95% CI −5.01 to −2.43) against a
    noninferiority margin of 2.0% — and administration-site reactions affected
    94.4% of the transdermal group. No transdermal abaloparatide product exists.
  - >-
    ACTIVExtend was not blinded throughout. Its own report notes that
    participants were no longer blinded to their original ACTIVE assignment
    during the later alendronate period, and everyone in the extension received
    the same open-label alendronate. The 43-month comparison is therefore between
    two prior treatments, not between two concurrent ones.
  - >-
    Several of the supporting analyses are post hoc. The trabecular bone score
    result, the density-target probability model and the bone turnover marker
    comparisons against teriparatide are all secondary or post hoc analyses of
    ACTIVE and ACTIVExtend. They describe the same participants repeatedly and
    are not independent confirmations.
  - >-
    The label's duration statement is stricter than teriparatide's. Section 2.3
    of the TYMLOS label states that safety and efficacy have not been evaluated
    beyond 2 years and that use beyond 2 years in a lifetime is not recommended,

    judgement. The two PTH1 receptor agonists are not interchangeable on this
    point.

Abaloparatide is the second osteoanabolic drug approved for osteoporosis in the United States, and the only one that is not built from parathyroid hormone. It is an analogue of parathyroid hormone-related protein — a different endogenous ligand that reaches the same PTH1 receptor — and the TYMLOS label puts the distance in numbers: 41% homology to human PTH(1-34) against 76% homology to human PTHrP(1-34).

It arrived in April 2017 on the strength of a single pivotal trial, and that trial did something unusual. It ran an open-label teriparatide arm alongside, which is why the two compounds are almost always discussed as a pair — including on this site.

### How It Works (Simplified)

    <p class="text-sm text-[color:var(--ink-muted)]">Parathyroid hormone-related protein is not parathyroid hormone.     <p class="text-sm text-[color:var(--ink-muted)]">Bone formation markers rise ahead of resorption markers, and the size of that early gap — the uncoupling index — predicts how much bone density is gained later. The same relationship has now been measured in women and in men.</p>

  <div class="bg-[color:var(--primary-soft)] rounded-lg p-5 border border-[color:var(--primary-border)]">

Two label facts deserve stating precisely, because they are commonly reported loosely.

**The contraindication is one item long.** Section 4 lists a history of systemic hypersensitivity, noting that reactions have included anaphylaxis, dyspnea and urticaria. Osteosarcoma, hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, urolithiasis and orthostatic hypotension all appear in section 5 as warnings and precautions, not as contraindications.

**The duration statement is stricter than teriparatide's.** Section 2.3 of the TYMLOS label states that safety and efficacy have not been evaluated beyond 2 years of treatment, and that use for more than 2 years during a lifetime is not recommended. The current FORTEO label frames the same period differently, as a benefit-risk judgement about continued high fracture risk. On this point the two drugs are not interchangeable.

One recent regulatory event, recorded here as an event only. On **3 August 2026** the FDA approved TYMLOS SUPPL 18 — the same day it approved labeling supplements to both teriparatide products, FORTEO (SUPPL 59) and BONSITY (SUPPL 21) — with labels and Medication Guides posted 11 August 2026. Three applications, three companies, one date: that is a class-level labeling change across every approved PTH1 receptor agonist rather than one sponsor's edit. **What the change says is not described on this page.** The drug-label record retrievable from openFDA is still the SPL version effective 4 April 2025, so the revision's content could not be confirmed and is not guessed at here.

### Clinical Evidence

**The pivotal trial.** ACTIVE was a phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial run between March 2011 and October 2014 at 28 sites in 10 countries, enrolling 2,463 postmenopausal women with a mean age of 69. Participants were randomized to placebo (n=821), abaloparatide (n=824) or open-label teriparatide (n=818) for 18 months; 1,901 completed. The FDA label reports the primary result: new vertebral fractures in 0.6% of the abaloparatide group versus 4.2% of the placebo group — an absolute reduction of 3.6% (95% CI 2.1 to 5.4) and a relative reduction of 86% (95% CI 61 to 95). Nonvertebral fractures were 2.7% versus 4.7%, a 43% relative reduction with a log-rank P of 0.049. [PMID:27533157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27533157/) · [NCT01343004](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01343004)

The one prespecified comparison against the teriparatide arm was a safety endpoint. Hypercalcemia occurred in 3.4% on abaloparatide versus 6.4% on teriparatide (risk difference −2.96, 95% CI −5.12 to −0.87; P=0.006). The trial's own authors wrote that further research was needed to understand its efficacy relative to other osteoporosis treatments.

**The extension.** ACTIVExtend (NCT01657162) enrolled 1,139 women — 92% of ACTIVE completers, 558 from the abaloparatide group and 581 from placebo — into up to 24 months of alendronate. Across the full 43 months, 0.9% of the abaloparatide-then-alendronate group had a new radiographic vertebral fracture versus 5.6% of the placebo-then-alendronate group, an 84% relative reduction (P&lt;0.001). A supplemental analysis prepared for regulators found no hip fractures in the abaloparatide sequence against five in the comparator sequence. [PMID:29800372](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29800372/)

**Men.** ATOM randomized 228 men aged 40 to 85 with osteoporosis 2:1 to abaloparatide or placebo for 12 months. Lumbar spine bone mineral density rose 8.48% versus 1.17%, total hip 2.14% versus 0.01% and femoral neck 2.98% versus 0.15%, all P&lt;0.0001. The primary endpoint was bone density — a trial of 228 men cannot test fractures — and the label's indication in men is written as increasing bone density rather than reducing fractures, which matches. [PMID:36190391](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36190391/) · [NCT03512262](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03512262) A companion analysis found early bone formation marker changes predicted 12-month density gains in men much as they do in women. [PMID:39791502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39791502/)

**Japan.** ACTIVE-J tested 78 weeks against placebo in Japanese postmenopausal women and men at high fracture risk. Lumbar spine density rose 12.5% (95% CI 10.3 to 14.8; P&lt;0.001), total hip 4.3% and femoral neck 4.3% versus placebo. Four vertebrae across 3 placebo participants fractured and none in the treated group — a count far too small to be fracture evidence, and the trial was not designed to produce any. [PMID:35977548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35977548/)

**Microarchitecture.** A post hoc analysis of 911 women from ACTIVE and ACTIVExtend found mean trabecular bone score rose about 4% over 18 months, reaching 4.4% at 43 months on the abaloparatide sequence versus 1.7% on the placebo sequence. Half the treated women had a rise above the least significant change, and that rise was associated with lower odds of vertebral fracture (odds ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.11 to 0.79 at 43 months). Post hoc, and not powered for fracture. [PMID:36588166](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36588166/)

**Independent syntheses.** A network meta-analysis screening 4,978 articles and including 22 found abaloparatide had the greatest treatment effect against placebo in all three networks — vertebral (relative risk 0.13, 95% credible interval 0.04 to 0.34), nonvertebral (0.50, 0.28 to 0.85) and wrist (0.39, 0.15 to 0.90) — with the caveat that adverse events and costs were not modelled. [PMID:30953114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30953114/) The 2025 network meta-analysis restricted to PTH1 receptor agonists found abaloparatide ahead of teriparatide for nonvertebral fractures (odds ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.80 to 0.95) and hip fractures (odds ratio 0.81, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.93), with no increased cardiovascular risk for either. [PMID:40047881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40047881/) The BMJ network meta-analysis of 69 trials and over 80,000 patients placed bone anabolic agents above oral bisphosphonates for clinical and vertebral fractures irrespective of baseline risk, at moderate-to-low certainty. [PMID:37130601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37130601/) The American College of Physicians living review graded the vertebral and clinical fracture reduction as moderate to high certainty while noting that both PTH1 agents increased withdrawals due to adverse events. [PMID:36592455](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36592455/) A 2026 systematic review pooling nine evidence sources reported vertebral fracture relative risks of 0.13 to 0.21 and concluded that evidence in men and long-term safety remain limited. [PMID:41598611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41598611/)

### Where the Evidence Is Negative or Null

- **The transdermal formulation failed.** A 511-patient open-label phase 3 noninferiority study compared a microstructured transdermal system against the subcutaneous form for 12 months, with a noninferiority margin of 2.0% on lumbar spine density. The transdermal group gained 7.14% versus 10.86%, a treatment difference of −3.72% (95% CI −5.01 to −2.43) — noninferiority was not demonstrated. Total hip gains were 1.97% versus 3.70%, PINP rose 52.6% versus 74.5%, and administration-site reactions affected 94.4% of the transdermal group against 70.5% of the subcutaneous group. No transdermal abaloparatide product exists. [PMID:37417725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37417725/) · [NCT04064411](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04064411)
- **The nonvertebral result is marginal.** The label's own figure is a log-rank P of 0.049 on a 43% relative reduction. That is a positive result at the conventional threshold and nothing more; it does not carry the weight of the vertebral finding.
- **Nothing in this evidence base is a head-to-head fracture trial.** ACTIVE was powered against placebo, and its teriparatide arm was open-label. Every abaloparatide-versus-teriparatide fracture comparison on this page comes from network meta-analysis — indirect evidence pooled across trials — and the 2025 analysis that favours abaloparatide at nonvertebral and hip sites is exactly that.
- **Registration is not evidence.** NCT04467983 (abaloparatide added to continued denosumab, 70 participants, primary completion September 2026) and NCT04167163 (abaloparatide before total knee arthroplasty, 58 participants, completed 2025) are registrations that this page cites no result from. NCT04626141, a phase 4 study in supracondylar distal femur fractures, was withdrawn with zero enrollment. NCT03710889, a 23-participant study of tissue-based indices of bone formation and resorption, is likewise a registration only.

### Safety Profile

No abaloparatide product carries a boxed warning. The following is what the current TYMLOS label states, retrieved from the openFDA drug label endpoint on 17 August 2026 (SPL effective 4 April 2025).

- **Osteosarcoma.** Section 5.1 of the label reports that abaloparatide caused a strength-dependent increase in osteosarcoma in male and female rats at exposures 4 to 28 times human exposure, that it is unknown whether the same happens in humans, that osteosarcoma has been reported in patients treated with a PTH analogue after marketing, that human observational studies have not shown an increased risk, and that data beyond 2 years are limited. The label directs prescribers away from use in people at raised baseline osteosarcoma risk — open epiphyses, metabolic bone diseases other than osteoporosis including Paget's disease, bone metastases or a history of skeletal malignancy, prior skeletal radiation, and hereditary predisposition.
- **Orthostatic hypotension.** The label describes orthostatic hypotension typically within 4 hours, with dizziness, palpitations, tachycardia or nausea, often resolving on lying down. In the pivotal trial, an orthostatic blood pressure fall of at least 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic one hour after the first administration occurred in 4% versus 3% on placebo.
- **Hypercalcemia.** The label states abaloparatide may cause hypercalcemia and is not recommended where hypercalcemia or an underlying hypercalcemic disorder such as primary hyperparathyroidism already exists.
- **Hypercalciuria and urolithiasis.** The label states abaloparatide may cause hypercalciuria and that whether it worsens existing urolithiasis is unknown.
- **Common adverse reactions.** Per the label, in postmenopausal women at 2% or more: hypercalciuria 11% versus 9%, dizziness 10% versus 6%, nausea 8% versus 3%, headache 8% versus 6%, palpitations 5% versus 0.4%, fatigue, upper abdominal pain and vertigo. In men, administration-site erythema, dizziness, arthralgia, administration-site swelling and pain, contusion, nausea, diarrhea, abdominal distension and pain, and bone pain. Serious adverse events were 10% versus 11% on placebo in the pivotal trial, with discontinuation for adverse events at 10% versus 6%.
- **Contraindication.** Section 4 lists a history of systemic hypersensitivity, and nothing else.

### How This Page Relates to Teriparatide

The [teriparatide](/peptides/teriparatide) dossier covers the other approved PTH1 receptor agonist, and the two pages are built as a deliberate pair. The most important thing to keep straight is that they are **not** the same kind of molecule: teriparatide is the first 34 residues of human parathyroid hormone itself, while abaloparatide is an analogue of parathyroid hormone-related protein, carrying a non-natural Aib residue and a C-terminal amide. Describing them interchangeably would be a scientific error, not shorthand.

Their evidence bases differ in shape as well as size. Teriparatide has two randomized trials with fracture as the primary endpoint — one placebo-controlled and one head-to-head against risedronate — spread across 20-plus years, five US applications and three generic entrants. Abaloparatide has one pivotal fracture programme, one sponsor and no generics, but a larger relative vertebral fracture reduction within it (86% against placebo, versus the 65% the FORTEO label reports for its own pivotal trial) and, in the pooled indirect evidence, an advantage at nonvertebral and hip sites. Neither of those comparisons comes from a trial designed to compare them on fractures, and no such trial exists.

The comparisons that do come from within a single randomized trial are ACTIVE's: hypercalcemia at 3.4% versus 6.4%, the early bone formation marker response correlating more tightly with later density gain for abaloparatide (r=0.561 versus 0.198), and a similar formation-resorption balance from month 3 onward. [PMID:30635696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30635696/) A post hoc model of the same cohorts predicted that more than half of women would reach a total hip T-score above −2.5 in 18 months from a starting T-score as low as −2.7 on either agent. [PMID:40214193](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40214193/)

### Important Limitations

- The fracture claim rests on one pivotal programme from one sponsor, without independent replication
- The male indication is supported by bone mineral density in 228 men, not by fractures
- The nonvertebral fracture result sits at P=0.049, on the significance threshold
- The transdermal formulation failed its noninferiority test and does not exist as a product
- ACTIVExtend's later period was unblinded, and everyone in it received the same open-label alendronate
- The trabecular bone score, density-target and marker analyses are post hoc on the same cohort
- Every abaloparatide-versus-teriparatide fracture comparison is indirect; no head-to-head fracture trial exists
- The label states safety and efficacy have not been evaluated beyond 2 years of treatment

This page is an evidence review, not medical guidance. It does not describe how any product is used, and nothing here should be read as a recommendation.
