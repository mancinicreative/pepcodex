    name: Weight Loss
    researchSummary: >-
      Semaglutide is FDA-approved for chronic weight management (as Wegovy) and
      has demonstrated substantial efficacy in multiple Phase 3 trials. The STEP
      1 trial showed 14.9% mean weight loss versus 2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks
      (N=1,961).

      The mechanism involves GLP-1 receptor activation in appetite-regulating
      brain regions, reducing hunger and increasing satiety. Higher dose studies
      (STEP UP, 7.2mg) achieved 20.7% weight loss. Approximately 50% of
      participants achieve 15% or greater weight loss.

      Semaglutide represents a significant advance in pharmacological obesity
      treatment. However, weight tends to return after discontinuation (STEP 4
      showed 2/3 of lost weight regained within a year of stopping), suggesting
      long-term treatment may be necessary.

  - slug: appetite-control
    name: Appetite Control
    researchSummary: >-
      Semaglutide's weight loss efficacy is primarily mediated through central
      appetite suppression. The drug activates GLP-1 receptors in the
      hypothalamus, specifically affecting POMC/CART (satiety) and NPY/AgRP
      (hunger) neuronal pathways.

      Clinical studies demonstrate 20-35% reduction in caloric intake. Patients
      report reduced hunger, increased fullness, and decreased food cravings.
      The drug also delays gastric emptying, prolonging post-meal satiety.

      These appetite effects are sustained during treatment but reverse after
      discontinuation, which is why weight regain occurs when the drug is
      stopped. This suggests semaglutide is managing appetite rather than
      permanently resetting hunger signals.

faqs:
  - question: What is semaglutide?
    answer: >-
      Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes
      (Ozempic, Rybelsus), chronic weight management (Wegovy), cardiovascular
      risk reduction in obesity, and MASH with liver fibrosis.
  - question: Is semaglutide FDA approved?
    answer: >-
      Yes. Semaglutide is FDA-approved under multiple brand names: Ozempic
      (diabetes), Wegovy (weight management), and Rybelsus (oral diabetes
      formulation).
  - question: How effective is semaglutide for weight loss?
    answer: >-
      In the STEP 1 trial, semaglutide achieved 14.9% mean weight loss versus
      2.4% with placebo at 68 weeks. Higher doses (7.2mg) achieved 20.7% weight
      loss in STEP UP.
  - question: What are common semaglutide side effects?
    answer: >-
      The most common side effects are gastrointestinal: nausea (30-45% during
      titration), vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation. These typically improve
      with continued use.
  - question: Does weight return after stopping semaglutide?
    answer: >-
      Yes. The STEP 4 trial showed approximately two-thirds of lost weight
      returns within one year of discontinuation, suggesting long-term treatment
      may be necessary for sustained benefit.

Semaglutide is a selective GLP-1 receptor agonist with extensive Phase 3 clinical trial data supporting its mechanisms in humans.

### How It Works (Simplified)

Semaglutide mimics GLP-1, a natural hormone released after eating, producing multiple therapeutic effects:

### 2025-2026 Developments

**Oral Wegovy Approval:** The FDA approved oral semaglutide 25mg (Wegovy) in December 2025, marking the first oral GLP-1 formulation approved for chronic weight management at a launch price of approximately $149/month.

**MASH Indication:** Semaglutide received FDA approval for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), expanding its therapeutic indications beyond diabetes and obesity.

**Safety Signal — Acute Kidney Injury:** A 2025 disproportionality analysis of the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) found that semaglutide carried a reporting signal for acute kidney injury, typically attributed to dehydration from GI adverse events; the authors recommend ensuring hydration and renal monitoring when initiating therapy (PMID: 41227073).

**Patent Landscape:** The core semaglutide compound patent expired in March 2026 across several major markets including India, Canada, China, Brazil, and Turkey, with 40+ biosimilar applications filed in India alone.

**Head-to-head against a combination product (REIMAGINE 2).** REIMAGINE 2, published on 7 June 2026, put semaglutide 2.4 mg and 1.0 mg directly against cagrilintide-semaglutide and against cagrilintide alone in 2,713 adults with type 2 diabetes across 30 countries over 68 weeks ([PMID:42251859](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42251859/), NCT06065540). On the efficacy estimand, which the paper names as primary, HbA1c fell 1.91 percentage points on the combination against 1.75 on semaglutide 2.4 mg. The difference of 0.16 percentage points (95% CI 0.05 to 0.27) reached statistical significance. Read from semaglutide's side, that is the size of the gap a second mechanism opened over semaglutide alone in this population, and it came with more adverse events: 86.9% versus 81.2%.

**Indirect comparison with orforglipron.** A population-adjusted indirect treatment comparison built oral semaglutide 25 mg against orforglipron 36 mg using individual patient data from OASIS 4 and aggregate data from ATTAIN-1 ([PMID:42225305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42225305/)). It reported greater weight loss with oral semaglutide by 3.2 percentage points on the treatment-regimen estimand (95% CI -5.9 to -0.4) and 3.0 points on the efficacy estimand (95% CI -5.8 to -0.3), and higher discontinuation with orforglipron. Two caveats belong beside that result: the analysis is indirect, comparing arms from separate trials rather than randomised head-to-head, and it was funded and authored by the manufacturer of the winning compound.

**STEP 12 — semaglutide 2.4 mg in Chinese adults.** STEP 12 was a phase 3b trial at 19 sites across mainland China and Taiwan, published in Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology on 10 August 2026 ([PMID:42575111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42575111/), NCT06041217). It enrolled 242 adults at locally defined BMI thresholds — 24 to under 28 with a weight-related comorbidity, or 28 to under 30 — randomised 2:1 to semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 44 weeks. Body weight fell 12.1% versus 2.2%, an estimated treatment difference of 9.9 percentage points (95% CI -11.8 to -8.0), and 80.5% versus 24.4% reached at least 5% reduction. The BMI entry thresholds are lower than in the Western STEP trials, which is the point of the trial and also the limit on transferring its numbers.

**OASIS 2 — oral semaglutide 50 mg in an East Asian population.** OASIS 2 was a 68-week phase 3a trial in Japan and South Korea, published in JAMA Internal Medicine in October 2025 ([PMID:40758358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40758358/), NCT05132088). Of 201 participants, about a quarter had type 2 diabetes. Body weight fell 14.3% versus 1.3% on placebo, an estimated treatment difference of 13.07 percentage points (95% CI -15.61 to -10.52), and 84.3% versus 17.2% reached at least 5% reduction. Gastrointestinal adverse events were reported by 63.4% of the semaglutide group.

**REALYSE — registry results only.** REALYSE (NCT05035082, phase 4, n=1,018) posted results to ClinicalTrials.gov on 10 August 2026. A dateless PubMed search on 17 August 2026 for both the registration number and the trial name returned no indexed publication, so no figures are quoted from it here.

### Important Limitations

- The favourable indirect comparison against orforglipron was authored and funded by semaglutide's manufacturer, and indirect comparisons cannot substitute for a head-to-head trial
- **Common GI side effects:** Nausea (30-45%), vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation, especially during dose titration
- **Boxed warning:** Thyroid C-cell tumors observed in rodents; contraindicated in personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN2
- **Gallbladder disease:** Increased risk (~2.5%) associated with rapid weight loss
- **Pancreatitis:** Rare (~0.3%); the FDA label directs discontinuation if pancreatitis is suspected
- **Pregnancy:** Not a labeled contraindication — section 4 lists only MTC/MEN 2 history and hypersensitivity — but the FDA label directs discontinuation once pregnancy is recognized, and at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy, attributing that interval to semaglutide's long half-life
- **Weight regain:** Approximately 2/3 of lost weight returns within 1 year of discontinuation (STEP 4)
- **Muscle mass:** ~30% of weight lost is lean mass
