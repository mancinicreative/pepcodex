Complete results from ATTAIN-1, the Phase 3 obesity trial of orforglipron, have been published in the *New England Journal of Medicine*. Orforglipron is an oral, non-peptide (small-molecule) GLP-1 receptor agonist, and the trial establishes what that approach delivers: meaningful weight loss, achieved without injection, at a level below the injectable benchmarks.

## What ATTAIN-1 Found

ATTAIN-1 enrolled 3,127 participants across ten countries and tested three doses against placebo over 72 weeks [attain-program-update] [lilly-orforglipron-data].

| Dose | Mean weight loss at 72 weeks |
|---|---|
| 6 mg | −7.8% |
| 12 mg | −9.3% |
| 36 mg | −11.2% |

Lilly's press release quotes 12.4% for the same dose. Both are real: −11.2% is the least-squares mean published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (treatment-regimen estimand, i.e. what happened including people who stopped or added other therapy), while 12.4% is the efficacy estimand, which models results as if every participant had adhered. We lead with the peer-reviewed figure.
| Placebo | −0.9% |

All three doses met the primary endpoint of superior weight reduction versus placebo.

**Key secondary endpoints at the highest dose:**

- 59.6% achieved ≥10% weight loss
- 39.6% achieved ≥15% weight loss
- Waist circumference reduced by 11.1 cm
- Non-HDL cholesterol −8.5%, triglycerides −21.6%, systolic blood pressure −6.7 mm Hg

### Safety and Tolerability

Adverse events were predominantly gastrointestinal and mostly mild to moderate, consistent with the GLP-1 class. Discontinuation due to adverse events rose with dose — 5.3% at 6 mg, 7.9% at 12 mg and 10.3% at 36 mg, against 2.7% on placebo [lilly-orforglipron-data].

That dose-dependent discontinuation is worth noting alongside the dose-dependent efficacy: the dose that delivers 11.2% is also the dose that roughly quadruples the placebo discontinuation rate.

## The Non-Peptide Approach

Orforglipron is a small organic molecule that binds and activates the GLP-1 receptor, rather than a peptide analogue of GLP-1 itself. It was characterised in Phase 1 as a novel oral non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist [oral-glp1-review].

The practical consequences of that chemistry:

- **Manufacturing:** small-molecule synthesis rather than peptide synthesis, using established chemical manufacturing infrastructure
- **No permeation enhancer:** it does not depend on the absorption-enhancing excipient that oral semaglutide requires
- **Distribution:** no cold chain, no injection devices

Note that specific bioavailability comparisons between orforglipron and oral semaglutide are frequently quoted without a source. This article does not repeat figures it cannot attribute to published data.

## Where It Sits Among the Options

Orforglipron's ATTAIN-1 result places it as a genuinely effective oral option that does not match the highest-performing injectables on weight loss alone. For a patient who will not use an injection, that comparison is beside the point; for a patient indifferent to route, it is the central trade-off.

The other Phase 3 work informs the picture:

- **ATTAIN-2** studied orforglipron in adults with obesity or overweight **and type 2 diabetes** — a different population from ATTAIN-1, not a head-to-head against oral semaglutide.
- The head-to-head against oral semaglutide was **ACHIEVE-3**, conducted in adults with type 2 diabetes rather than in obesity.

## Market Implications

The oral non-peptide approach carries real structural advantages:

**Manufacturing.** Small-molecule synthesis is typically cheaper and more scalable than peptide production.

**Supply.** Oral formulations avoid the fill-finish constraints that limited injectable GLP-1 supply during the shortage.

**Distribution.** Room-temperature stability and no injection device simplify the supply chain.

Whether any of this reaches patients as lower prices depends on payer dynamics and competition rather than on manufacturing cost alone.

## What's Next

Lilly stated at the time of the ATTAIN-1 publication that regulatory action on the obesity indication was expected as early as the following year, with a type 2 diabetes submission anticipated in 2026 [lilly-orforglipron-data].

The oral GLP-1 space is becoming genuinely competitive, with Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide now approved for weight management and multiple other small-molecule agonists in development. ATTAIN-1 establishes that the non-peptide oral route works; what it does not establish is that it closes the gap with the best injectables.
