BPC-157 is sold widely in oral form, and the usual justification is that it survives the stomach. That claim is, unusually for this space, partly supported by the published pharmaceutical literature. What is not supported is the impression that oral BPC-157 is a developed drug.

## Why Oral BPC-157 Is Even Plausible

Most peptides are destroyed in the gastrointestinal tract before absorption, which is why peptide therapeutics are overwhelmingly injectable and why oral semaglutide needs a permeation enhancer to work at all [oral-peptide-delivery].

BPC-157 is a partial exception. A biopharmaceutical review of BPC-157 notes that it exhibits unusual stability in gastric juice and shows activity via the oral route [bpc-157-review]. Its origin explains why: it is a synthetic pentadecapeptide derived from a sequence identified in human gastric juice, so gastric stability is not surprising.

Much of the preclinical work has examined gastrointestinal effects, which raises a genuine possibility that local exposure in the gut could be relevant independently of systemic absorption.

## Where the Pharmacology Falls Apart

The same review is blunt about how much remains uncharacterised [bpc-157-review]:

- **Intramuscular bioavailability** ranges from 14% to 51% depending on species — a very wide range for a compound in widespread use
- **Plasma half-life** is under 30 minutes, which sits awkwardly against biological effects reported to last hours to days
- **No approved formulation exists**, and pharmaceutical development is described as rudimentary
- The compound **lacks fundamental pharmaceutical characterisation**

That half-life-versus-duration mismatch is the most interesting unresolved problem. Either the reported durations of effect are not attributable to circulating peptide, or the pharmacokinetics are not being measured in a way that captures what matters. Neither possibility has been settled.

## The Human Evidence

The human dataset is very small. The review reports **fewer than 30 subjects across three uncontrolled pilot studies** [bpc-157-review].

"Uncontrolled" is the operative word. Without a comparator arm, these studies cannot separate drug effect from natural recovery, expectation, or co-intervention — which is a fundamental limitation for a compound whose main claimed uses involve healing from injury, where spontaneous improvement is the norm.

One interventional trial is now registered: **NCT07437547**, a Phase 2 study of pentadecapeptide BPC 157 versus placebo for acute hamstring muscle strain repair, sponsored by Hudson Biotech and recruiting [bpc157-hamstring-trial]. This is a placebo-controlled design in a defined injury, which is the kind of study that could actually answer a question. No results have been posted.

## What It Means

BPC-157 occupies a regulatory grey area. It is available through research chemical suppliers and some compounding channels, and it has never completed the development process required for approval.

For anyone weighing an oral BPC-157 product, the relevant facts are:

- Gastric stability is real, but stability is not efficacy
- There is no established human dose, no characterised human pharmacokinetic profile, and no controlled human efficacy data
- Product content and purity outside a pharmaceutical supply chain are unverified, and there is no approved formulation to compare against
- The registered Phase 2 trial has not reported

Formal clinical development is the appropriate path to establishing whether the preclinical signal translates. The relevant milestones would be completion of controlled trials with published results, characterisation of human pharmacokinetics, and eventually a defined formulation and indication. None of those has yet been reached.
