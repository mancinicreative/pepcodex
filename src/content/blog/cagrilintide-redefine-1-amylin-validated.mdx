**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this article described REDEFINE-1 as a trial of cagrilintide monotherapy and reported approximately 11% weight loss for cagrilintide alone. That description was wrong. REDEFINE-1 tested CagriSema — the fixed combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide — against placebo. The article below reflects the published trial and the verified state of the cagrilintide monotherapy evidence.

The obesity treatment landscape continues expanding around the amylin pathway. The pivotal trial in that story, REDEFINE-1, was published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2025 — but it is a trial of the [CagriSema](/peptides/cagrisema) combination, not of amylin agonism alone. Understanding what it does and does not establish about the amylin mechanism matters for reading the field correctly.

## What REDEFINE-1 Actually Tested

REDEFINE-1 (NCT05567796) enrolled 3,417 adults with overweight or obesity and randomised them to once-weekly CagriSema (cagrilintide 2.4 mg / semaglutide 2.4 mg) or placebo, alongside lifestyle intervention, for 68 weeks [redefine1-nejm].

On the **treatment-policy estimand** — the analysis that keeps every randomised participant in, regardless of adherence — CagriSema produced 20.4% mean weight loss versus 3.0% with placebo (estimated difference −17.3 percentage points; 95% CI −18.1 to −16.6) [redefine1-nejm]. The PubMed abstract fetched 2026-09-02 does not report a trial-product percent; this page does not quote one.

Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most common side effects (79.6% with CagriSema versus 39.9% with placebo), mainly transient and mild-to-moderate [redefine1-nejm].

## Where the Amylin Monotherapy Evidence Really Sits

Amylin is a hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells in response to meals. It promotes satiety, slows gastric emptying, and reduces glucagon secretion. [Cagrilintide](/peptides/cagrilintide) is a long-acting amylin analog designed for once-weekly administration, and its standalone evidence comes from two places.

**Phase 2 dose-finding.** In the Lancet phase 2 dose-finding trial in people with overweight and obesity, cagrilintide 4.5 mg produced 10.8% mean weight loss at 26 weeks, with efficacy comparable to the active comparator liraglutide 3.0 mg [cagrilintide-phase2-lancet]. That remains the core monotherapy weight-loss result.

**A phase 3 comparator arm.** REIMAGINE 2, published on 7 June 2026, is the first phase 3 study to carry a cagrilintide monotherapy arm ([semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) 2.4 mg and 1.0 mg, CagriSema at two strengths, cagrilintide 2.4 mg alone, and placebo, in 2,713 adults with type 2 diabetes over 68 weeks) [reimagine2-lancet]. The trial's primary comparison was CagriSema against semaglutide, not against cagrilintide, and the published abstract reports adverse-event rates for the monotherapy arm — 82.2%, against 81.2% for semaglutide 2.4 mg — without quoting an HbA1c figure for it. Cagrilintide monotherapy now exists in a phase 3 dataset, but as a comparator, not as the trial's question.

So the honest summary is: amylin agonism alone produces meaningful, double-digit weight loss in phase 2; the phase 3 record tests it in combination or as a comparator arm.

## What the Combination Result Means for the Mechanism

REDEFINE-1's 20.4% is a combination result, and REIMAGINE 2 gives the cleanest available reading of what cagrilintide adds. On the primary glycaemic comparison, HbA1c fell 1.91 percentage points with CagriSema 2.4 mg/2.4 mg against 1.75 with semaglutide 2.4 mg alone — a statistically significant difference of 0.16 percentage points (95% CI 0.05 to 0.27), and a small absolute margin [reimagine2-lancet]. Adding the amylin analog to semaglutide improved glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes, but not by much; the combination's case rests more on weight than on glucose.

The mechanistic rationale is unchanged: amylin's effects centre on the area postrema in the brainstem, influencing satiety signals differently than GLP-1's hypothalamic and reward-centre effects. Engaging both pathways is the design logic of CagriSema, and of newer unimolecular GLP-1/amylin agonists such as zenagamtide (formerly amycretin).

## What We Don't Know

The comparative efficacy of cagrilintide monotherapy versus GLP-1 receptor agonists at matched phase 3 scale has not been the primary question of any completed trial. Whether cagrilintide offers advantages for specific subgroups, or for people who cannot tolerate GLP-1 medications, remains open.

Cardiovascular outcome data for cagrilintide, alone or in combination, do not yet exist; the REDEFINE cardiovascular outcomes trial is ongoing. Long-term durability beyond the 68-week trial windows is likewise uncharacterised.

## What's Next

Novo Nordisk filed an NDA for CagriSema on 18 December 2025 on the basis of the REDEFINE programme; a first FDA decision is expected in late 2026, and CagriSema is not approved by any regulator as of 17 August 2026. Our coverage of the [REDEFINE 2 diabetes result](/blog/cagrisema-phase3-endpoint) and the [NDA filing](/blog/cagrisema-nda-filed) tracks the regulatory path.

For the field, the amylin pathway is validated as a contributor: phase 2 monotherapy data show real standalone activity, and phase 3 combination data show it adds to GLP-1 agonism. What the record does not yet contain is a phase 3 trial built to test amylin monotherapy as its primary question.
