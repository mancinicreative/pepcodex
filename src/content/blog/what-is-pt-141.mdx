## PT-141 vs Vyleesi: Approved Label vs Research Name

The beginner definition is the [PT-141 guide](/guide/what-is-pt-141). The compound record is [PT-141](/peptides/pt-141). Adverse-event tables live on [PT-141 safety](/safety/pt-141-safety). This page is approved Vyleesi vs the research-chem name — not a second definition.

PT-141 is the development name for bremelanotide (Vyleesi), FDA-approved for HSDD in premenopausal women. Research-chem listings that reuse "PT-141" are not the approved product record.

## Key Facts

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **FDA Approved** | Yes (2019) |
| **Brand Name** | Vyleesi |
| **Indication** | HSDD in premenopausal women |
| **Administration** | Self-injection, as needed |

## How Does It Work?

PT-141 activates melanocortin-4 receptors in the brain, affecting neural pathways involved in sexual response. Unlike other treatments, it works on desire rather than physical arousal.

## Who Is It For?

Vyleesi is approved for premenopausal women with:
- Low sexual desire that causes distress
- Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder (HSDD)

It is **not approved** for:
- Men
- Postmenopausal women
- General sexual enhancement

## Common Side Effects

Frequency tables and warnings live on [PT-141 safety](/safety/pt-141-safety). The published program reported nausea as the most common event, plus flushing, headache, and local site reactions.

## Important Notes

- Used on-demand (not daily)
- Label frequency limits live on [PT-141 safety](/safety/pt-141-safety)
- Works through the brain, not blood flow
- Requires prescription
