MOTS-c was described in a 2015 paper as a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded within mitochondrial 12S rRNA. The experiments connected it with cellular metabolism and mouse insulin resistance. They did not establish therapeutic effects in people. [PMID:25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/)

The molecular display describes the native free-base sequence; the reported mass is rounded to the precision in the [FDA’s May 2026 briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download). This identification does not apply interchangeably to salts, engineered analogues, or formulated products.

## What the evidence actually measures

The very-low evidence grade describes clinical benefit from administering native MOTS-c. Human biomarker findings and cell/animal results are described separately below.

The 12-publication inventory contains four primary reports with human biomarker observations, five preclinical reports, two reviews, and one hypothesis paper. Two of the human-observation papers also contain animal experiments; each publication is counted once. Registry records and regulatory documents are not included in these publication counts.

### Human biomarkers: association is not a treatment effect

An observational study in healthy men found lower circulating MOTS-c with age but higher skeletal-muscle expression in older groups. A general claim that MOTS-c declines in every tissue would misrepresent these findings. [PMID:32182209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32182209/)

A 2024 meta-analysis combined six case-control studies and one cross-sectional study, involving 602 participants. Its subgroup findings differed: circulating MOTS-c was lower in type 2 diabetes and higher in obesity. Neither direction demonstrates that restoring or increasing the peptide would improve health. [PMID:39160573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39160573/)

Exercise findings also vary. The 2021 physical-performance paper reported exercise-related increases in endogenous human MOTS-c; the administered-peptide experiments in that paper were in mice. A 2026 study of people with cerebral palsy and typically developing comparison groups found comparable basal mitochondrial-peptide levels and absent or modest exercise-related changes. [PMID:33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/) [PMID:42349896](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42349896/)

A 2026 systematic review included nine studies of exercise and mitochondrial-derived peptides. It described a limited, heterogeneous evidence base. Those are not nine clinical trials administering native MOTS-c. [PMID:42640735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42640735/)

### Cell and animal experiments

- The original metabolic study reported effects on folate and purine metabolism, AMPK activation, and prevention of diet-induced insulin resistance and obesity in mice. [PMID:25738459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25738459/)
- A separate mouse study reported reduced ovariectomy-associated bone loss. An AMPK inhibitor partly reduced the observed cellular effect; the paper does not establish a practical timeline for human metabolic benefit. [PMID:27237975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27237975/)
- Cell experiments connected metabolic stress with nuclear translocation of MOTS-c and interaction with antioxidant-response transcription factors, including NRF2. These findings do not establish antioxidant benefit in patients. [PMID:29983246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29983246/)
- A 2025 report combined mouse/islet experiments on senescence and glucose intolerance with a comparison of endogenous circulating MOTS-c in people with and without type 2 diabetes. Its human measurements were not peptide treatment outcomes. [PMID:40855115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40855115/)
- An August 2026 eLife report described host-defense findings in cells and a mouse infection model. These are not clinical infection-treatment results. [PMID:42611943](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42611943/)

### An important negative result

In June 2026, researchers studied adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells from six donors with obesity and six lean donors. MOTS-c activated AMPK but reduced proliferation and increased senescence-related and inflammatory signals. In a mouse kidney-injury model, pretreatment failed to restore the reparative effects of cells from donors with obesity and blunted the effects of cells from lean donors. Pathway activation therefore did not translate into improved repair in this setting. These results do not establish a patient adverse-event rate. [PMID:42324588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324588/)

## Aging and the longevity hypothesis

The 2021 paper described improved physical capacity in mice, including experiments started late in life. This is distinct from demonstrating longer life or better physical function after native MOTS-c administration in humans. [PMID:33473109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33473109/)

An earlier paper proposed that a mitochondrial variant in the MOTS-c-encoding region might help explain exceptional longevity in Japanese populations. It was a hypothesis, not evidence that the variant causes longevity or that administered MOTS-c extends human life. [PMID:26289118](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26289118/)

## Trial records require separate verification

The record [NCT07505745](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07505745), retrieved September 5, 2026, describes a Phase 2 MOTS-c study and labels it recruiting, with an estimated 120 participants. Its last posted update is April 1, 2026, and no results are posted in the record. Those fields do not independently verify recruitment, administration, or outcomes.

There is an additional reason for caution: another record naming the same sponsor, Hudson Biotech, explicitly describes its TB-500 study as fictional. The MOTS-c entry does not contain that statement. This shared sponsor context is a verification concern, not proof that the MOTS-c entry is fictional. Its conduct and product identity remain unverified in this review. [NCT07487363](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07487363)

Two other records answer different questions. [NCT07678073](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07678073) compares anesthetic approaches in renal transplantation and measures MOTS-c as a biomarker; it does not administer MOTS-c. [NCT03998514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03998514) concerns CB4211, which the [sponsor identifies as a MOTS-c analogue](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1522602/000121390018006921/f8k052918ex99-1_cohbar.htm); it cannot supply native MOTS-c treatment or pharmacokinetic evidence.

## Safety and regulatory context

The [FDA safety page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks) reviewed September 5, 2026 lists MOTs-C among withdrawn nominations and describes human safety-data gaps and immunogenicity and product-characterization concerns. The [May 2026 advisory briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193347/download) is not a final compounding determination.

See the [MOTS-c safety review](/safety/mots-c-safety) and the [trial-record explainer](/blog/motsc-human-trials). This dossier summarizes research; it does not establish clinical benefit, safety, or treatment recommendations.
