## Different indications and mechanisms

Thymosin alpha-1 (thymalfasin) is a 28-residue immunomodulatory peptide with human trials in defined diseases, including chronic hepatitis B. LL-37 is the mature 37-residue human cathelicidin peptide with experimental antimicrobial and context-dependent inflammatory signaling. These mechanisms do not establish a preferred general immune treatment. [thymosin-alpha-1 hepatitis trial, PMID 9581695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9581695/) · [LL-37 processing, PMID 8681941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8681941/) · [LL-37 rosacea mechanism, PMID 17676051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17676051/)

| Evidence question | Thymosin alpha-1 | LL-37 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct human treatment | Controlled trials in specific illnesses; results vary | Three native topical wound-trial populations; mixed results |
| Regulatory boundary | Approved in some countries according to the C25 dossier; no US FDA-approved product verified here | No FDA-approved LL-37 medicine verified |
| Therapeutic-claim grade | **Moderate** within studied indications; not a general immune claim | **Low** for broad wound or anti-infective claims |

In a 98-person chronic hepatitis B trial, a prespecified treatment group had a greater later virological response than untreated controls. A separate 97-person double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trial did **not** confirm statistically significant efficacy on its complete-response endpoint. These are indication-specific findings, not proof of broad immunity or infection prevention. [PMID 9581695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9581695/) · [PMID 10607256](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10607256/)

Native topical LL-37 had selected healing signals in a 34-person venous-ulcer pilot and a diabetic-foot-ulcer cream trial. A larger 148-person venous-ulcer trial found no significant full-population healing benefit, and the diabetic-foot-ulcer study did not significantly reduce measured aerobic bacterial colonization. [LL-37 topical pilot, PMID 25041740](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25041740/) · [larger phase IIb trial, PMID 34687253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34687253/) · [diabetic-foot-ulcer trial, PMID 37480520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37480520/)

A completed melanoma registry posts intratumoral LL-37 outcomes for just four people; it is not a topical trial or general cancer result. Engineered *L. lactis* expressing LL-37 is a different oral intervention, with nonsignificant overall viral-negative-time results. [NCT02225366](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02225366) · [PMID 37605995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37605995/)

The C25 thymosin alpha-1 dossier reports non-US approvals, but the exact country/indication list has not been independently reverified in this comparison packet; no precise country count is used here. FDA describes insufficient safety information for compounded LL-37. Neither source stream establishes safety or benefit from combining the two peptides. [FDA compounding safety review](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)

This selected-source review is educational, not advice to use either peptide.
