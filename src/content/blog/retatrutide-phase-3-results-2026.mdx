Those figures could not be traced to any published trial report or registry results posting, and they have been removed. [Retatrutide](/peptides/retatrutide) (LY3437943) is an investigational peptide developed by Eli Lilly that simultaneously activates three hormone receptors: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. It is in phase 3 development under the TRIUMPH clinical trial program and is not FDA-approved or available outside clinical trials.

This article summarizes the published evidence from phase 2 and the one published phase 3 trial, clearly distinguishing what has been confirmed at each stage.

## What Makes Retatrutide Different

Every approved GLP-1 medication targets one or two incretin receptors. Retatrutide adds a third.

| Medication | Mechanism | Receptors |
|---|---|---|
| [Semaglutide](/peptides/semaglutide) (Ozempic, Wegovy) | GLP-1 agonist | GLP-1 |
| [Tirzepatide](/peptides/tirzepatide) (Mounjaro, Zepbound) | Dual incretin agonist | GLP-1 + GIP |
| **Retatrutide** | Triple agonist | GLP-1 + GIP + Glucagon |

**The role of the glucagon receptor** is what sets retatrutide apart from tirzepatide. While glucagon raises blood glucose in a fasting state, its receptor in the liver drives fat oxidation and thermogenesis in brown adipose tissue. In retatrutide, the GLP-1 and GIP components counterbalance the glucose-raising effect of glucagon activation, allowing the fat-burning and energy-expenditure benefits without worsening blood sugar [pmid-38367045].

## Phase 2 Results: Established Evidence

The clearest picture of retatrutide's efficacy in obesity comes from its phase 2 program. These are published, peer-reviewed results in leading journals.

### NEJM Phase 2 Obesity Trial (Jastreboff et al., 2023)

The landmark phase 2 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine enrolled adults with obesity but without type 2 diabetes [pmid-37366315]. At 48 weeks:

- **24.2% mean weight loss** at the highest (12 mg) maintenance level
- **93% of participants** at that level achieved at least 10% weight loss
- **83% achieved at least 15%** weight loss
- Weight loss was **still progressing** at 48 weeks — no plateau observed

The absence of a weight-loss plateau was notable, as plateauing has historically been a limitation of GLP-1 monotherapy.

### Phase 2 Type 2 Diabetes Trial (Rosenstock et al., 2023)

A separate phase 2 trial in participants with type 2 diabetes showed [pmid-37385280]:

- **HbA1c reduction of 2.02%** at the 12 mg level over 36 weeks
- **82% of participants** achieved HbA1c of 6.5% or below
- **16.9% body weight reduction** despite the shorter 36-week duration

### Phase 2a Liver Disease Trial (Sanyal et al., 2024)

A phase 2a substudy in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) demonstrated [pmid-38858523]:

- **81-82% relative reduction** in liver fat content at 24 weeks (8 mg and 12 mg)
- **79-86% of treated participants** achieved normal liver fat levels (below 5%)
- 0% in the placebo group achieved liver fat normalization

The glucagon receptor component is thought to contribute to the direct hepatic fat-reduction effect, beyond what would be expected from weight loss alone.

## Phase 3: What Is Actually Published

### TRANSCEND-T2D-1 — the first published phase 3 trial

The first phase 3 trial of retatrutide to report anywhere is TRANSCEND-T2D-1, published in The Lancet on 6 June 2026 [transcend-t2d-1]. It ran 40 weeks at 48 sites in the USA, Mexico, and India, and randomized 537 adults whose type 2 diabetes was inadequately controlled by diet and exercise alone.

On the **treatment-regimen estimand** — the analysis that keeps every randomized participant in, regardless of whether they stayed on the drug — HbA1c fell 1.69, 1.86, and 1.94 percentage points at the 4, 9, and 12 mg levels, against 0.81 with placebo. Body weight, a key secondary endpoint, fell 11.5%, 13.9%, and 15.3% across the three levels versus 2.6% with placebo. Gastrointestinal events were the most frequent adverse events and were generally mild to moderate; no severe hypoglycemia was reported.

**What TRANSCEND-T2D-1 does not tell us:** this is a diabetes trial, in a population selected for glycemic control. It does not answer the obesity question the TRIUMPH program was built to answer, and it sits outside the TRIUMPH basket.

### The TRIUMPH program — design published, results not

The phase 3 TRIUMPH registrational program consists of four multicenter, randomized, double-blind trials enrolling over 5,800 participants, evaluating weekly subcutaneous retatrutide against placebo for obesity and two related complications — obstructive sleep apnea and knee osteoarthritis. Two weight-management trials (TRIUMPH-1 and TRIUMPH-2) carry nested OSA and OA protocols, TRIUMPH-3 studies a population with cardiovascular disease, and TRIUMPH-4 is a stand-alone knee osteoarthritis trial [triumph-program-design].

**As of 17 August 2026, a search of PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov returns no TRIUMPH efficacy or safety results — only the program design paper and adjacent methodology records.** Weight-loss figures circulating for the TRIUMPH trials cannot be traced to a primary source, and this article does not report them. For a closer look at the osteoarthritis arm, see [the TRIUMPH-4 status review](/blog/retatrutide-triumph-4-weight-loss-pain-relief).

## How It Compares: Context Table

Cross-trial comparisons require caution — different populations, protocols, and follow-up durations make direct head-to-head inferences unreliable. These figures are estimates from separate trials, not equivalents.

| Treatment | Trial | Weight Loss | Duration | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) | STEP 1 | 14.9% | 68 weeks | FDA approved (2021) |
| Tirzepatide 15 mg (Zepbound) | SURMOUNT-1 | 20.9% | 72 weeks | FDA approved (2023) |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | Phase 2 (NEJM) | 24.2% | 48 weeks | Investigational — phase 2 |
| Retatrutide 12 mg | TRANSCEND-T2D-1 (T2D population) | 15.3% | 40 weeks | Investigational — published phase 3 |

**Key caveat:** the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial showed tirzepatide outperforming semaglutide 2.4 mg (20.2% vs 13.7% at 72 weeks) [surmount-5]. No head-to-head trial between retatrutide and tirzepatide has reported results; see [the direct-comparison review](/blog/retatrutide-direct-comparison).

## What Remains Unknown

**Phase 3 obesity efficacy.** The single most important number — TRIUMPH weight loss in a general obesity population — is not published. Phase 2's 24.2% remains the verified obesity benchmark.

**Long-term safety.** Cardiovascular outcomes, rare adverse events, and tolerability beyond the 48-week phase 2 window require the ongoing phase 3 program to mature. Glucagon receptor activation at therapeutic exposure has not been assessed over multi-year periods.

**Cardiovascular outcomes.** Semaglutide demonstrated a 20% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events in SELECT. Whether retatrutide achieves comparable cardiovascular protection is unknown.

**Post-discontinuation effects.** What happens to body weight and metabolic parameters after stopping retatrutide is unstudied. Weight regain after GLP-1 cessation is documented with other agents and likely applies here.

**Regulatory status.** Retatrutide has not been approved by the FDA or EMA, and Eli Lilly has not published a confirmed submission date as of 17 August 2026. No product claiming to be retatrutide outside clinical trials is legitimate.

## Bottom Line

Retatrutide has the strongest phase 2 efficacy signal of any obesity compound: 24.2% weight loss at 48 weeks, published in the NEJM. Its first published phase 3 trial, TRANSCEND-T2D-1, confirmed large effects in type 2 diabetes — HbA1c reductions up to 1.94 percentage points and weight loss up to 15.3% at 40 weeks.

The phase 3 obesity picture, though, is still unwritten as of 17 August 2026. Until TRIUMPH results appear in a peer-reviewed journal or a registry results posting, any specific TRIUMPH weight-loss figure is unverified — and the questions that will decide retatrutide's clinical role, from cardiovascular outcomes to the head-to-head against tirzepatide, remain open.
