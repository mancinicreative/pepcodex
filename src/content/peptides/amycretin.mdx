### A note on the name

The compound covered on this page is now called **zenagamtide**. Both Lancet phase 2 reports published on 30 July 2026 open with the phrase "Zenagamtide (formerly amycretin)" ([PMID:42532080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532080/), [PMID:42532079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532079/)). The development codes NNC0487-0111 and the earlier name amycretin refer to the same molecule, and the older literature — including the 2025 phase 1 and phase 1b/2a papers — is published under the amycretin name. This page keeps its original address so existing links continue to work.

The proposed mechanisms are based on phase 1 and phase 2 clinical data and extensive preclinical validation of the dual GLP-1/amylin pathway approach.

### Class systematic review (2026)

Moiz et al. (*Ann Intern Med*, 2026; [PMID:42673585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42673585/)), fetched 2026-09-02, is an updated systematic review of GLP-1 receptor agonists and co-agonists for weight loss among adults without diabetes (MEDLINE/Embase/CENTRAL through 25 March 2026; 38 RCTs). The abstract names amycretin among emerging multiagonists and reports placebo-subtracted weight loss of −23.9% (95% CI −29.3% to −18.5%), alongside a retatrutide figure that is not used as amycretin evidence. The authors state heterogeneity precluded quantitative synthesis. This is not a new primary amycretin RCT, and the abstract does not name the contributing trial or estimand. Primary-trial numbers below are unchanged.

### Phase 2 in type 2 diabetes (2026)

Two 36-week phase 2 dose-finding trials, run at the same 83 sites across 11 countries under the single registration NCT06542874, reported in The Lancet on 30 July 2026.

The subcutaneous trial randomised 262 adults with type 2 diabetes on metformin, with or without an SGLT2 inhibitor, to one of six once-weekly doses (0.4 to 40 mg) or placebo ([PMID:42532080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532080/)). From a mean baseline HbA1c of 7.8%, estimated change at week 36 ranged from -0.9% at 0.4 mg, a difference versus placebo of -0.77% (95% CI -1.26 to -0.28), to -1.7% at 40 mg, a difference of -1.56% (95% CI -2.05 to -1.07). Serious adverse events were reported in 21 of 261 exposed participants (8%), and no deaths occurred.

The oral trial randomised 186 adults to once-daily 6, 25 or 50 mg or placebo ([PMID:42532079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532079/)). Estimated HbA1c change at week 36 was -0.9%, -1.3% and -1.4% respectively, against baselines of 7.9 to 8.1%. Gastrointestinal adverse events rose with dose, from 26% at 6 mg to 47% at 50 mg, against 23% on placebo.

An estimand caveat applies to both trials, and it is stated in the papers themselves: the primary outcome was assessed using the **efficacy estimand**, based on on-treatment data without rescue medication. That analysis describes what happened in participants who stayed on treatment, not what happened across everyone randomised. Neither report gives a treatment-regimen figure that could be preferred over it, so the numbers above should be read as efficacy-estimand results. Neither trial had body weight as its primary endpoint.

### Important Limitations

- The 2026 Annals class systematic review (PMID 42673585) names amycretin but is not a new primary RCT and does not identify the trial or estimand behind its −23.9% figure
- Peer-reviewed efficacy data extend to phase 2 (two 36-week type 2 diabetes trials, The Lancet, 2026) alongside the 2025 phase 1 oral and phase 1b/2a subcutaneous trials; phase 3 outcome data are not yet available
- The phase 2 primary outcomes rest on the efficacy estimand only, which tends to give a larger apparent effect than a treatment-regimen analysis of the same data
- A large phase 3 program (AMAZE) is registered and underway, but its results have not yet been published
- The published phase 2 evidence is in type 2 diabetes; the phase 3 obesity question is still open
- Subcutaneous phase 1b/2a trial had a high participant withdrawal rate, which limits interpretation of the bodyweight findings
- Long-term safety beyond 36 weeks not established
- Head-to-head comparisons with semaglutide/tirzepatide are indirect only
- Molecular structure and sequence not publicly available
