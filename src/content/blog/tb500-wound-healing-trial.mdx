**Correction notice:** An earlier version of this briefing described a Phase 2 trial of a thymosin beta-4 fragment ("TB4-AF") in 180 patients with diabetic foot ulcers, reporting 52% versus 31% complete wound closure. No such trial or compound could be located in any trial registry or the published literature, and those figures have been removed. The verified clinical record is summarised below — and it points the other way.

## What Was Actually Tested in Humans

Thymosin beta-4 has been through controlled human wound-healing trials, conducted as a topical gel formulation (RGN-137). The registered dose-response study in venous stasis ulcers was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in 72 participants [tb4-venous-ulcer-trial]. A parallel Phase 2 programme evaluated pressure ulcers.

**What those trials found:** the peptide was safe and well tolerated across all dose levels, with no dose-limiting or drug-related serious adverse events. On efficacy, they did not show statistically significant differences from placebo for complete wound healing or rate of wound healing. The mid-dose level was described as appearing the most active, with more rapid initiation of healing, but that observation did not reach statistical significance.

Earlier clinical work on thymosin in venous ulcers has also been published in the peer-reviewed literature [guarnera-thymosin-venous].

This is the central point for anyone evaluating TB-500 claims: thymosin beta-4 has been taken into properly controlled human wound trials, and those trials did not establish efficacy.

## The Science of TB-500 and Thymosin Beta-4

Thymosin beta-4 is a naturally occurring peptide involved in actin sequestration, cell migration, and angiogenesis. TB-500, widely referenced in research contexts, is a synthetic version of a portion of the thymosin beta-4 sequence — the two are related but not interchangeable, and marketing material frequently blurs the distinction.

The proposed mechanism involves several pathways. Thymosin beta-4 promotes cell migration, drawing keratinocytes and fibroblasts to the wound site. It stimulates angiogenesis, improving blood supply to healing tissue. It also has anti-inflammatory properties that could in principle modulate the chronic inflammatory state characteristic of non-healing wounds.

That mechanistic case is genuine. It is also, so far, where the evidence stops: a plausible mechanism plus preclinical activity that has not converted into a positive controlled human trial.

## What It Means

**For patients with chronic wounds:** diabetic foot ulcers and venous stasis ulcers remain a serious unmet need, and no thymosin beta-4 product is approved to treat them. Standard wound care remains the evidence-based option.

**For the research peptide community:** the thymosin beta-4 programme is a useful cautionary case. Strong preclinical wound-healing data and a coherent mechanism did not reproduce as statistically significant benefit in randomized placebo-controlled trials. The same caution applies to preclinical-only claims made for TB-500 and BPC-157.

**On product claims:** TB-500 sold as a research chemical is not the trial formulation, has not been through the trials described above, and carries no verified purity or dosing basis.

## What's Next

Thymosin beta-4 development has continued in other indications, including ophthalmic formulations. Any future claim of wound-healing efficacy should be checked against a registered trial with posted results rather than a press release or a vendor page.
